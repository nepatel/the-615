Lead

AI CAPEX RECKONING

Intel's worst day since 1974, a capex-driven Amazon stumble and a Reddit miss drag the Nasdaq to its worst month since March — even after Thursday's GDP print beat

Thursday's data, briefly, because Friday ran it over: the BEA's advance estimate put Q3 GDP growth at 3.4% annualized, above the roughly 3.2% consensus and faster than Q2's 2.2%, with the PCE price index inside the same report showing inflation slowing to 2.1%. Initial jobless claims for the week ended Oct. 24 fell to 194,000, a three-month low and below both a Reuters-cited 199,000 estimate and a separately reported Dow Jones forecast of 200,000, with the four-week average easing to 196,750. Both resolve items this brief flagged as still-unreleased in issue No. 32; both were, on their own terms, good news for an economy the Fed just told markets it isn't in a hurry to ease for.

Friday erased that signal. Intel reported Thursday after the close with a Q3 that beat on both lines it's measured on — $16.5 billion revenue, $0.52 adjusted EPS, against a $17.1 billion/$0.43 analyst consensus (LSEG) — with data-center and AI revenue up 78% even as client (PC) revenue fell 5%. The stock didn't move on that print. It moved on the guide: Q4 revenue of $15.0-16.0 billion against this quarter's own $16.5 billion actual, and adjusted gross margin collapsing to a guided 38.0% from 44.1%, with management citing a PC memory-chip shortage pushing prices up and demand down. Shares fell 28.58% Friday, closing at $65.76 — Intel's worst single-session decline since 1974, by multiple outlets' count.

Amazon compounded it rather than offsetting it. AWS revenue rose roughly 39% to about $46 billion, its fastest growth in nearly five years, with Andy Jassy guiding to AWS growth exceeding 40% in both 2027 and 2028 — but consolidated EPS of $1.89 missed a consensus clustered around $1.96-1.97, revenue of $201.94 billion landed just under a roughly $202.2 billion estimate, and trailing-12-month free cash flow was negative $42.4 billion against quarterly capex near $77 billion. Reports of the after-hours move itself ranged from roughly 6% to 11% depending on source and timestamp, a spread wide enough that this brief won't print one figure as the move. Reddit added a third leg: shares fell roughly 24%-29% (sources disagree) on weaker-than-expected daily active users, soft Q4 revenue guidance, and a plan to require login for forum access.

Read: this is the Microsoft-vs.-Alphabet capex-discipline split this brief has tracked since Wednesday's earnings, now widening past the two hyperscalers it started with. Intel's crash wasn't about AI demand — data-center/AI revenue grew 78% — it was about the gross margin the company itself says that demand now costs to serve, the same mechanism investors punished in Alphabet's capex guide last week. Amazon's AWS growth was arguably the best print of the week on a pure-growth basis, and the market sold it anyway because $77 billion in one quarter's capex and a ninth straight quarter of negative trailing free cash flow reads, to a market already primed by Wednesday and Thursday, as the same story with a new ticker. Reddit's miss is a separate, company-specific thread — DAUs and a login-wall plan, not capex — but it hit the same tape and widened the day's losses. Watch: whether Monday's open (Nov. 2) extends Friday's rout or treats it as a one-day flush; the first market reaction to a six-day-old 31%-32% Reuters/Ipsos approval reading four days before the midterms; and Tuesday's election itself, which the Fed's Wednesday hold and hawkish dissent axis now sit directly upstream of.
