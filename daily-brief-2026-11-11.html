Lead

CPI · RATES · NVIDIA/COREWEAVE

A hot October CPI pushes the 10-year toward 5% and claws back Monday's rally — while CoreWeave's blowout quarter undercuts the AI-capex panic that Nvidia's slide keeps retelling

Tuesday's data did what Monday's oil-driven optimism couldn't: it gave the market a reason to doubt itself. October CPI rose 0.4% month-over-month, a tenth above the 0.3% Dow Jones consensus, with core CPI up 0.3% for a third consecutive month — also a tenth hot versus the 0.2% estimate. [fact, BLS/CNBC] There is no year-over-year figure this cycle: last year's government shutdown stopped BLS from collecting October 2025 prices, so there is no comparable base to measure against. Shelter rose 0.3% and accounted for more than 30% of the headline gain; gasoline added 1.0%, food 0.5%, transportation services 0.8%, medical commodities 0.5% and electricity 0.9%, while used cars fell 1.2%. [fact] None of this captures Monday's oil plunge — that shows up, if it holds, in November's report — which means the one input that most clearly argues for disinflation from here wasn't in Tuesday's number at all.

Rates read the print as exactly what it was: a second straight month of 0.3% core inflation that puts a floor under how much the Fed can ease, not evidence of reacceleration. The 10-year Treasury spiked intraday to 5.004% before settling at 4.98%, up 10 basis points on the day; the more Fed-sensitive 2-year jumped 17 basis points to 4.73%; the 30-year rose 9 to 5.35% — all per Treasury's own par curve, with the bond market open despite the Veterans Day holiday. [fact] The December 9 FOMC meeting, which looked like an afterthought a week ago, is suddenly live again: Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said this week she wants "at least two more" 25-basis-point hikes, and Governor Christopher Waller had already flagged a 50-basis-point move as something the Fed should consider if core inflation kept printing near 0.3% — which it just did, for the third month running. [fact] Equities gave back a slice of Monday's rally rather than panicking: the S&P 500 closed down 0.38% at 7,520.93, the Dow fell 0.30% (-157.86 points) to 52,231.89, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.74% to 25,065.87 — a measured retracement against a session that had just posted its best gain since April. [fact] The read: the market is treating Tuesday's print as a reason to trim rate-cut hopes, not as a reason to re-price the soft-landing case Monday's oil news had reinforced. [read]

Nvidia's slide extended into a second session, falling more than 5% Tuesday — one account put the intraday drop near 7%, briefly pulling the company's market cap below $4 trillion — with the rate-repricing compounding, not replacing, the overhang from Monday's disclosure that its board has opened an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a woman US intelligence reportedly assesses as having ties to Chinese intelligence. [fact; flag: exact closing print not independently cross-verified against a second named source this run] No new reporting on that investigation surfaced since Monday, and Congress's own deadline for Huang to accept — or propose an alternative to — a November 18 hearing date falls today, with no response found yet. [flag, unresolved] CoreWeave supplied the session's one unambiguous strength: reporting after Tuesday's close, it posted revenue of $3.66 billion, up 168% year-over-year and above the roughly $3.55 billion Street consensus, raised full-year revenue guidance to $13.0–13.3 billion from a prior $12.4–13.2 billion, and said its contracted backlog grew to $132.5 billion from $104 billion in June — even as its net loss widened to $709 million on interest expense that nearly tripled to $846 million. [fact; see Business] That combination — demand and backlog both re-accelerating even as financing costs bite — is the most direct rebuttal yet to the AI-capex-payback fear that drove November 5's selloff, and it arrived on a day when the macro story was working against risk assets, not for them.

Watch: whether Friday's PPI, and especially November's CPI — the first report that will actually capture Monday's oil crash — keep today's hot-inflation framing alive into the December 9 FOMC; whether Huang responds to Congress's hearing ultimatum; whether chip and AI-infrastructure stocks can decouple from Nvidia's slide on CoreWeave's numbers; and whether the Qatar-brokered Hormuz truce survives the week, given a named Qatari official's on-record pushback and unconfirmed claims of fresh tanker attacks since Sunday.
