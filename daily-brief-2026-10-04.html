Lead

LABOR MARKET — A SECOND SOFT PRINT, A ONE-WAY MARKET REACTION

September payrolls add just 29,000 — the week's second straight soft print pushes the Fed to the sidelines and stocks to records, but Hormuz keeps a floor under oil

Friday's jobs report was the week's second consecutive data point to break sharply dovish. The Bureau of Labor Statistics said nonfarm payrolls rose just 29,000 in September, against consensus estimates clustered in the 84,000-90,000 range across trackers — one of the larger misses of the cycle. [fact] The unemployment rate rose to 4.2% from 4.1%. [fact] July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000, with August cut from an initially reported 162,000 to 133,000. [fact] Coming four days after Wednesday's cooler-than-expected August PCE print (this brief's Thursday lead), the two releases together have done more to reshape the rate-hike debate than anything since the Sept. 16 hike itself. [read]

The market reaction was immediate and one-directional, unlike Thursday's split verdict between a dovish PCE and a Hormuz-driven crude rally. CME FedWatch's probability of a quarter-point hike at the Oct. 27-28 meeting collapsed to roughly 17%, with the probability of a hold near 83% — down from Thursday's already-reduced ~35% and from above 70% a week earlier. [fact] December remains the market's preferred timing for the next hike, with FedWatch showing north of 75% odds there, per one tracker. [fact, single-sourced]

Equities took the "bad data, dovish Fed" read and ran with it: the S&P 500 gained 0.7%, the Dow 0.5%, and the Nasdaq Composite 1.2% to a fresh record close of 27,191, with chipmakers and megacaps leading — the Philadelphia semiconductor index firmed over 3%, and Nvidia touched an intraday record, up roughly 2.5%. [fact] That is a continuation of Thursday's resilience, where stocks held up even as oil and the PCE data pulled in opposite directions. [read]

Bonds told a more complicated story than the headline suggests. Treasury yields fell immediately on the 8:30a release, as the market priced out hike risk, but reversed over the session: the 10-year and 2-year both finished roughly 5 basis points higher on the day, at 5.28% and 4.84% respectively. [fact] That intraday round-trip — lower on the data, higher by the close — suggests the initial flight-to-duration bid gave way to concerns the miss reflects deeper labor-market softening than a one-off, or simply that a market already positioned for a dovish Fed found less reason to extend the rally in rates once the number was in hand. [read] The dollar index eased modestly to 101.93 (-0.17%), and gold spiked about 1% intraday to roughly $4,217 before giving back some of that move. [fact]

Crude sits apart from this story, as it did Thursday. Brent settled Friday at $102.72 on the active ICE contract, still elevated, with no clean WTI settle located this run. [fact/flag] A weaker-dollar, lower-rates backdrop would ordinarily support crude on its own, but the more immediate driver remains the Strait of Hormuz, where UKMTO confirmed another tanker struck by an unidentified projectile Thursday, this one causing a fire. [fact] With no new data this run on transit counts or vessel-holding levels to confirm whether late-September's attack spike and the sharp vessel-count drop have stabilized, oil's risk premium looks unresolved rather than receding. [read]

Stakes heading into the week: a Fed that now has real cover to hold on Oct. 27-28 unless the picture changes again before Oct. 14's CPI and Oct. 15's PPI — both of which just gained weight given how far two data points moved the odds in four trading days. Today's OPEC+ JMMC meeting could add a second, oil-specific variable on top of the Hormuz risk premium; no outcome is in yet. [flag] Whether Thursday's UKMTO incident is a one-off or the start of a fresh escalation cycle, and whether Tehran's review of Washington's counter-proposal (Iran & Hormuz, below) produces any public signal before markets reopen, are the two threads most likely to move Monday's open.
