Lead

SAUDI ARABIA · INFLATION · THE FED

Riyadh's airport reopens after nine days — but the Houthi-driven oil spike is already sitting inside September's hot CPI and PPI prints

King Khalid International Airport reopened Saturday, Oct. 17, after being closed since the Oct. 10 missile barrage that killed at least 12 people and wounded roughly 300, per Saudi civil-aviation figures reported across multiple outlets. [fact] International capacity has not snapped back: Lufthansa, KLM, Air France and several Gulf and Asian carriers have extended cancellations past the reopening, and the broader air war has not stopped — Saudi authorities reported a Houthi projectile hit a residential area of Najran on Sunday, Oct. 18, injuring six and damaging homes and vehicles, while the coalition said it intercepted two drones near Riyadh and the Eastern Province and a third, unattributed, drone near the capital that same morning. [NEW, fact] The Gulf Cooperation Council issued a formal statement Sunday condemning the Houthi campaign and framing an attack on any member state as an attack on all six, explicitly backing Saudi Arabia's right to respond; Egypt's national security council separately said it would support the kingdom, though Egypt's parliament postponed a vote on authorizing that support rather than taking it up Sunday as expected. [NEW, fact] None of this has produced the "firm" Saudi response promised after the Oct. 10 strike, and regional officials continue to describe Saudi interceptor stocks as strained from the sustained tempo of the barrages. [fact, continuity]

That's the security layer. The macro layer moved just as much, and in a way that ties directly back to it. September CPI, due as this brief went to press on Oct. 14 and unconfirmed in that issue, landed hot: headline inflation rose 0.6% on the month and 3.7% year-over-year, above the pre-release range of roughly 3.6%-3.7% cited last issue, with core CPI at 2.6% y/y, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics. [NEW, fact] The composition matters for this story specifically: energy CPI rose 5.2% on the month and 20.4% year-over-year, with gasoline up 9.1% m/m and 34.2% y/y — a direct line from the Hormuz-and-Houthi-driven crude rally (Brent has run from $104.72 on Oct. 9 to $108.60 this morning) into the headline number households and the Fed both watch. [fact] The next day's prints pushed the same direction harder: September PPI rose 1.3% m/m against a roughly 0.6% consensus, with core PPI up 0.7% against 0.3% expected and the annual PPI rate at 6.3%; retail sales rose 0.8% m/m against a 0.3%-0.4% consensus, with gas-station receipts up 6.4% doing much of the work; initial jobless claims came in at 199,000, in line with expectations. [NEW, fact]

Read: two consecutive upside inflation surprises, both substantially energy-driven, land nine days before the Fed's Oct. 27-28 meeting and reinforce — rather than challenge — what prediction markets were already pricing: cut odds for that meeting sit in the mid-teens-to-20s on Manifold, little moved by the data, consistent with a Fed that held its target range at 3.50%-3.75% in June on a 9-3 vote where every dissent wanted a hike, not a cut. [read, flag: dissent characterization from a forecasting-site analysis, not a primary Fed transcript] This is the second-order consequence of the war worth tracking into next week's FOMC: a conflict that started as a shipping-and-insurance story in the Strait of Hormuz has become, via Saudi Arabia's refining and export capacity sitting adjacent to the front line, a direct input into the US inflation print — which narrows the Fed's room to respond to anything else that breaks between now and year-end. [read] Set against that: equity futures are constructive this morning (S&P +0.50%, Nasdaq +1.13%) and the VIX is down sharply overnight, even as Europe opens broadly lower (FTSE -0.90%, DAX -1.11%, CAC -1.42%) — a split tape that doesn't yet agree on how much the Gulf escalation matters to risk assets, separate from how much it matters to inflation. [read, flag: VIX move single-sourced, not cross-verified] Watch: whether the Houthis follow through on threats to widen targeting to Aramco's Dammam headquarters; whether Egypt's parliament actually authorizes support this week; and whether Tuesday's CPI-adjacent news cycle leaves any room for Netflix earnings or the Knicks' title-defense opener. Worth flagging against the tape: issue No. 25 noted Trump's Oct. 6 prediction that consumer prices would "come tumbling down" as the Iran war ended; the actual September print moved the opposite direction, and the war itself has neither ended nor shown signs of doing so in the two weeks since. [fact/read]
