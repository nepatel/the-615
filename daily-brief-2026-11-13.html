Lead

NVIDIA · SUCCESSION · CPI

Nvidia's board replaces Jensen Huang with CFO Colette Kress as a hot CPI print flips the market's rate bet from cut to hike

Nvidia confirmed Thursday that Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO and director, effective immediately — a sudden end to a 33-year tenure that made him the most recognizable executive in the AI buildout. The company's 8-K, filed with the SEC, names CFO Colette Kress interim CEO and independent director Harvey C. Jones the board's first-ever independent chair, with a search underway for a permanent successor from internal and external candidates. [fact, primary source: SEC 8-K] Nvidia gave no official reason for the departure. Press coverage (Axios, the Washington Post, NBC) has tied it to reporting from Nov. 8–9 that Huang's companion, Alice Huang, is assessed by US intelligence as a Chinese asset and may have used the relationship to target him — the matter issue No. 39 reported as an open board investigation. That framing is now superseded: this is a resignation, not an ongoing probe, though the board's own public silence on cause leaves open whether this was a negotiated exit or something closer to a forced one. [fact, multi-sourced on the departure; interpretation on cause]

The mechanism matters more than the personnel. Huang was not a figurehead CEO; he was, in a real sense, the narrative collateral behind the AI-capex trade — the person investors looked to for conviction that hyperscaler spending would keep translating into Nvidia revenue even as skepticism about capex payback has built since October. Removing that person abruptly, with no stated cause, and under a cloud of foreign-intelligence allegations, strips out a specific kind of reassurance at the exact moment the broader AI trade needed it least. [read] The stock reportedly fell as much as 13% intraday Thursday before paring losses, though no independently confirmed closing price surfaced this run [not confirmed as of press time] — a gap this brief would normally fill but can't, which is itself a signal of how fluid Thursday's trading was.

The second-order consequences run through two calendars. Nvidia now reports Q3 FY27 earnings Tuesday, Nov. 17 — Kress's first call as CEO, and the company's first chance to separate "is the AI-capex story intact" from "is Nvidia's leadership intact," two questions the market had been treating as one. Separately, House committee chairs have asked for a Huang briefing on Nvidia's relationship with Megaspeed, a request that predates Thursday's resignation and will now proceed without him in the CEO chair, changing its political texture. [fact] Whether Kress or Jones make any public remarks before the 17th will be an early tell on whether this becomes a controlled succession story or a crisis-management one.

That shock landed in the middle of a three-session whipsaw this brief can now reconstruct cleanly for the first time. Tuesday's October CPI ran hot — headline +0.4% month over month against roughly 0.3% expected, core +0.3% against roughly 0.2% expected — and pushed rate-path commentary from an assumed Fed hold toward live discussion of a Dec. 8–9 hike; the Dow fell 0.38% that session even as CPI's deceleration from September's +0.6% kept the S&P narrowly positive. Wednesday reversed sharply: the S&P gained 1.31% and the Nasdaq 1.6% on a CoreWeave-led AI-infrastructure relief trade — CoreWeave's Q3 beat and raised full-year guidance read as evidence the capex-payback fear was overstated — and Apple touched a record $5.16 trillion market cap intraday. Thursday gave almost all of it back (S&P −0.35%, Nasdaq −0.39%) on the session Huang's exit became public. [fact] Read together, the three days show two unresolved tensions converging rather than resolving: a rates re-pricing toward higher-for-longer, and an idiosyncratic leadership shock at the single most important stock in the AI trade. Thursday's selling, concentrated on the day the resignation landed rather than spreading evenly across the week, suggests the market is treating Nvidia's leadership uncertainty as a risk distinct from the capex debate CoreWeave's print had just calmed — not a reason to re-open that debate, but a new, separate one. [read]

Watch: Thursday's actual NVDA close and any Kress/Jones public remarks before the 17th; today's PPI print against a CPI base that already moved the rate conversation; and whether Tuesday's earnings call becomes the venue where Nvidia tries to separate its leadership story from its capex story.

Business

CoreWeave's Q3 was the week's clearest beat: revenue of $3.661 billion, up 168% year over year, against consensus clustered near $3.54–3.55 billion, alongside a GAAP diluted loss of $1.27 a share. [fact, primary source: CoreWeave investor release] The company raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $13.0–13.3 billion (from August's $12.4–13.2 billion), raised its exit-2026 annualized revenue-run-rate target to $20–21 billion, and disclosed a $132.5 billion backlog; adjusted EBITDA came in at $2.256 billion, a 62% margin. Its after-hours reaction was likewise reported inconsistently [flag]. Read together, both chipmakers printed clean beats-and-raises the same afternoon Nvidia's leadership news broke — a reminder that Thursday's broad-market selling was concentrated on Nvidia specifically rather than reflecting a reassessment of semiconductor fundamentals generally.
