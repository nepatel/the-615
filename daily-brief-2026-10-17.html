Lead

Hot Prints, Hawkish Fed, Hormuz Crude

September's inflation trio comes in scorching hot, and a Fed that hiked a month ago won't rule out doing it again

September's inflation data arrived this week and all three prints ran hot. CPI, released Wednesday, showed headline inflation at 3.7% year-over-year (+0.6% m/m), both above the roughly 3.6% consensus cited in issue No. 25, with core at 2.6% y/y (+0.3% m/m) — both up from August's 3.4%/2.4% (BLS). [DEVELOPING] Thursday's PPI was worse: headline final-demand producer prices rose 1.3% m/m against a roughly 0.5% consensus, and 6.3% y/y; the closest BLS core measure — final demand less foods, energy and trade — rose 0.5% m/m and 5.0% y/y (BLS). [NEW] Advance retail sales for September rose 0.8% m/m to $745.0bn, also beating a roughly 0.3% estimate, though the control group (ex-auto, ex-gas) posted a statistically insignificant +0.2% (Census). [NEW] Initial jobless claims for the week ended Oct. 10 were 199,000, roughly in line, with continuing claims at 1.710mn (DOL) — the labor side of the ledger, unlike prices, isn't flashing anything unusual. [NEW]

The read: this looks less like a demand-side inflation surprise than a supply-side one landing on a services base that hadn't cooled as much as hoped. [read] PPI's energy and trade components are the likely transmission channel from this month's oil spike — Brent is up from the $104.72 Oct. 9 settle cited in issue No. 25 to Friday's $109.86 close, itself a continuation of the Hormuz/Saudi-strike risk this brief has tracked since early October — and that pass-through has further to run into October's CPI print. [read] Retail sales' control-group softness, by contrast, suggests the consumer isn't keeping pace with headline spending once autos and gas are stripped out — a split tape, not a clean "hot economy" story. [read]

This lands on a Fed that is hiking, not cutting: the FOMC raised rates a quarter point to 3.75%-4.00% on Sept. 16 and penciled in another hike by year-end. [fact, continuity — supersedes this brief's prior "cut odds" framing, which was tracking a now-stale pricing window] Hike odds for the Oct. 27-28 meeting spiked toward 47% intraday on the CPI/PPI/retail-sales trio, per CME FedWatch-sourced reporting, before settling back to roughly 15-17% after Chair Kevin Warsh's Oct. 16 remarks in Bangkok, where he said "I don't prejudge meetings," described "a synchronous tightening in monetary policy in most places around the world," and called the AI buildout's economic transition "very bumpy." [fact] Goldman Sachs economists read Warsh's comments as signaling an October pause with "a second and final hike" in December. [fact, single-sourced via one bank note]

Equities took the hot prints badly. Wednesday's CPI release drove the S&P 500 down 1.1% to 7,723.59 and the Nasdaq Composite down 1.9% to 26,692.87; stocks never fully recovered, and Friday's cash close was S&P 500 7,659.44 (-1.09%), Nasdaq Composite 26,140.03 (-2.14%, concentrated in chip and AI-infrastructure names — CoreWeave and Micron both cited among the week's worst decliners), and Dow 52,002.41 (-0.34%, cushioned by energy). [fact] For the week: S&P -1.95%, Nasdaq -4.48%, Dow +0.67%. [fact] The 10-year yield closed Friday at 5.09% (Treasury.gov), up from 4.64% on Aug. 25 — a climb consistent with a hiking Fed and a reasserting inflation risk premium, not the cutting cycle this brief tracked over the summer. [fact/read]

Watch: whether Oct. 22's claims print holds near cycle lows — a resilient labor market strengthens the case for a December hike regardless of what the Fed does on Oct. 28 — and whether the Hormuz/Saudi war, the proximate driver of this month's energy pass-through, escalates further before the FOMC meets. [read] A Fed debating whether to hike again, into an oil shock it didn't cause, is a different problem than the one markets were pricing as recently as this summer. [read]

Economics

Beyond the headline prints: September's retail-sales control group — ex-autos, ex-gas, the cleaner read on underlying demand — rose just 0.2%, with the Census confidence interval including zero, i.e., not statistically distinguishable from flat. [NEW] August retail sales were revised up to 1.3% m/m from an initial 1.1%. [fact] Initial claims' 4-week moving average ticked up slightly to 198,500 (+250), still historically low; insured unemployment fell to 1.710mn (DOL). [fact] None of the three labor/demand prints argue for weakness — the hot inflation data is a prices story, not a growth story, which is precisely why it reads as a complication for the Fed rather than grounds for a pause on its own. [read]
