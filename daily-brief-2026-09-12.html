Lead

HOT CORE CPI, A FED PRICING A HIKE, AND A STOCK MARKET THAT SHRUGGED

A hot core CPI print pushes Fed hike odds above 85%, but stocks rally through it and oil — this week's other inflation driver — reverses hard on a Gulf diplomacy report

Friday delivered two readings that should have pointed the same direction and didn't. August CPI matched consensus on the headline — +0.4% m/m, +3.4% y/y, unchanged from July — but core came in at +0.3% m/m against a 0.2% consensus, the largest core advance since April, while core held at +2.4% y/y. [fact] BLS attributed more than a third of the headline gain to a 3.9% m/m jump in gasoline prices, meaning the "hot" surprise was concentrated in the core reading — shelter, services, or goods pass-through — not simply energy passing through twice. [fact]

The market's first move was mechanical: CME FedWatch's odds of a hike at the Sept. 15-16 FOMC — a hike, the unusual direction this cycle has been pricing since Thursday's oil/PPI shock — jumped further, to somewhere in the 86-91% range depending on the snapshot, up from Friday morning's already-elevated 69-70%. [fact] The 10-year yield touched 4.979% intraday before closing at 4.96%, just short of the 5% level that's held since November 2023; the 2-year's move was reported larger, to 4.63% by one source, though that figure comes from a single yield-snapshot service and isn't independently corroborated here. [flag: single-sourced] A short end moving harder than the long end is the same bear-flattening signature this issue has seen before an inflation-driven hike repricing: it says the market is pricing a near-term policy response to a supply shock, not a reassessment of where inflation or growth sit over the next decade. [read]

What didn't follow the script was equities. All three major averages snapped Thursday's four-session losing streak: the Dow rose 0.98% to 52,573.29, the S&P 500 gained 0.86% to 7,656.98, and the Nasdaq added 0.96% to 26,333.04. The VIX fell 11.21% to 15.84, its sharpest one-day drop of the week. [fact] That's not the reaction textbook logic would predict for a session that also saw the market price in higher hike odds — unless the second half of Friday's data explains it: oil, the week's other inflation driver, reversed hard. Brent settled at $104.61, down 2.8% on the day (still up 8.7% on the week), and WTI fell 2.4% to $100.05 (+9.4% on the week), with the pullback tied to reports that GCC and Iranian diplomats are set to meet Monday on a temporary Hormuz shipping arrangement. [fact, single-sourced this run via CNBC]

Put together, Friday's tape reads as a market treating the CPI print and the oil reversal as separate signals rather than reconciling them into one view: hike odds went up because the data said so, but risk appetite came back because the marginal driver of this week's inflation scare — oil, not core services — looked like it might already be turning, days before the Fed even meets. [read] That creates a real setup risk into Wednesday: if the Fed hikes on Thursday's spike and Friday's CPI, and the Gulf talks produce even a partial de-escalation before the decision, policymakers could find themselves tightening into a shock the market has already started pricing out — a "one meeting behind" problem rather than a policy mistake, but one this issue will be watching for in Wednesday's statement language. [read] Nothing about Monday's reported meeting has been confirmed as an actual, scheduled, on-the-record event by any of the parties involved; treat it as a reported signal that already moved the oil market, not a fact about what will happen Monday. [flag]

[fact] These are Bab el-Mandeb/Red Sea developments, a distinct chokepoint from the Strait of Hormuz itself, though a Houthi-controlled Red Sea coastline widens the set of maritime pressure points a Gulf settlement would eventually need to address.
