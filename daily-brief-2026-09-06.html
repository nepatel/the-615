Lead

RATES · OIL · IRAN

A hot jobs print and a Hormuz missile exchange erase two weeks of dovish drift in 48 hours

Friday's August jobs report reversed Thursday's Fed-split rally almost exactly. Nonfarm payrolls rose 162,000 in August — roughly triple the Dow Jones consensus of about 53,000 — while June and July were revised up a combined 55,000 (July's shock -23,000 miss now reads +21,000). Unemployment held at 4.1%, average hourly earnings rose 0.3% to $37.75, and labor-force participation ticked up to 61.6%. [NEW] [fact] Markets read it as good-news-is-bad-news for the Warsh–Waller split flagged in Thursday's issue: CME FedWatch's implied odds of a September hike jumped to 60.4% at Friday's close from roughly a coin flip Thursday, the ten-year yield resumed rising to 4.78%, the two-year touched its highest level since January 2025, and equities gave back Thursday's gains — the Dow fell 271.85 points (-0.51%) to 53,414.25, the S&P 500 dropped 0.38% to 7,718.60, the Nasdaq slipped 0.29% to 26,506.99. [fact] Fifth Third's Bill Adams put the read plainly: the print puts the Fed "squarely on controlling inflation when they meet next in September." [read] In three sessions, hike odds ran from 65.9% (Wed) to a coin flip (Thu, on Gov. Waller's dovish break) to 60.4% (Fri, on payrolls) — the committee split is still live, and Waller's own named tiebreaker, the Aug. CPI print due Sept. 11, now carries more weight than ever.

Then Saturday, the war caught up with the rhetoric. Iran fired multiple ballistic missiles at a U.S. Navy carrier and a guided-missile destroyer in the Gulf; CENTCOM says both ships evaded them with no American casualties. [NEW] [fact] Washington retaliated within hours, striking and disabling or destroying three more Iranian crude tankers — the Downy off Kharg Island, the Stark 1 near Jask, and the Kylo in the Gulf of Oman, whose crew was ordered to abandon ship. [fact] CENTCOM's Adm. Brad Cooper: "If you shoot at two of our ships, we will impose an even higher economic cost — taking out three of yours." Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth vowed to keep sinking tankers "if Iran shoots at U.S. ships." [fact] Iran's Khatam al-Anbiya command threatened retaliation "more severe than before"; separately, the IRGC navy claimed it hit three more tankers and three U.S.-linked vessels overnight — unconfirmed by the U.S. as of this filing. [fact, flag: Iranian claim, not independently confirmed]

The timing matters. Less than 24 hours before Saturday's missile exchange, Trump had called the six-week-old conflict "small potatoes" and Vance said he "wouldn't call it a war" — a framing the administration has not publicly reconciled with a carrier group now dodging live ballistic missiles. [read] Brent was already up roughly 9% for the week into Friday's settle, its best week since mid-July, on the earlier round of strikes alone; Saturday's escalation postdates that settle entirely, so none of it is in a confirmed price yet. [fact/read] With OPEC+'s core group meeting today to set Q4 quotas (outcome not yet confirmed as of this filing) and the Aug. CPI print — the data point Waller named as decisive for his vote — due Thursday, a Monday oil gap-up would land at nearly the worst possible moment for the Fed's inflation math, just as the committee is genuinely split for the first time this cycle. [read] That is the setup this issue's reader carries into Monday: a jobs print that just re-hawkished the curve, a war the administration calls over that the Pentagon is actively fighting, and a CPI print eight days out that both sides of the Fed have now flagged as the thing that decides September.
