Okay, the lead. Let's correct something first. This show has been telling you for days that the live question at this week's Fed meeting was a possible rate cut, with pretty thin odds — call it a one-in-five chance, unchanged. That was already out of date. Here's what actually happened: back on September sixteenth, the Fed raised rates a quarter point, to a range of three-seventy-five to four percent. That was their first hike since the summer of twenty twenty-three. They cited a strong labor market, inflation that's still too high, and the energy shock from the Iran war. And here's the part that matters more than anything happening this week: in the projections that came out of that September meeting, sixteen of the eighteen Fed officials said they expect at least one more quarter-point hike is likely appropriate before the year is out. That's not a coin flip. That's the committee all but telling you where it's going next — in December.

So walk into Wednesday with that in mind. The market's pricing a hold this week at something like nine-in-ten odds, a hike at under one-in-ten, and a cut basically off the table entirely. But December is a real live wire — traders have a hike there at somewhere around three-in-four odds, up from about seven-in-ten shortly after Kevin Warsh took over as Fed chair back in the spring.

Here's the mechanism worth sitting with. This isn't your classic overheating economy with runaway demand. The inflation the Fed's fighting is mostly cost-push — it's the same Iran war oil shock that put Brent at a wartime high on Friday, layered on top of heavy AI-related capital spending and an economy that just won't slow down. A December hike aimed at an energy-driven inflation problem risks dragging down growth without doing much to fix what's actually driving prices higher. And Chair Warsh hasn't had to square that tension yet, because unlike most Fed chairs, he doesn't like to telegraph his next move — he said just last week he won't commit to an outcome ahead of time. Which is a little strange, given that the Fed's own September projections already more or less told everyone what's coming.

And the market's reaction this morning backs that reading up. Futures are a little higher, oil and gold are both easing rather than rallying together — that's not a clean risk-on morning or a clean risk-off morning, it's a market repricing around hike risk that's been pushed to December, not resolved. Watch Wednesday's statement for any language pointing toward December, and watch Thursday morning's growth and inflation data — that's the next real data point before the committee meets again.

One more wrinkle worth flagging: there's actually a dispute buried in the Fed's own numbers. Most of the reporting on September's projections says the committee is pointing toward one more hike by year-end. But at least one other read of those same projections says the median official already has two hikes baked in and nothing more coming. We can't reconcile those two readings this morning, so take the December-hike framing as the more widely supported one, not the only one on the table.
