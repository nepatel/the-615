The 6:15, for Thursday, August 27th. Here's what moved overnight: Nvidia blew way past its own guidance again, and it's dragging the whole market higher into the open.

Quick numbers before we dig in. Brent's sitting in the high eighties, WTI's just under eighty-three, the ten-year's at about four and two-thirds percent, the dollar index is just under ninety-nine, stock futures are up solidly this morning on Nvidia — S&P futures up around seven-tenths of a percent, Nasdaq futures up almost a full percent — the VIX is down near fifteen and a half, gold's just under forty-six fifty an ounce, the Nikkei and the Hang Seng both closed a touch lower overnight, gas is holding just about four-ten a gallon nationally, the Strait of Hormuz is now at day one seventy-nine of its closure with tanker traffic still near historic lows, and on the sports side, the Yankees beat Houston nine to three.

Okay, the lead. Nvidia reported after the bell Wednesday, and the numbers were big even by Nvidia standards. Ninety-six point two billion dollars in revenue, well past what Wall Street was looking for, with data center revenue alone up over a hundred percent from a year ago. Earnings beat too. But the number that actually moved the market was the guidance — Nvidia's telling investors to expect a hundred and eight billion dollars in revenue next quarter, which blew past what analysts had penciled in by close to four billion dollars. The CFO even said she expects around seventy percent revenue growth all the way out into fiscal twenty-twenty-eight, which is an unusually specific promise for a company that normally only guides one quarter at a time. The stock jumped about six percent after hours and again in this morning's premarket, and it dragged the futures market up with it. And buried in the numbers was another data point worth knowing: the big cloud companies — the hyperscalers — more than doubled their spending with Nvidia year over year, which tells you this is still being driven by the biggest players building out capacity, not by some broader wave of smaller enterprise customers. Salesforce and CrowdStrike also beat Wednesday night, both raising their own guidance, so this wasn't a one-company story — it was a good night across software and AI infrastructure generally, and honestly a sharper move than Monday's Iran sanctions news produced in either direction.

Here's why it matters beyond just tech stocks, though. The other big release Wednesday was the second read on second-quarter GDP, and growth itself didn't change — still one and a half percent. But the inflation number buried inside that report got revised up, not down — headline inflation now running around five and a third percent, core around three and six-tenths. That's the same direction as the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, which also came out hotter than hoped. So you've got a red-hot earnings season landing at the exact same moment inflation data is ticking the wrong way — and that's an awkward combination the day before the Fed's big Jackson Hole conference kicks off, two days before the new Fed chair, Kevin Warsh, gives his very first speech in that job. A strong economy and sticky inflation together argue against the rate cuts markets have been hoping for.

And there's a wrinkle on top of that. Treasury Secretary Bessent has been buying back long-term bonds more aggressively this month — he basically doubled the size of the program — and that's helped push yields down. Sounds good on the surface, but bond strategists are saying it's actually muddying the signal Warsh needs from the market to figure out where to set policy. So Nvidia's blowout quarter doesn't really solve Warsh's problem — it might even complicate it, because it tells him the economy's fine at the same time the inflation data says he can't relax.

On politics: Lisa Cook's lawyer sent a formal letter to the White House Wednesday, right at the deadline, pushing back hard on the mortgage-fraud allegations behind Trump's push to remove her from the Fed board, calling them baseless. The ball's in Trump's court now on whether he tries to remove her again. Separately, the government funding fight in Congress is still unresolved, still no new movement there.

On the economy: initial jobless claims are due out later this morning and hadn't printed as of when we wrote this. The most recent reading, from a couple weeks back, was two-oh-six thousand, better than expected, so the labor market still isn't showing cracks. Buried in that same GDP report was a strong upward revision to corporate profits — up over four hundred billion dollars in the quarter, more than five times the prior quarter's gain — so the underlying economy looks like it strengthened more than the headline growth number alone suggests. Jackson Hole itself just opens today with registration and some academic sessions — the real headline event, Warsh's speech, is tomorrow morning, and he's been calling it a blank piece of paper, refusing to preview what he'll say.

On business: the other big story from Wednesday was Dick's Sporting Goods, which cratered thirty percent in a single session — its worst day in years — after missing earnings and cutting its outlook. The core Dick's business actually grew nearly five percent, so this isn't a story about the parent company struggling — it's the Foot Locker business they acquired that's dragging things down, with sales there down almost four percent as athletic footwear stays heavily promotional. On the flip side, Intuit had a strong quarter, beating estimates comfortably on both revenue and earnings and guiding to double-digit growth for next year. And Canada now has a firm date for retaliating against U.S. tariffs — dollar for dollar, starting September eighth, hitting steel, dairy, appliances and a handful of other sectors.

On technology: Marvell reports tonight, and after Nvidia's number, expectations are elevated for the rest of the chip sector. Nvidia also flagged rising costs for memory chips, which is pressuring margins industry-wide, not just for them.

On geopolitics: Russia and Ukraine kept trading heavy strikes overnight — well over a hundred drones launched by Russia, and Ukraine hit a Russian logistics hub and an oil refinery in return. No sign either side is slowing down.

And on Iran and the strait: Oman's foreign minister met with his Iranian counterpart in Tehran this week to talk about Hormuz specifically, but no breakthrough came out of it, and Tehran was quick to say the meeting had nothing to do with Washington. That unattributed tanker strike from earlier in the week is now testing Washington's own claim that the strait is still functioning normally, and the traffic numbers back up the skepticism — one tracker counted just two tankers crossing on a single day this week, the lowest in months and well below the recent average. Iran's new permit-and-toll system for the strait, which threatens detention for anyone who doesn't comply, looks like the bigger reason ships are avoiding the route altogether rather than risking it.

Quick sports wrap: Yankees beat Houston nine to three behind a big go-ahead hit from Cody Bellinger — they're four games back of Tampa Bay but still holding the top wild card spot. Knicks are in the off-season, with Josh Hart's contract extension still unsigned, reportedly trending toward the sixty-to-sixty-two-million-dollar range. Northwestern's off-season too, opening their season a week from Saturday.
