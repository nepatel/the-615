The 6:15, for Wednesday, October 28th. Here's what moved overnight: this brief has to start with a correction — the Fed's actual chair is Kevin Warsh, not Jerome Powell, and the actual policy rate is in the high threes, not the mid-threes, both of which this show got wrong yesterday. More on that in a second, because today happens to be the day it matters most — the Fed's rate decision lands this afternoon.

Quick scan of the board: today brings the Fed's decision at two this afternoon and Chair Warsh's press conference a half hour later, with Microsoft and Alphabet reporting earnings tonight; tomorrow brings the first read on third-quarter growth, the Fed's favorite inflation gauge, and Amazon's earnings; OPEC-plus meets Saturday; and the midterms are six days out, with the House leaning solidly Democratic in prediction markets but the Senate genuinely a coin flip depending which market you look at. Brent's around one fourteen fifty a barrel this morning, WTI just over a hundred and one, both down from yesterday. The ten-year's just under five percent. Futures are basically flat to slightly up. The fear gauge eased to about seventeen and a half. Gold's back above four thousand dollars. Gas prices fell for a second straight day. And in sports: the Yankees' season has been over since early October, the Knicks beat Detroit last night to avoid an oh-and-four start, Northwestern's on a bye at six and one, and the Brewers survived a walk-off loss in extra innings Tuesday night but still lead the World Series three games to one, with a chance to close it out tonight in Tampa.

Now, the correction, and the lead. Here's what happened: the Federal Reserve's chair is Kevin Warsh, not Powell. The Senate confirmed him back in May, and he's been running the committee for over five months now. Powell's still on the board as a governor, but he hasn't chaired a meeting since mid-May. And the policy rate itself isn't sitting where this brief said yesterday — the Fed actually raised rates a quarter point back in September, its first hike in three years, putting the target at three-quarters to four percent, not the mid-threes range this show carried yesterday. Both of those were just wrong, and they're fixed as of today. The honest explanation is that this brief tracked both facts correctly back in September, then the Fed topic went quiet for a month between meetings, and the old, outdated version crept back in when Fed coverage picked back up yesterday. Nothing else in yesterday's reporting is affected — just those two facts, and they're corrected now.

So here's today, for real. This is Warsh's second rate decision since that September hike. The meeting wraps up with the decision at two this afternoon, and his press conference follows at two-thirty — no fresh economic projections at this meeting, so no new dot plot to parse. A hold is priced as close to a sure thing as these things get — prediction markets have it at roughly ninety-five percent odds of no change, with hike odds in the single digits and cut odds basically zero. The standard reference tool for this, the CME's FedWatch page, was down again this morning, so this brief is leaning on those prediction-market numbers instead. Here's the thing worth actually paying attention to: September's hike wasn't a one-off the committee wants to take back. The minutes from that meeting said most participants thought another increase would likely be appropriate by year end. So a hold today isn't the Fed softening — it's a pause in the middle of a hiking cycle, not a turn toward cutting. Watch what Warsh actually says at two-thirty for any hint of whether December's still in play.

Oil, meanwhile, eased rather than spiking on fresh Hormuz claims — both benchmarks are trading below where they were yesterday, even as Iran's army reframed Monday's tanker-fire incident as an alleged mine strike and threatened legal action against the ship's owner. If a real energy-driven inflation shock were building, you'd expect to see it in crude by now, and it's not there — which takes one hawkish argument off the table for this afternoon.

And then there's Big Tech, landing right on top of all this. Microsoft and Alphabet report after the close tonight, Amazon follows tomorrow — all three within about a day of the Fed decision, and a day ahead of tomorrow's growth and inflation data. Analyst estimates for all three are genuinely scattered across different trackers right now, so no specific numbers here. But the setup is this: a Fed that holds without signaling the hiking cycle is over, landing hours before two enormous earnings reports the market's already pricing for strength, doesn't leave much room for either company to disappoint quietly.

Quickly on politics and the economy: a Reuters-Ipsos poll from last week put presidential approval at thirty-one percent, the lowest either of this administration's approval readings have gone — though other polling averages show it higher, in the high thirties, so there's real disagreement across pollsters right now. On the calendar, tomorrow brings both the first estimate of third-quarter economic growth and the September inflation reading the Fed watches most closely, both at eight-thirty in the morning — though one calendar source has that inflation number landing a week later instead, so that date isn't fully nailed down. No sign of any government funding fight disrupting this week's data releases, which is itself worth noting given how often that's happened lately. Separately, a widely watched real-time growth tracker out of the Atlanta Fed has been bouncing around in the high fives to low sixes percent for the quarter — a rough guide, not a hard forecast, but another reason Thursday's official number matters.

On Iran and the strait: beyond the mine-strike story already covered, the share of tankers running dark on tracking systems actually pulled back overnight, though the tracking site changed how it measures its baseline, so that's not a clean apples-to-apples comparison. The actual ship-transit count is still stuck at roughly four vessels a day against a normal baseline of eighty-five, now ten days without an update. Monday's explosion claim near Sirik, and yesterday's claim from Iran's military that the strait is completely closed, are both still unconfirmed by any Western monitoring group. Nothing's moved diplomatically — Iran's seven-point reopening offer is still sitting on the table, still unanswered. OPEC-plus meets Saturday with next month's output levels already locked in, unchanged.

Sports, quickly. Yankees: season's been over since early October, nothing new. The Knicks beat Detroit one eleven to ninety-seven last night at the Garden, avoiding an oh-and-four start — Towns had twenty-seven and twenty boards, Brunson had twenty-three and seven assists; they're one and three now, next up at Philadelphia Friday. Northwestern's on a bye at six and one, back in action a week from Saturday against Iowa. And the World Series took a turn — the Rays won Game Four in extra innings on a walk-off single Tuesday night, avoiding the sweep, but Milwaukee still leads three games to one. Game Five is tonight in Tampa, with the Rays facing elimination again; if they win, the series shifts back to Milwaukee for Games Six and Seven this weekend.
