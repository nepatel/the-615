The 6:15, for Tuesday, October 13th. Here's what moved overnight: Houthi missiles have now hit Riyadh's main airport three days running, Trump says the US may join Saudi Arabia's fight against them, and in the middle of it, Trump let slip an admission that the US killed so many Iranian leaders that it's not clear who's left to negotiate with.

Here's the board in one breath: oil's down about two percent this morning to the low hundreds for Brent after jumping nearly two percent yesterday on all the Saudi news; the ten-year's sitting around five and a quarter percent, little changed with the bond market closed for Columbus Day, and nobody's got a fresh two-year quote today; the dollar index is a touch softer; S&P futures are roughly flat and Nasdaq futures are down about half a percent; the VIX is near fifteen; gold's up almost half a percent near forty-two hundred; Tokyo closed down close to two percent while Hong Kong and Shanghai both edged higher, and Frankfurt's up around half a percent at midday; national gas prices actually eased a touch to about four thirty-four a gallon; Wells Fargo and JPMorgan open bank earnings season this morning, not out yet as I'm recording, with CPI landing tomorrow morning and producer prices the day after; the Strait of Hormuz saw a dozen tankers hit in the week through last Monday by one count, with strikes continuing nearly every day since and transit traffic still running a small fraction of its pre-war pace; and on the sports side, the Yankees' season is over, the Knicks are off to an oh-and-two preseason, and Northwestern blew out Ball State to go four and one.

Now the lead. Houthi missiles have hit Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport three days in a row. Saturday's strike killed thirteen people, including one American, and wounded more than three hundred. Sunday's follow-up killed one more. Monday brought another claimed missile strike plus a drone attack on a Saudi air base, which Saudi forces say they intercepted. The airport's been shut since Saturday. Delta's pushed its planned Atlanta to Riyadh route all the way back to next March. And overnight into today, the Houthis say Saudi jets hit back at them on Yemeni soil.

Trump said over the weekend the US, quote, "may" join the Saudi campaign against the Houthis, and by Monday he was calling their behavior, quote, "misbehaving very badly." A Houthi spokesman told the French press agency that any American involvement would bring a, quote, "swift and painful response," and specifically named Saudi oil and airport workers as legitimate targets. That's a direct threat against the kind of energy infrastructure this show has been tracking all year.

Here's the connective tissue, and it's the most important line in today's issue. In remarks this week, Trump said the US is struggling to negotiate with Iran because, in his words, "we might have gone too far with the leaders, because we don't know who the hell we're dealing with anymore." Think about what that actually is: an American president admitting, out loud, that killing so much of Iran's regime leadership has made it harder, not easier, to cut a deal. That's not a talking point, that's a mechanism. And it matters today because a Houthi front gives Iran a way to keep escalating by proxy, through a group that doesn't depend on Iran's own depleted leadership to act. That's happening right as Washington has tried to put a hard deadline on itself not to strike Iran directly before the midterms.

Diplomatically, nothing's moving. Iran's security chief said over the weekend that the American reply to Iran's own plan to reopen the strait was, quote, "insufficient." Qatar's Emir came out today calling for doubled efforts to protect Gulf shipping, which tells you the anxiety isn't just a US-Iran-Saudi triangle anymore, it's regional.

And the market reaction is genuinely messy. Oil jumped nearly two percent yesterday on all of this, then gave more than that back this morning, and nobody has a clean story for why — maybe profit-taking, maybe positioning ahead of bank earnings and CPI, maybe traders deciding a Houthi-focused response is lower-intensity than a direct Iran campaign. Meanwhile the physical oil data — the actual barrels moving, tracked by Kpler — still shows Middle East exports running at or above pre-war levels, even as attack counts hit their worst pace of the entire war. Those two facts coexist because dark, trackerless shipping and rerouting are absorbing a lot of the disruption. That gap between the headline risk and the physical flow is something to watch, not something to resolve too quickly.

On politics: California's wealth tax fight is the marquee item on next month's ballot. Proposition 40 would tax net worth above a threshold aimed at billionaires, and two industry-backed counter-measures qualified after its backers refused to pull it despite pressure from the governor. If it passes, even if it ends up tied up in court, it becomes the first state-level precedent other legislatures can point to.

On economics: tomorrow's CPI at eight thirty is the next real catalyst. The Cleveland Fed's model points to inflation ticking up to around three and six tenths percent from last month's three and four tenths, though that's a model estimate, not a surveyed forecast. Rate-hike odds for the Fed's month-end meeting are still low, in the high teens to twenty percent, holding at the three-three-quarters to four percent range set last month. Gas prices actually ticked down slightly at the pump even with oil's whipsaw.

On business: Wells Fargo and JPMorgan kick off bank earnings this morning. Neither's out yet. Street estimates have Wells Fargo's per-share profit up nicely year over year, and JPMorgan's similarly higher, though both banks have beaten estimates pretty consistently lately, so the bar is high. Separately, CCC Intelligent Solutions jumped almost ten percent yesterday on buyout talk, and eBay rose more than five percent after a Morgan Stanley upgrade.

On technology: the memory chip selloff tied to China's CXMT is still working through the sector — Sandisk and Micron both fell again yesterday. Worth noting, CXMT makes a different kind of memory chip than the kind Sandisk sells, so some of that reaction looks more like fear of what's coming than a direct hit today. No major AI lab released anything new that we could verify, fourth day in a row we're saying that.

Geopolitics and Hormuz, briefly: beyond what's in the lead, a tanker was hit by a drone off the UAE this week, crew was fine, and another vessel was reportedly hit near Qatar's coast with casualties reported, though that's a single source. Iran's claiming a couple of new strikes that haven't been independently confirmed. And Secretary of State Rubio says the strait is open, while Iran's forces say it's closed and under their full control. Those two claims can't both be true, and neither one is being treated as fact here.

Sports, quickly. The Yankees' season is done, swept by the Rays, and there's no front-office move yet even though Aaron Judge is publicly pushing ownership to go all in — manager Aaron Boone and GM Brian Cashman both have unresolved contract situations. The Knicks are oh and two in the preseason, idle since a loss to Washington, playing at Toronto tonight. And Northwestern blew out Ball State forty-five to fourteen on Saturday to move to four and one on the season.
