The 6:15, for Sunday, October 4th. Here's what moved overnight: Friday's jobs report was ugly — the economy added just twenty-nine thousand jobs in September, way under what was expected, and it's the second soft data point this week after Wednesday's cool inflation read. Traders now think the Fed is very unlikely to hike later this month. Stocks loved it — the Nasdaq hit a fresh record. Bonds had a weirder day: yields actually dropped right when the report came out, then crept back up and finished a touch higher by the close. And oil's still elevated, because overnight Thursday brought yet another tanker hit in the Strait of Hormuz.

Quickly, the rest of the board: Brent settled a bit above a hundred and two dollars; we don't have a clean number for WTI this morning. The ten-year and the two-year are both around five and a quarter percent and the high fours, after an odd round trip we'll get into in a minute. The dollar eased slightly. Gold popped about a percent right after the jobs number hit, up near forty-two hundred, though we don't have Friday's final settle. Tokyo and Hong Kong both closed lower Friday; we don't have European closes. On Hormuz, a tanker caught fire after being hit by an unknown projectile Thursday — no casualties — but we don't have fresh numbers on ship traffic or how many vessels are still holding back from the strait, so take the oil-risk story as unresolved, not improving. OPEC's meeting today, no result yet. Coming up this week: the next inflation reports land October fourteenth and fifteenth, and they matter a lot more now given how far this week's data moved things. Here's the lead. Friday's jobs report was the week's second straight data point to come in soft. The government said employers added only twenty-nine thousand jobs in September — forecasters were looking for something like eighty-five to ninety thousand, so this was a real miss. Unemployment ticked up to four point two percent. And the two previous months got revised down by a combined sixty thousand jobs, with August cut almost in half from its first estimate. Coming right after Wednesday's cooler inflation number, these two reports together have done more to change the interest-rate conversation than anything since the Fed's hike a few weeks back.

The market reaction was fast and all one direction this time — different from Thursday, when the inflation data and the oil story were pulling against each other. The odds traders assign to another Fed rate hike later this month collapsed to somewhere around one in six, with better than four-in-five odds now on the Fed just holding steady. That's down sharply from Thursday's already-reduced number, and down a lot more from where things stood a week ago. December is now where the market expects the next hike, if there is one.

Stocks ran with the good-news-is-bad-news-is-actually-good-news logic: the S&P gained about three quarters of a percent, the Dow about half a percent, and the Nasdaq jumped over one percent to an all-time high, led by chip stocks — Nvidia touched a record intraday. That's a continuation of Thursday, when stocks shrugged off conflicting signals and just kept climbing.

Bonds told a trickier story. Yields fell the moment the jobs number hit, as you'd expect when hike odds collapse — but then they reversed over the course of the day, and the ten-year and two-year both ended up finishing a bit higher than where they started. That round trip suggests the market's initial relief gave way to some worry that a jobs miss this big might mean something more than just one soft month — maybe the early signs of a real slowdown, not just a single noisy report. The dollar eased a little, and gold spiked about a percent right on the release before giving some of that back.

Oil is sitting apart from all of this, same as Thursday. Brent settled Friday above a hundred and two dollars — still elevated — and the reason isn't really about the Fed at all. It's the Strait of Hormuz again: another tanker got hit by an unidentified projectile Thursday, and this one caused an actual fire. We don't have fresh data this morning on ship traffic through the strait or how many vessels are holding back, so there's no way to say yet whether that attack is a one-off or the start of another cluster like we saw in late September.

So here's where the week sits: the Fed now has real cover to just stand pat at its late-October meeting, unless the picture flips again before the next inflation reports in two weeks. Today's OPEC meeting could add another variable on the oil side, and we don't have a result yet. And whether Iran's government says anything public about the American proposal they've been reviewing since midweek is probably the other thing most likely to move markets when they reopen tomorrow.

On economics: Thursday's manufacturing survey also came in a bit soft, reinforcing the theme that the data this week points toward a cooling economy rather than a hot one — which raises the real question of whether the Fed's done hiking entirely, not just pausing for one meeting. The inflation reports in two weeks now matter even more given how far this week's two data points moved the needle.

On business: Nike's results Thursday night were a blowout on the surface — earnings way above what was expected — but a big chunk of that was a one-time nearly billion-dollar benefit tied to an expected tariff refund, so it's not a clean read on the underlying business. Revenue did beat too, a smaller but real beat. That's seven straight quarters now where Nike's topped expectations. The company also announced a new operating model it's calling Pace, meant to scale what's been working in its sport-by-sport strategy — we don't have much detail on that yet beyond the name.

On geopolitics: Russia hit Ukraine hard again overnight — more than a hundred drones and a few ballistic missiles — Ukrainian air defense knocked down a good chunk of them, but enough got through to hit over twenty locations, and one region in the north, Chernihiv, lost power for around fifty thousand customers after the energy grid took a hit.

On Iran and the strait: beyond that Thursday tanker fire, there's no public update on where Tehran's review of the American proposal stands. Nothing's been disclosed by either side, and it's been quiet since midweek — which given this conflict's pattern doesn't really tell us anything either way.

And sports. The Yankees lost game one of their playoff series at Tampa Bay, one to nothing — a solo homer was the only run of the game, and the Rays' pitcher struck out ten over eight strong innings. The Knicks have no moves to report; their preseason opens tomorrow at Philadelphia. And Northwestern beat Penn State at home Friday night, twenty-two to twenty-one, improving to three and one.
