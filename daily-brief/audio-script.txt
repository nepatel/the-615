The 6:15, for Monday, November ninth. Here's what moved overnight — or rather, what didn't: markets spent the weekend digesting last week's rout, and the real test is tomorrow, when October's inflation report lands.

Quick lay of the land before we get into it. Veterans Day today, so the bond market's closed but stocks trade a full session; tomorrow brings the October inflation print, followed by Applied Materials and Cisco earnings Wednesday and producer prices Friday; the S&P closed Friday around seventy-three seventy-seven, the Nasdaq near twenty-four six, the Dow just over fifty-one two, after Thursday's three-trillion-dollar rout and Friday's partial bounce; Brent settled Friday just under ninety-eight and a half, WTI just under ninety-one, both barely moved on the day; the ten-year sits around four eighty-six; the VIX fell back to the mid-twenties after spiking into the high twenties Thursday; and gold settled just over forty-oh-seventy-five. In the Gulf, Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed Sunday that three tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz — the third time in eleven days they've made almost that exact same claim, with nothing independently confirmed yet again. On the transactions side: nothing new — the Yankees are still waiting on their closer's decision.

Here's the lead. Friday's numbers finally settle the argument over last week's wild ride, and that hands the real test of this week over to tomorrow's inflation report.

Remember Thursday: a brutal, three-trillion-dollar selloff — the worst day for stocks since last spring — all concentrated in the AI and chip trade. Nvidia down about nine percent, Micron and AMD down more than fifteen at the worst point, the semiconductor index off twelve percent. Then Friday, a strong October jobs report — a hundred sixty-five thousand jobs added, way above the forty- to seventy-thousand economists expected, unemployment down to four point one percent — gave the market a reason to buy back in. By Friday's close, the S&P was basically flat on the week... well, down about two and a half percent, the Nasdaq down three to five and a half depending how you measure it, the Dow down under one percent. Not a great week, but nowhere near Thursday's panic.

Now here's the thing worth sitting with on a quiet Monday morning: oil barely moved through any of this. Brent gained three cents Friday. Three cents! If Thursday had really been about fear of a slowing economy, you'd expect oil to fall right alongside stocks — it didn't. That tells you this was a positioning unwind in a crowded trade, not a economic growth scare. Which means tomorrow's inflation number is the actual test, not a replay of last week. One forecaster we found — just one, so take it with a grain of salt — expects headline inflation to cool to three-tenths of a percent for the month, continuing to run a bit above three and a half percent for the year. Cool enough, and the relief rally in chips probably continues. Hot, and we're right back to asking whether the Fed can keep cutting rates into an economy that's still adding jobs this fast without reigniting the inflation fight everybody thought was finished. No Fed meeting until December ninth, so for now it's all about how the market reads the data, not how the Fed reacts to it.

And keep an eye on breadth. Thursday's drop was narrow — basically Nvidia, Micron, AMD, the semiconductor index, and the AI data-center names, the so-called neoclouds. Friday's bounce was the same narrow list bouncing back, those same names recovering in the high single digits. If tomorrow's inflation number moves that same cluster of stocks again, that confirms this is still a positioning story, confined to one crowded corner of the market. If it spreads out to the rest of the index either way, that's a different and more worrying signal — it would mean the market's repricing something bigger than just AI-spending anxiety.

One more thing on the overnight: the one actually new development is in the Gulf, and it reinforces that same disconnect we just talked about in oil. Iran made its tanker claim Sunday, and Brent moved three cents. Three cents, on a day when the IRGC said ships were on fire in the strait. That tells you traders are treating these claims as noise at this point, not as a real supply threat — which lines up with everything else we've been seeing out of the Hormuz numbers for weeks now: elevated crisis readings, a strait that's basically closed by transit count, but a market that's stopped flinching at each new claim.

On to the sections. Politics: good news on one open question — Ohio's governor's race, which we'd flagged as uncalled, is now resolved. Democrat Amy Acton beat Vivek Ramaswamy on election night, making her the state's first woman elected governor. That's part of the broader sweep we've been tracking — Democrats took both chambers of Congress, and Hakeem Jeffries is on track to be the first Black Speaker once the new Congress is seated. Kansas, though, is still too close to call — Republican Ty Masterson is ahead with ninety-nine percent of the votes counted, but it's not over yet.

Economics: tomorrow's inflation print is the whole story this week, with producer prices following Friday. Both of these are landing inside a compressed calendar that's still catching up from the shutdown delays earlier this year.

Business: watch CoreWeave's earnings tomorrow after the close — one of the AI infrastructure stocks that got hit hardest Thursday, and the first real look at how that business is actually doing since the market freaked out about AI spending. For context, a similar company, Iren, just reported about two hundred million in AI revenue against a quarterly loss of two hundred sixty-three million — that gives you a sense of the gap investors are nervous about. Applied Materials and Cisco report Wednesday; Applied Materials especially is a direct read on how much semiconductor-equipment spending is actually happening.

Technology: Anthropic's IPO is still in limbo. Back in the summer, bankers were telling investors an October listing could raise over a hundred billion dollars at something like a two-trillion-dollar valuation, after a funding round earlier this year that already valued the company near a trillion. That October window's now passed with no confirmation it actually happened. We're not going to repeat last issue's figure on that since we couldn't re-confirm it this time around — better to say nothing than to repeat a number we can't stand behind.

Iran and Hormuz: the big one here is already in the lead — Iran's claim about three tankers on fire Sunday, the third nearly identical version of that claim in under two weeks. Separately, the UK's maritime monitoring agency logged a different tanker fire off the coast of Oman the day before, which it hasn't been able to verify the cause of either. Shipping traffic through the strait, by different measures, is running anywhere from a sliver of normal to about a fifth of pre-war levels — different trackers, different methods, and they don't agree with each other, which we flag every single time because it matters.

Northwestern is three and seven after losing at home to Iowa Saturday, next up at Ohio State the following weekend.
