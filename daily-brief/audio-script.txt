The 6:15, for Thursday, November twelfth. Here's what moved overnight: the ten-year Treasury yield punched through five percent for the first time in weeks, after a hotter than expected inflation report, and even though stocks staged a furious comeback Wednesday on a blowout earnings report from an AI cloud company, that bond market move hasn't gone away — it kept getting worse into this morning.

Quickly, the rest of the board. Applied Materials reports earnings tonight after the close, and we're flagging that Cisco, which we'd told you was reporting alongside them, couldn't be confirmed this morning — more on that in a minute. Producer prices land tomorrow morning. Anthropic's IPO could price as soon as tomorrow, with a listing as early as Monday. The ten-year's just above five percent, the two-year's around four and three-quarters, the dollar index is near one-oh-three and climbing, gold's pulled back to around four thousand an ounce, the VIX is down near seventeen and a half, stocks in Asia were mixed to higher overnight, Europe closed higher yesterday, gas is hovering just above four dollars a gallon nationally, Iran's Revolutionary Guard says two oil tankers were disabled by mines in the Strait of Hormuz overnight — though that's not independently confirmed — and the Yankees' closer has until next Tuesday afternoon to decide on his qualifying offer.

Now the lead. Tuesday's inflation report came in hot on both the headline and the core number — a third straight month of stronger-than-expected underlying price growth. A Fed governor had already warned that a third hot core reading could put a bigger, half-point rate hike on the table in December, and bond traders believed him: the ten-year jumped about ten basis points in a single session to just under five percent, the two-year jumped even more, and futures moved to fully price in a December hike with more to come next year. Stocks took it badly Tuesday, giving back about a third of Monday's big oil-truce rally.

But Wednesday flipped the mood without flipping the rate story. Bond markets were closed for Veterans Day, but stocks traded, and CoreWeave — one of the big AI cloud infrastructure companies — put up a monster quarter the night before: revenue up about a hundred and seventy percent from a year ago, beating estimates by a healthy margin, and raised guidance for the rest of the year. That gave the AI trade its best hard evidence in a week that the spending boom is still working, and stocks rallied hard — the S&P up around one and a third percent, the Nasdaq up more. But here's the thing: that rally happened with the dollar still climbing and bond yields still pointed higher underneath it. That's not really a reversal of the hawkish mood — it's one good earnings report getting layered on top of it.

And Nvidia didn't even participate in the bounce. Its board has been meeting for two nights straight trying to decide the fate of CEO Jensen Huang, after a report that directors are actually weighing removing him, and the stock kept sliding. No decision as of this morning.

By this morning, the rate move just kept going — the ten-year crossed five percent for the first time we've confirmed in a while, the thirty-year's sitting just above five and a third. And weirdly, gold — which usually likes this kind of nervous market — actually fell, down about three percent from Monday, which tells you traders are pricing higher real interest rates rather than panic. Futures are choppy and a little lower again this morning, with Nvidia still the drag. The thing to watch: a five percent ten-year raises the cost of every long-term, capital-heavy bet out there, AI infrastructure very much included, at the exact moment CoreWeave's numbers are being used as proof that AI spending still pays off. Those two forces are pulling against each other inside the same stocks right now, and tomorrow's producer price report, plus whatever Nvidia's board finally decides, should tell us which one wins the week.

On politics: Kansas's governor's race still isn't officially called, but the Democrat's lead has widened to about seven thousand six hundred votes, a little under one percent of everything cast. Two separate audits are underway — a routine post-election audit and a closer-race audit triggered by how tight the margin is — and we still don't have an Associated Press call. The Republican hasn't conceded, the Democrat and the outgoing governor both say it's over.

On the economy beyond the lead: tomorrow's producer price report is expected by some to show the biggest year-over-year jump since twenty twenty-two, though we couldn't pin down a specific consensus number for that. And we're hearing the bond market is still waiting on both that number and some housing data before the next move.

On business: Applied Materials reports tonight, with Wall Street looking for revenue a bit above ten billion dollars and earnings around four dollars and six cents a share — not far from the company's own guidance. We're flagging again that Cisco's reporting date, which we told you yesterday was tonight too, couldn't be verified this morning, so don't assume that one. And digging a little deeper into CoreWeave's numbers: its net loss actually widened sharply from a year ago, mostly because interest expense on its debt nearly tripled — a trend worth watching given how much it's still spending to build out capacity.

On technology: that Nvidia board drama is the story — a second straight evening of meetings, with removing Huang reportedly one of the options on the table, alongside putting him on leave or a formal reprimand. No word yet. Meanwhile, Anthropic's IPO had a wild two days — a leaked price range in the seventies-to-eighties-per-share area got pulled back as inaccurate within hours, and its updated public filing now shows third-quarter revenue of about seventeen billion dollars, up more than eleven times from a year ago, but also an operating loss, because its compute costs are enormous — total compute commitments now run to roughly nine hundred billion dollars stretching out to twenty forty-five. They're reportedly aiming for something close to a two trillion dollar valuation, with pricing possibly as soon as tomorrow and a Nasdaq debut, under the ticker A-N-T, as early as Monday or as late as the following Wednesday, depending which report you believe.

On the Strait of Hormuz: that mine claim from Iran's Revolutionary Guard overnight — two tankers disabled — is so far only coming from Iranian state media, nothing independent backing it up yet. And Monday's proposed deal, where Iran would reopen the strait in exchange for the US lifting its blockade, hasn't moved either way since we last reported it — no confirmation, no denial. Traffic counts through the strait, meanwhile, depend entirely on who's counting: one tracker counted eight ships crossing on a single day earlier this month, another counted ten, a third counted just one — against a pre-war normal of something like eighty-five ships a day.

Sports, quickly. The Yankees' season is over; their closer, David Bednar, has until next Tuesday afternoon to accept or decline a twenty-three million dollar qualifying offer. The Knicks blew out Milwaukee by thirty-two points Tuesday night at the Garden, their fourth straight win, and they're now six and four. Northwestern is three and seven and heads to Ohio State Saturday.
