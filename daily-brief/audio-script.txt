Now the lead. Thursday was the clearest inflation scare of this whole war so far, and it came from two directions at once. Oil jumped nearly six percent on Brent and almost seven percent on WTI, both settling at their highest since May nineteenth — that's an extension of the escalation we've been tracking since Tuesday and Wednesday's strikes on Iranian tankers, a Jordanian air base, Saudi refining country, and a Black Sea terminal, working through the market with a bit of a lag rather than some brand-new flashpoint.

Same morning, the producer price report came in exactly in line on the headline — up point four percent, right on consensus. But underneath that in-line headline, the details were ugly. Energy prices in that report jumped over four percent, diesel alone jumped twenty-four percent, and the core measure — stripping out food, energy, and trade — was running almost five percent higher than a year ago. That's cost pressure building in the pipeline behind a headline number that looked tame, and bond traders read it exactly that way: the ten-year pushed up toward that five percent level, the highest since late twenty twenty-three.

Here's what makes this week different from prior inflation scares: it happened despite the Treasury actively trying to stop it. They ran a six billion dollar buyback of long-term debt this week specifically to cap long yields, and the Treasury Secretary went on a podcast and called any suggestion it isn't working "nonsense." The ten-year kept climbing anyway, right through the intervention. That sequencing matters more than this morning's inflation number, honestly — a policy tool built to hold yields down, visibly not doing that in real time, tells you the market's pricing something that a single data point can't easily fix. Oil-driven cost pressure and a little bit of a fiscal credibility worry, and today's consumer inflation report doesn't really speak to either of those directly. A soft number this morning would help the mood, but it wouldn't address what's actually been moving markets this week.
