Here's the lead. Nvidia reports tonight after the closing bell, and it's reporting into a market that's already in trouble. Monday was the worst trading session yet in the five days since Jensen Huang was forced out as CEO. The Nasdaq fell two and a half percent, its third straight losing day, putting the index into correction territory — more than ten percent below its October peak. The S&P 500 fell almost two percent, the Dow dropped about seven hundred forty points. And Nvidia's own stock fell another four percent on top of that, on top of the roughly four percent it lost the day Huang resigned. One market estimate has the stock down around twenty-nine percent and one point seven trillion dollars in value from its peak this year — that figure's from a single source, so treat it as directional, not exact. Coverage is pinning the broader selloff on a mix of Nvidia-specific governance jitters and renewed worry about chip-export policy, though we couldn't nail down the exact policy trigger ourselves this morning.

The leadership situation, meanwhile, keeps getting more tangled, not less. Nvidia's board said Friday it's still picking a search firm for a permanent CEO — no timeline. The Financial Times reported the board has reached out to Arm's CEO, Rene Haas, though Nvidia won't confirm it and says talking about candidates is premature. Online, a petition to reinstate Huang has passed a hundred seventy thousand signatures, with Elon Musk and other tech names publicly behind him. And Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent threw a new log on the fire Friday, telling CNBC the real question is whether Nvidia knew its customer in this whole Megaspeed affair, and publicly blaming Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's department for being slow to restrict the chip exports at the center of it. That's an unusually public cabinet-level finger-pointing.

And now there are two separate congressional tracks chasing Huang. House committees want him for a closed-door interview on December first. The Senate Banking Committee, separately, wants a public hearing with Huang on December third and with Lutnick on December tenth. Lutnick's office has agreed to show up voluntarily. Huang hasn't confirmed either date.

So tonight's print matters enormously. Nvidia reports under interim CEO Colette Kress, who's also still the chief financial officer. Wall Street's looking for revenue somewhere around a hundred ten billion dollars and earnings per share around two-fifty, against Nvidia's own guidance of about a hundred eight billion, give or take two percent. Analysts note Nvidia has beaten estimates in each of its last four quarters, by an average of nearly six points. The real question the market's asking is whether this is a Nvidia-specific governance mess, or an early warning sign for AI spending broadly — and the fact that CoreWeave, Applied Materials, and Cisco all beat estimates and raised guidance just last week argues the underlying demand is still there, even as Nvidia's own stock gets hit by company-specific trouble. Watch tonight for whether the call settles the stock down or sends it further into the hole, and whether there's any fresh word on a permanent successor.
