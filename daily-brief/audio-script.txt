Here's what moved overnight: Anthropic's new stock, ticker ANT, had an absolutely wild first day on the Nasdaq — opening thirty-four percent above its offer price, spiking above a hundred fifteen dollars, round-tripping all the way down to the low nineties, and finally closing up seventeen percent. Quickly, the rest of the board: gas prices keep easing, down to about three dollars sixty-nine cents a gallon nationally; most other market levels — oil, the dollar, gold, stocks in Asia and Europe — just weren't confirmable with clean, fresh numbers this morning, so treat anything you hear elsewhere with some skepticism; the Treasury curve's last solid read, from earlier this week, had the two-year around four point eight percent and the ten-year a touch above five; Hormuz tanker attacks appear to have picked up pace, with two more vessels hit in the last few days; and on the calendar, watch for Walmart's results already out, Best Buy on Monday, the Fed's favorite inflation gauge next Wednesday, Thanksgiving closing the markets Thursday, and a big OPEC-plus meeting a week from Sunday that'll set next year's oil output policy.

Now the lead, in full. Anthropic's shares opened at a hundred eleven fifty on the Nasdaq Thursday — about a third above the eighty-three dollar price bankers set the night before. From there it was a rollercoaster: up to a high around a hundred fifteen, then all the way back down to the low nineties, before closing the session up seventeen percent, at ninety-seven seventeen. More than eight hundred forty million shares changed hands. After the bell, the stock kept sliding, down almost five percent more.

Here's why that matters. Eighty-three dollars was already a rich price — above the range bankers had floated just a couple weeks earlier, and it implied something like a trillion and a half dollars in total value. What Thursday's trading shows is that even that rich price undershot what some buyers were willing to pay at the open — and then gave a big chunk of that pop right back within the same session. A close up seventeen percent on that much volume looks like classic first-day churn: early allocation holders cashing out into the pop, new buyers chasing it, and nobody quite agreeing yet on fair value. One of the Wall Street voices we flagged yesterday had framed a hundred dollars as a solid first day and a hundred forty as full confirmation the AI trade's back; the stock did clear a hundred for a few minutes before fading under it again.

The bigger picture: this isn't just about Anthropic's own cap table. Several of its chip suppliers and biggest customers — Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, AMD, Broadcom — already have billions of dollars riding on this stock through a private placement tied to the IPO. So a soft second day for Anthropic doesn't stay contained — it lands on a chip sector that's already nursing its own leadership headache, with Nvidia still waiting on a congressional hearing in less than two weeks. One trade outlet claimed a big short position was still being built into Thursday's rally — we can't verify that independently, so take it as a rumor, not a fact. What we can say: roughly eighty-five percent of the entire IPO float traded hands in a single session. That's a lot of hot money, not patient money.

Watch today whether the stock holds above that ninety-seven dollar close or slides back toward the original offer price — and whether this stays an Anthropic story or starts dragging its chip partners down with it. Worth remembering too: earlier this year, some private valuations for this company ran as high as two trillion dollars, before cooling off along with the rest of the AI trade. So yesterday's price, as wild as the swings were, is really the market's first real public vote on where that number should actually land.

On the economy: fed funds markets still think a December rate hike is the most likely outcome at next month's Fed meeting, though we couldn't pin down one clean current probability — estimates floating around run anywhere from the mid-eighties to high-nineties percent. And worth flagging: the big OPEC-plus meeting a week from Sunday on oil output for next year actually lands before the next big inflation report, so any oil-price move from that meeting won't show up in the data the Fed sees before it decides in December — just afterward.

Sunday's OPEC-plus meeting matters here too — whatever they decide on output still has to move through this same stretch of water.
