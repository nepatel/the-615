The 6:15, for Friday, August 28th. Here's what moved overnight: Marvell beat and raised guidance on every measure that matters, and the stock got sold off hard anyway, dragging Nasdaq futures lower right as the Fed's new chair gets ready for his first big speech.

Quick numbers before we dig in. Brent's bouncing around the high eighties with no clean settle, WTI's around eighty-three, the ten-year's at about four and two-thirds percent, the dollar index is just above ninety-nine, stock futures are mixed this morning — Nasdaq futures down around three-tenths of a percent on the chip selloff, S&P futures roughly flat, Dow futures up a touch — the VIX is down near fourteen and a half, gold's sitting just under forty-six hundred an ounce, the Nikkei and the Hang Seng both closed a bit higher overnight, gas has ticked down to around four-oh-nine a gallon nationally, the Strait of Hormuz is now at day one-eighty of its closure with a second unattributed tanker strike logged this week, and on the sports side, the Yankees lost to Houston five to one.

Okay, the lead. Marvell reported after the bell Thursday, and by the numbers it was a clean beat. Two point seven four billion dollars in revenue, a record, up thirty-seven percent from a year ago. Earnings beat too. And the guidance for next quarter was even better — three point one five billion dollars, which is above the number their own CEO had already flagged a full quarter ahead of schedule. By any normal read, that's exactly the kind of print that should send a stock higher. Instead it fell as much as eight percent in after-hours and premarket trading. Here's the mechanism: alongside all that good news, Marvell guided next quarter's profit margin down a bit, because their custom chip business — built to order for the big cloud companies — is growing faster than their higher-margin standard products, and custom chips just make less money per dollar of revenue right now. So investors heard "faster growth, thinner margins" and sold first, asked questions later. And Marvell wasn't alone. Rubrik, a smaller backup-software company, beat its own numbers by a wide margin and still dropped about nine percent overnight for basically the same reason — a stock that had run up hard into the print getting punished for anything less than perfect. A security company called SentinelOne did a variant of the same thing: beat on revenue, raised its full-year sales outlook, but trimmed its profit guidance, and got sold too.

Here's why it matters beyond just three stocks, though. Just two days ago, Nvidia blew out its own numbers and the stock ripped six percent higher, because nothing in that report complicated the growth story. Marvell's beat was arguably just as good on the top line, and it got punished, because the margin story did complicate things. Put those two nights side by side and you get a pretty clear signal: the market isn't buying every AI-related earnings beat anymore just because it's AI-related. It's starting to ask what kind of growth you're delivering — clean, high-margin growth like Nvidia's, or growth that comes bundled with thinner margins, like Marvell's custom-chip business. That's a more selective, more discriminating version of this trade than what we'd seen through most of August, and it's a genuinely different market backdrop than the one Nvidia's numbers created on Wednesday.

And the timing is awkward. All of this lands the morning of Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's very first Jackson Hole speech as chair, ten o'clock Eastern this morning. He's called it a blank piece of paper and has been cagey about previewing it, and a recent survey found nearly half of investors don't even expect him to say much about the rate path at all. He's walking into a market that's suddenly a lot choosier about what it rewards, which makes it a tougher room to read than the one he'd have faced on Wednesday afternoon.

On politics: Lisa Cook's lawyer sent the White House a formal letter this week laying out exactly why the administration's mortgage-fraud case against her doesn't hold up — the White House is basing its push to remove her on a housing-finance-agency referral alleging she misstated home addresses on mortgage paperwork and didn't report rental income. Her side says none of that rises to a fireable offense, and there's been no White House response yet. Worth remembering, this comes right as the new Fed chair is trying to establish his own credibility — a fresh fight over a Fed governor's seat is an awkward backdrop for that.

On the economy: jobless claims actually came in Thursday, and they were good — two-oh-three thousand for the week, down from the week before and better than expected, so the labor market's still holding up heading into next week's jobs report. We also got a confirmed date for the next inflation report — CPI lands September eleventh. But really, almost everything today is about the Warsh speech.

On business: three richly-valued tech and software names got hit for basically the same reason Thursday night — Marvell, Rubrik, and SentinelOne, all beat-and-raise, all sold off on the margin or profit side of the guide. One outlier going the other way: a company called IREN, which builds AI data-center capacity, actually missed on revenue but rallied anyway, because they've already got four billion dollars of next year's capacity locked in under contract, with most of it already sold. So the market's telling you: when the near-term number disappoints but the long-term backlog is solid, that's forgivable. When the near-term number is great but the margin trend isn't, apparently it isn't.

On technology: the same story that hit Marvell — hyperscalers pushing more of their AI chip spending toward custom-built silicon instead of off-the-shelf parts — is exactly the dynamic Broadcom has flagged too, so that's the thing to watch when Broadcom reports next. On the AI model front, there were a handful of release claims floating around this week, but nothing that checked out against an actual company announcement, so we're not reporting anything specific there today.

On geopolitics: Russia hit nine Ukrainian cities overnight in what its own defense ministry called a massive barrage — over two hundred and fifty drones plus cruise and ballistic missiles, most of it shot down, but people were killed in two regions and more hurt in Kyiv. Ukraine kept hitting back at Russian oil infrastructure, taking out a major refinery outside Moscow that's one of the country's biggest fuel sources.

And on Iran and the strait: Qatar's prime minister flew to Tehran this week — the first high-level Qatari visit since the war started back in February — to talk about reviving talks with Washington and a plan for a temporary shipping corridor through Hormuz with joint mine-clearance. No agreement came out of it. That trip came right after a second unattributed tanker strike this week, a vessel hit off the coast of Oman, fire put out, crew safe, nobody's claimed it. And separately, the Navy confirmed another aircraft carrier group is heading to the region for a deployment of more than seven months — which tells you the U.S. isn't expecting this to resolve itself anytime soon.

Quick sports wrap: Yankees lost to Houston five to one, they're seventy-four and fifty-eight, second in the AL East, still holding the top wild card spot. Knicks are in the off-season, Josh Hart's extension still unsigned, still trending toward that sixty-to-sixty-two-million-dollar range. Northwestern named its captains for the season this week and opens a week from tomorrow against South Dakota State.
