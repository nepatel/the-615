Here's the lead. Two of the biggest events of this entire AI boom are landing within about forty-eight hours of each other this week. Nvidia reports its fiscal third quarter tomorrow after the close — its first earnings since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on November twelfth. And Anthropic is separately expected to price its IPO as soon as Wednesday, with shares reported to start trading Thursday on the Nasdaq. What's complicating the Nvidia side of that story is a campaign, reported just yesterday, to bring Huang back.

The Financial Times reported Sunday that Huang is actually discussing a possible return as CEO with allies, who are putting together what one of them called a war room of Silicon Valley executives to lobby Nvidia's board — and they're planning to seek support from the White House too. A public petition to bring Jensen back has topped a hundred and sixty thousand signatures, with backers including Elon Musk, Epic Games' Tim Sweeney, and Y Combinator's Garry Tan, though those are self-reported and not verified by the petition itself. It's worth saying this isn't a unanimous push — Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had actually called for a dramatic shake-up of Nvidia's leadership right after Huang left, so the administration isn't all rowing in one direction here. And one Huang ally admitted to the Financial Times the obvious problem with any comeback, saying, quote, having an affair with a subordinate is a fireable offense — a reference to Huang's relationship with Kimberly Powell, a direct report, on top of the still-unresolved board investigation into his other relationship, with Alice Huang. Nvidia and Huang's people wouldn't comment. Arm's CEO, Rene Haas, is still the other name reportedly under consideration for a permanent job, with CFO Colette Kress running things on an interim basis for now.

The stakes for tomorrow's earnings call are enormous on their own. Nvidia fell nearly thirteen percent last week on the Huang news alone, dropping below four trillion dollars in market value. A clean beat-and-raise from Kress would test whether investors can keep treating this as a Nvidia-specific governance mess rather than a read on AI spending overall — which is how Wall Street treated it last week, when competitors AMD and Broadcom actually rallied. But a miss, or fresh reinstatement drama breaking during the call itself, would make that separation a lot harder to hold onto.

And here's how the two stories connect: Anthropic's bankers are pricing the AI sector's largest-ever IPO into the twenty-four hours right after Nvidia's print. The math in Anthropic's filing implies a value of up to one point four trillion dollars at the top of its price range, but reports citing Bloomberg say investor demand has actually pulled private talks down to something more like one point two to one point three trillion — down from one point four to one point five trillion just a few weeks ago, and down from around two trillion as recently as early October. So Nvidia's tone tomorrow, especially on AI spending guidance, is basically the last big data point underwriters get before they lock in Anthropic's price Wednesday.

Watch for whether the Huang reinstatement push gains real traction on the board before tomorrow's call, whether Nvidia's own guidance language touches the leadership transition directly, and where Anthropic actually prices relative to its range.

A few other things. In politics, Kansas's governor's race is still uncalled, with Democrat Cindy Holscher's lead holding steady around seventy-six hundred votes while county audits continue; there's also an uncalled attorney general's race in Kansas that Republican Chris Mann has claimed but that hasn't been settled either.

On the economy: October's producer-price report came in a little cooler than expected on the month, up three-tenths of a percent instead of the four-tenths forecast, but the year-over-year rate jumped to six and a half percent, the fastest pace since late twenty twenty-two — though part of that jump is a quirk from last year's reporting gap during the shutdown. Meanwhile, expectations for a December Fed rate cut have been fading, which has pushed the dollar to its highest level since last spring and pushed Treasury yields sharply higher across the board.

On technology, beyond the Nvidia story: Huang now has an actual date tied to his congressional situation — three House Republican committee chairs want a closed-door briefing from him tomorrow, the day after Nvidia's earnings, about his contacts with a Chinese businesswoman tied to an accused chip-smuggling operation. It's not clear as of this taping whether he's agreed to show up, and lawmakers have floated a subpoena if he doesn't. And on Anthropic's actual business, separate from the valuation fight: its own filing shows eleven point six billion dollars in revenue last quarter and its first quarterly operating profit, with investors expecting something like a hundred to a hundred twenty billion dollars in revenue for the full year.

On geopolitics: Russia and Ukraine held their first direct talks since February last week, two days of U.S.-mediated negotiations in Abu Dhabi led by Trump's envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. They covered security guarantees, prisoner exchanges and humanitarian issues, but there's no agreement and real differences remain — this was the first session in eight months, so expectations should stay modest.

And on Iran and the strait: Iran's deputy foreign minister says Tehran has handed Washington a two-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and that it's now up to the U.S. to act — a notable shift from just days earlier, when Iran's security chief flatly denied any talks were even happening. Either way, the strait stays closed for now. Iran's Revolutionary Guard has also claimed a string of mine strikes on tankers earlier this month, though those are unconfirmed claims from Iranian state media only, with no independent verification. Commercial tanker traffic through the strait remains a small fraction of its prewar level by every tracker's count, even though those trackers don't agree with each other on the exact numbers.

Sports, quickly: the Yankees are still waiting on Bednar's qualifying-offer decision, due tomorrow. The Knicks beat Toronto in overtime Sunday and are seven and six on the season, sixth in the East, after also losing to Indiana and to Cleveland last week. And Northwestern's season wrapped with a 28 to 19 loss at a ranked Ohio State team Saturday, finishing six and four.
