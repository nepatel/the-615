The 6:15, for Friday, September 4th. Here's what moved overnight: the Fed just split in public. Governor Waller broke with Chair Warsh's hawkish line, and September hike odds went from two-thirds to a coin flip in minutes.

Quick rundown of the board before we get into it. Brent's sitting in the mid-nineties, the ten-year eased back toward four and three quarters percent, the dollar slipped, the Dow had its best day in a month, closing up more than one percent, the VIX cooled back into the mid-teens, gas is right around four fifteen a gallon nationally, the Strait of Hormuz is on day one eighty seven of effective closure with just a handful of ships crossing a day, and on the sports side, the Yankees took two of three from the Angels, the Knicks are still finalizing Josh Hart's extension, and Northwestern opens its season tomorrow night against South Dakota State.

Now the lead. On Thursday, Fed Governor Christopher Waller broke publicly with Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish framing from Jackson Hole. In remarks prepared for a Reuters interview, Waller said that if disinflation continues in the data due over the next two weeks, he'd be inclined to support holding rates steady — he put it as, quote, give disinflation a chance, we can wait one meeting. The market reaction was immediate. The odds of a quarter-point hike at the September FOMC meeting collapsed from around two-thirds on Wednesday to basically a coin flip — one trading desk clocked the swing at twelve percentage points in a matter of minutes. That unwinds the move Warsh himself set off at Jackson Hole a week earlier, when he argued that softer inflation readings didn't yet show underlying trends had improved, and pushed hike odds up from around a third to two-thirds. This is the first time a sitting governor has publicly broken with Warsh's framing since this hiking debate started.

Yields and the dollar fell together on the news — the ten-year eased back to around four and three-quarters percent, off its highs earlier in the week — while stocks had their best session in a month: the Dow gained roughly six hundred points, its best day since early August; the S&P rose about one percent; the Nasdaq added close to a percent and a half. The VIX, which had spiked above sixteen on the Iran escalation Tuesday, opened back down around fifteen on Thursday.

Waller was specific about what would change his mind: a hot August inflation print, due September eleventh. That leaves today's August jobs report — out at eight thirty, after we go to press — as the more immediate but less decisive input. The consensus is looking for a gain of around fifty-three thousand jobs, with unemployment holding around four point one percent, against July's shocking drop of twenty-three thousand. A number near consensus doesn't really settle the Warsh-Waller fight on its own, since Waller already named the inflation print a week later as the one that matters most to him. But a repeat of July's miss would hand the doves fresh ammunition heading into the September meeting, and a strong beat hands it right back to Warsh. Either way, what looked like the market simply pricing in Warsh's preference two weeks ago is now a genuine two-sided fight inside the committee, and the next eight days bring two of the inputs that will decide it.

Two more things layered on top. Broadcom's results, out Wednesday after the close, beat cleanly — revenue up eighty-six percent year over year, AI chip revenue more than tripling — but the stock still fell almost three percent, because next quarter's guidance landed just a hair under what the Street wanted. After Nvidia and Marvell cleared a low bar late in August, Broadcom's reaction suggests the AI spending trade now needs guidance that beats, not just results that do. And separately, Wednesday into Thursday, Iran kept up its retaliation with missile and drone attacks it claimed against U.S.-linked targets in Jordan, Kuwait, the UAE, and Bahrain — all reported intercepted or without confirmed damage. Vice President Vance struck a notably downbeat tone, calling the six-week exchange essentially over and saying he wouldn't call it a war. Brent crude is still near six-week highs though, which keeps an oil-driven upside risk hanging over that same inflation print Waller just made decisive. A Fed that's now split in public, a jobs report still to come, a higher bar for AI earnings, and a war both sides are calling winding down while oil stays elevated — that's what's in play this morning.

On politics: House Speaker Johnson has canceled votes for the rest of September, leaving the House only a few working days before the midterms, so don't expect much legislative action before then. Reports say Trump told House Republicans that losing the midterms could mean impeachment — that's single-sourced, so treat it cautiously. The Massachusetts Senate primary and the funding bill both carry over unchanged from earlier this week.

On the economy: Thursday's jobless claims came in at two hundred six thousand, just a touch above expectations and up slightly from the prior week — still no real cracks at the weekly level even though the monthly jobs numbers have been rough. Today's jobs report is next, but remember, Waller already said it's the inflation print in a week that matters most to him.

On business: Amazon finally responded to that big FTC lawsuit over advertising prices, calling it a fundamental misunderstanding of how its ad auctions work. And Nvidia agreed to buy Hugging Face for almost thirteen billion dollars — a small check for Nvidia, but another sign it's trying to become more of an AI platform than just a chip supplier.

On technology: OpenAI rolled out a new model called GPT-6 Astra to business testers, which it says is the first model to cross its own threshold for serious cybersecurity risk — it aced an exploit benchmark and found real zero-day vulnerabilities on its own in testing. Wider access is coming in the next few days. Meanwhile Anthropic is still on track to release its IPO paperwork after Labor Day, aiming for a public listing late this month or in early October.

On geopolitics: Russia hit Ukraine for a seventh straight night, striking Odesa and injuring at least seventeen people. Ukraine struck back at targets including a support ship in the Russian port of Sochi. Attacks are increasingly nonstop rather than coming in nightly waves.

On Iran and the strait: beyond Tuesday's big strike and the first-ever direct hits on Iranian government tankers, Iran kept retaliating into Wednesday and Thursday against Jordan, Kuwait, the UAE, and Bahrain, with no confirmed hits on any U.S. facility. The Strait of Hormuz remains at day one eighty seven of effective closure, with only about four ships crossing as of Friday against a normal ten-day average in the teens, and more than eighty percent of what traffic there is now running dark. War-risk insurance is running three to ten times normal hull value per transit — these different trackers count different things, so we don't net them against each other.

Sports: the Yankees took two of three from the Angels in Anaheim, including a ten-inning win Thursday; Tampa Bay still leads the AL East, and the Yankees hold a wild card spot. The Knicks are still finalizing Josh Hart's contract extension. Northwestern opens its season tomorrow night at home against South Dakota State.
