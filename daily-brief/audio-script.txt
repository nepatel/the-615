The 6:15, for Thursday, October 29th. Here's what moved overnight: the Fed held rates steady yesterday, but with two officials wanting a hike instead of a cut, and Microsoft and Alphabet gave Wall Street a split verdict on AI spending — Microsoft up after hours, Alphabet down about ten percent — setting up a loaded Thursday with growth data, jobless claims, and Amazon's earnings still to come.

Quick scan of the board: today brings the first read on third-quarter growth and the weekly jobless claims report, both at eight-thirty, and Amazon reports after the close tonight. Saturday brings OPEC-plus and a Northwestern road trip to Oregon. The midterms are five days out. Brent's sitting around one fourteen a barrel this morning, WTI just over a hundred, both down a bit. The ten-year's just above five percent. Futures are soft, especially in the Nasdaq. Gold's slipped back under forty-one hundred. Gas prices ticked down again. And the World Series is over — the Brewers won it in five games, their first title ever.

Now the lead. Wednesday packed three big market events into about four hours, and the pattern matters more than any single number. The Fed held its rate steady for a third straight meeting, in the mid-threes to four percent range, by a ten-to-two vote — but the dissents were hawkish, not dovish: two officials wanted to raise rates, not cut them. The Fed chair said he doesn't believe in forward guidance, called the economy strong, and said the committee would consider rate changes at fewer meetings going forward — not exactly a dovish signal. Prediction markets now put the odds of a December rate hike meaningfully higher than they did a week ago.

Then came the split that mattered more for how people are positioned. Microsoft's revenue grew nineteen percent, beating expectations, and its cloud business, Azure, grew forty-eight percent, beating even Microsoft's own forecast — and it did that while spending less on capital investment than it had guided investors to expect. Alphabet told the opposite story: revenue grew twenty-five percent, also a beat, and its cloud business more than doubled — but its core search business slowed down, growing twelve percent instead of the seventeen percent it posted the quarter before, and its capital spending more than doubled. Microsoft rose around four or five percent after hours. Alphabet, despite beating on revenue, fell about ten percent.

The read here: investors split almost exactly along capital discipline, not the headline beat or miss. Microsoft spent less than promised and grew faster. Alphabet spent far more and grew slower in its core business. A revenue beat wasn't enough to save the stock whose spending and growth mix both moved the wrong way in the same quarter. Amazon Web Services has posted the fastest cloud growth of any of the big three most of this year, but Amazon has also been one of the most aggressive spenders on data centers and chips — so tonight is exactly the test: if its results look more like Alphabet's than Microsoft's, meaning capital spending jumps while a core business slows, expect a similarly rough reaction after the bell.

The second-order point is this: a Fed that's now leaning toward a December hike, not a cut, pulls away the cheap-money cushion that's supported a year of enormous AI spending by these companies. That assumption just got a lot less safe, right as today's growth and jobs data land, and as Amazon's results test whether Wednesday's verdict was a one-off or sets the bar for the whole group. Watch for growth and claims data at eight-thirty, how markets react today to Wednesday's dueling earnings, and whether Amazon's report tonight breaks the same way Alphabet's did.

On politics: with the midterms six days out at the time this was written, the president's approval rating sits at a record low for either of his terms, in the low thirties, with roughly two-thirds disapproving. Forecasters mostly expect Democrats to take the House; the Senate is closer to a coin flip. A handful of swing states keep coming up as the ones to watch.

On the economy, beyond the Fed: today's growth and jobless-claims numbers hadn't printed as of this writing. Economists expect growth to come in a little above three percent annualized, up from a weaker read last quarter. Last week's jobless claims hit a three-month low, a sign the labor market's still holding up.

On business: Microsoft's future contracted revenue jumped seventy-three percent, a strong signal for its cloud backlog. It's also stretching out how long it depreciates its data centers, which mechanically flatters its margins going forward — worth keeping in mind when comparing quarters. Amazon reports tonight after the close; there's no reliable consensus estimate to cite heading in. Gas prices ticked down again for a second straight reading.

On technology: Alphabet's cloud growth leaned partly on its own AI chips, a lower-margin, more capital-heavy business than search advertising — and search, still its profit engine, is the piece that slowed. Microsoft's restraint on spending while still beating its own cloud growth forecast is one data point in its favor, but it's one company's results, not a verdict on the whole sector yet.

On Iran and the strait: the most-watched tanker-tracking number swung hard overnight — the share of tankers running without transponders dropped sharply from where it sat earlier this week, down to around a quarter of screened ships from over seventy percent on Monday and Tuesday. Take that with a grain of salt — actual ship traffic through the strait is still next to nothing, under five vessels a day against a baseline of eighty-five, so a falling percentage might just mean there's less total traffic to screen, not that more ships are suddenly behaving normally. The composite risk indices, meanwhile, barely moved — still reading extreme, flat for a second day. Overnight, a British maritime monitor reported a vessel fire off the coast of Oman — that one came from two separate outlets citing the same monitor, so it's a bit more solid than the usual claims. Separately, Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed three tankers hit mines and that it captured a U.S. drone — both claims come only from Iranian state media and aren't confirmed by anyone else, continuing a month-long pattern of unconfirmed claims from that side. One more claim, if true, would be notable: a report that Iran says it's in contact with intermediaries about a plan to reopen the strait. That's a single, unconfirmed source, so treat it as a maybe, not a development.

Sports, quickly: the World Series is over — the Brewers beat the Rays in Game Five to win their first championship in the franchise's fifty-seven-year history. The Yankees' season ended back on October 7th. The Knicks are still 0 and 3, with no game since Sunday; they're back in action Friday at Philadelphia. Northwestern is 6 and 1 and heads to Oregon Saturday for the first meeting ever between the two schools.

That's The 6:15. Trains are running.
