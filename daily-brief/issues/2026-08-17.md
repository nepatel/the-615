## Lead: Trump vows to make the Strait of Hormuz "a territory of the United States"; weekend transits hit zero for the first time this war

President Trump said Friday he intends to declare the Strait of Hormuz "a territory of the United States" once Iran is "defeated," telling reporters it would happen "pretty soon" — an escalation from the "100% Navy control" language he used two weeks ago to something closer to outright annexation. [NEW] Iran's response was uniform across factions that otherwise agree on little: Deputy FM Kazem Gharibabadi said the strait "cannot be seized by tweet, nor by aircraft carrier, nor by issuing an order, nor by an election speech" and "has been Iranian, is Iranian, and will remain Iranian"; the judiciary chief called the claim the "delusions of a feeble-minded individual"; Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf said separately Sunday that Iran "truly won this war both militarily and politically." [fact: statements dated 8/14-16; read: the uniformity of the rebuttal, across power centers that don't usually agree, suggests Trump handed Tehran a unifying line at a moment its own economy is visibly struggling — Pezeshkian said this weekend, "we used to sell oil, but now we cannot sell it."]

The rhetoric landed alongside the worst run of actual shipping attacks since the ceasefire MoU collapsed in July. Three separate ADNOC-affiliated tankers were struck Thursday and Friday, and a bulk carrier was hit by an unidentified projectile near Kumzar, Oman, over the weekend; UAE's Foreign Ministry called the ADNOC strikes a "flagrant violation" of international law. [NEW] The concentration of hits on a single state oil company's fleet, rather than the more diffuse targeting of prior months, reads as a deliberate signal aimed at the UAE specifically — a U.S.-aligned Gulf state that has stayed rhetorically firm but hadn't previously taken the brunt of Iranian retaliation. [read]

The transit numbers confirm the deterioration. PortWatch's satellite tracking, which lags roughly a week, showed a single vessel transiting on Aug. 9 against a ~73/day pre-war baseline; Lloyd's List's fresher weekly count (through Aug. 9) showed total transits down 18% week-on-week to 78, with non-Iranian-linked traffic down 29%. Then came the clearest number yet: 5 vessels transited Saturday and zero Sunday, against 31 the weekend before. [NEW] Zero is a reading this war hadn't produced before, even at its worst prior stretches.

Oil is pricing the deterioration, not the rhetoric. Brent settled Friday at $88.52 (+1.7%) and is bid to $89.46 (+1.06%) in early trading — up roughly $5 from two Mondays ago and pushing back toward the year's highs, though still short of them. WTI is harder to read cleanly this morning: the front-month contract rolled from September to October on Friday, and the two aggregators we checked disagree by nearly a dollar on the resulting level — treat the exact print as unsettled until the roll clears. [flag: WTI level unreconciled]

Equities are mostly shrugging it off on the surface: S&P futures are bid toward 7,815.75 this morning, building on a week that produced back-to-back record closes (7,748.50 Wed 8/12, then 7,798.99 Thu 8/13) even as retail sales and consumer sentiment both missed hard the same week. [read: a familiar 2026 pattern — the market treats growth-scare data softness as Fed-friendly rather than economy-worrying, so long as it doesn't show up in earnings] Energy is the one place the strait crisis is visibly showing up in index-level pricing.

The Fed angle is where this converges with last week's data. July CPI already showed energy up 14.7% YoY even as headline and core landed in line with consensus; a sustained push back toward $90 Brent sets up a bigger energy contribution in the August print, due mid-September. That arrives into a Fed conversation that had, until Thursday, been moving the other way — September hike odds on CME FedWatch roughly halved, from ~60% on Aug. 11 to an estimated 31-33% by Aug. 14, as a weak July payrolls number was reinforced by a surprise 0.6% drop in July retail sales and a UMich sentiment reading that cratered to 51.0 from 55.2. Whether a renewed oil shock outweighs two soft activity prints is the live question heading into Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote on Aug. 28 — itself now shadowed by the Aug. 26 deadline Trump has set Fed Governor Lisa Cook to respond to a renewed termination effort. ## Politics

[DEVELOPING] Separately, the White House reopened its effort to remove Fed Governor Lisa Cook: a letter dated Aug. 5 from Deputy Chief of Staff Dan Scavino cites the same mortgage-fraud allegations behind the first, Supreme-Court-blocked attempt, and gives Cook until Aug. 26 to respond before Trump says he'll make a "final decision." [NEW] That deadline lands one day before Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as chair — a Fed-independence storyline now running directly alongside the rate-path debate.
