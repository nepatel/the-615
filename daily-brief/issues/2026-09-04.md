## Lead: Waller breaks with Warsh, cutting September hike odds to a coin flip; markets rally on the split as today's jobs report becomes the next tiebreaker before Waller's own decisive data point

Thursday, Fed Governor Christopher Waller broke publicly with Chair Kevin Warsh's post-Jackson-Hole hawkish framing. In remarks prepared for a Reuters interview, Waller said that if disinflation continues in the data due over the next two weeks, "I would be inclined to support holding the target for the federal funds rate at its current setting" — paraphrasing John Lennon: "give disinflation a chance. We can wait one meeting." [NEW] [fact] The reaction was immediate: CME FedWatch's implied probability of a 25bp hike at the Sept. 15-16 meeting collapsed from roughly 65.9% Wednesday to close to a coin flip — a swing one markets desk clocked at 12 percentage points "in minutes." [fact] That directly unwinds the trajectory set by Warsh's Aug. 28 Jackson Hole speech, which argued recent softer inflation readings "do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved" and had pushed hike odds from roughly 35% to 66.1% over the following days — the first public daylight between a Warsh-led FOMC majority and a sitting governor since the current hiking debate began.

Treasury yields and the dollar fell in tandem on the news — the 10-year eased to roughly 4.74-4.75% Thursday from as high as 4.79-4.81% earlier in the week, and DXY slid to the 98.99-99.37 area (sources disagree on the exact print) — while equities staged their best session in a month: the Dow gained roughly 600 points (+1.18%) to 53,686.11, its best day since Aug. 4; the S&P 500 rose 1.06% to 7,747.71; the Nasdaq Composite added 1.40% to 26,584.06. [fact] The VIX, which had closed above 16 Tuesday on the Iran escalation, opened Thursday at 15.25, in a 14.23-15.44 range. [fact]

Waller was explicit about what would change his mind: a hot August CPI print, due Sept. 11, that shows inflation trends aren't cooperating. [fact] That leaves today's August jobs report — due 8:30a ET, after this issue's press time — as the more immediate but less decisive input: a Dow Jones consensus calls for roughly +53,000 payrolls (other desks range as high as +90,000) against July's shock -23,000 miss, with the unemployment rate seen holding at 4.1%. [fact, flag: consensus estimates vary meaningfully by source] [read: A print near consensus does little to resolve the Warsh-Waller split on its own — Waller himself pointed to the CPI print a week later as decisive — but a repeat of July's miss would hand the dovish faction fresh ammunition heading into the Sept. 15-16 meeting, while a strong beat hands it back to Warsh. Either way, what was a market pricing in Warsh's preference two weeks ago is now a genuine two-sided committee debate, and the next eight days carry two of the inputs that will settle it.]

Two more threads sit alongside this. Broadcom's fiscal Q3 print, released after Wednesday's close, beat cleanly — revenue $29.6B (+86% Y/Y) against $29.43B consensus, non-GAAP EPS $3.32 (+96% Y/Y), AI-semiconductor revenue $16.7B (+221% Y/Y, +54% Q/Q) — yet the stock fell 2.75% Thursday to $357.16, with Q4 revenue guidance of $34.8B landing just under the Street's $35.03B. [fact] After Nvidia's and Marvell's late-August prints cleared a low bar, Broadcom's guide-just-shy reaction reads as a sign the AI-capex trade now needs guidance that beats, not merely results that do. [read] Separately, Wednesday-into-Thursday's Iran escalation — missile and drone attacks Iran claimed against U.S.-linked targets in Jordan, Kuwait, the UAE and Bahrain, each reported intercepted or without confirmed damage — drew a notably downbeat framing from Vice President Vance, who called the six-week exchange effectively over and said "I wouldn't call it a war." [fact] Brent nonetheless remains near six-week highs (see Iran & Hormuz), keeping an oil-driven upside risk to the very CPI print Waller just made decisive. [read] A Fed newly split in public, a jobs report still outstanding, an AI-earnings bar that just moved higher, and a war both sides are describing as winding down while oil stays elevated — that is the set of crosscurrents this issue's reader carries onto the subway this morning.
