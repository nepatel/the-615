## Lead: Anthropic prices the largest IPO on record at $83 a share — a $1.5 trillion valuation — with ANT set to open on Nasdaq this morning

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 a share late Wednesday, above the $70–78 range set in its second amended S-1, implying a valuation of roughly $1.5 trillion including employee stock options and restricted shares and raising about $79.4 billion for the company — potentially $89.6 billion if underwriters exercise their option — according to AFP's reporting of the pricing, corroborated by CBS News's own review of the filing. [fact] Anthropic is selling 956,351,354 of the roughly 980 million Class A shares on offer; the remainder is being sold by existing stockholders, from whose sale Anthropic receives no proceeds. [fact] Shares are set to begin trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT at the 9:30 a.m. ET open today — after this issue's press time. [fact]

Mechanism: the raise tops the $75 billion SpaceX sold in its June placement (which valued SpaceX at roughly $1.77 trillion and, after its own underwriters' option, ultimately brought in $86 billion), making Anthropic's primary sale the largest on record even before any overallotment. [fact] It lands alongside a separate $26 billion private placement already committed by Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, AMD and Broadcom — the same hyperscalers that are both Anthropic's largest customers and, for Nvidia and Google specifically, its chip suppliers — a structure that ties the AI-infrastructure trade's health directly to Anthropic's public share price for the first time. [fact, per S-1 as reported] Anthropic posted nearly $17 billion in third-quarter revenue, up 45% from the second quarter, but is not yet profitable and carries $6.2 billion in debt — "substantial," in its own filing's words — against almost $1 trillion in disclosed chip and infrastructure commitments through 2045. [fact]

Second-order consequence: the pricing lands nine days after Jensen Huang's resignation threw Nvidia's own earnings call into disarray (see issue No. 40) and two days after Nvidia's actual Q3 beat (see Business) — so the AI trade gets a fresh, highly public price-discovery event at the application layer in the same week its infrastructure layer absorbed a leadership vacuum. Renaissance Capital's Matthew Kennedy called the deal "so central to the AI thesis" that it would mark a turning point for the sector, framing a first-day pop to $100 as "solid" and a move toward $140 as confirmation AI enthusiasm has fully returned; University of Florida IPO researcher Jay Ritter countered that the market is "pricing in very lofty expectations" that "could be hard for management to execute," though he expects the company to reach profitability quickly. [read, both attributed] Growth, at least, is not in dispute: Anthropic's roughly $17 billion third-quarter revenue compares with about $1.4 billion a year earlier, a more than tenfold increase that underpins the bull case even as critics question durability once hyperscaler-funded compute credits roll off. [fact] Dario and Daniela Amodei founded the company in 2021 after leaving OpenAI, and the IPO sets up what several outlets are already framing as a direct public-market proxy war between the two labs, which do not otherwise disclose comparable financials. [fact/read]

That split matters because Anthropic valuation estimates ran as high as $2 trillion earlier this year before compressing amid the AI-stock pullback that also hit Nvidia and helped push the Nasdaq to a second straight weekly loss as of issue No. 40 — so the $1.5 trillion print is itself a data point on where the market has reset AI-growth expectations, not just a number about Anthropic. [read] Sen. Elizabeth Warren criticized the offering on social media as failing "to answer for the potential threats to Americans' retirements," a reminder that Anthropic's own prospectus warns its systems "could pose catastrophic or existential risks to society if not developed and deployed responsibly" — language now attached to a security that index funds will shortly hold by default once ANT clears relevant benchmark float thresholds. [fact/read]

*Watch:* where ANT trades relative to the $83 offer at today's open and close; whether the move reads as Anthropic-specific or spills into Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom, which are financing part of the deal; and whether this is the AI sector's last major private-to-public transition of the year or the first of several still queued behind it. The underwriting syndicate was not named in any source this brief checked; this issue does not guess at it.
