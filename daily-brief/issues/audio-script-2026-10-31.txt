The 6:15, for Saturday, October 31st. Here's what moved overnight: Intel had its worst trading day since 1974, down nearly thirty percent, after a brutal guidance cut overshadowed an otherwise solid quarter — and it dragged Amazon and Reddit down with it, giving the Nasdaq its worst month since last March.

Quick scan of the board: Northwestern travels to Oregon this afternoon for the first-ever meeting between the two programs, kickoff three-thirty Eastern on Peacock, with Oregon favored by about a field goal and a half. OPEC-plus meets today too, though next month's output is already locked in flat from their early-October decision. The midterms are three days away. Friday's close was ugly across the board — the S&P down close to two percent, the Nasdaq down nearly three, the Dow off about one percent, and the fear gauge up nearly thirteen percent. Oil actually rallied as stocks fell — Brent back above one thirteen, WTI just over one-oh-four. The ten-year yield eased a touch, just under four point nine three percent. Gold dropped more than three percent. Gas prices kept falling, now under four dollars twenty cents nationally and still sliding. And a quick note on the Knicks and Yankees in a moment.

Now the lead. Thursday's economic data was actually good news, and it's worth a beat before we get to Friday's carnage. The government's first look at third-quarter growth came in at three point four percent annualized — above expectations and faster than the prior quarter's two point two percent. Inflation inside that same report cooled to around two percent. And weekly jobless claims fell to a three-month low, a hair under one hundred ninety-four thousand, well below what economists expected, with the four-week average easing too. On their own, those are the kind of numbers that would normally carry a news day — a strong-growth, cooling-inflation, resilient-labor-market combination is about as good as this economy has looked all year. They didn't carry Friday, because Friday ran right over them.

Intel reported Thursday night, and the quarter itself was fine — revenue and profit both beat Wall Street's estimates, with the data-center and AI side of the business up seventy-eight percent. The stock didn't care about that. It cared about the guidance: next quarter's revenue is expected to come in below what Intel just reported, and the profit margin guidance collapsed — from about forty-four percent this quarter to thirty-eight percent next, with management blaming a memory-chip shortage that's pushing PC prices up and demand down. Intel shares fell nearly twenty-nine percent Friday, closing around sixty-six dollars — its worst single day since nineteen seventy-four.

Amazon piled on. It reported Thursday night too. Amazon Web Services grew about thirty-nine percent, its fastest pace in nearly five years, and the CEO said he expects AWS growth to stay above forty percent for the next two years. That's a genuinely strong number, arguably the best cloud print of the week from any of the big three. But profit per share came in a bit light against estimates clustered near two dollars, overall revenue landed just under what analysts expected, and the company burned more than forty-two billion dollars in free cash flow over the trailing twelve months against roughly seventy-seven billion dollars in capital spending in this quarter alone. The stock fell after hours — reports differ on exactly how much, somewhere between six and eleven percent depending on the source and when you check, so take any single figure with a grain of salt. Reddit added a third leg to the selloff, falling somewhere around a quarter of its value, estimates range from the mid-twenties to near thirty percent, on weak daily-user numbers, soft guidance, and plans to require logins just to browse the site.

Here's the read: this is the same story that's been building since Wednesday, just spreading to more companies. Microsoft spent less than promised and grew faster; Alphabet spent far more and its core business slowed — that was last week's split. Intel's crash wasn't really about AI demand; data-center revenue was up nearly eighty percent. It was about what that demand now costs to deliver, in margin terms — the same mechanism that hit Alphabet. And Amazon's cloud growth was arguably the best number of the week, and the market sold the stock anyway, because seventy-seven billion dollars of quarterly spending and a ninth straight quarter of negative free cash flow reads, at this point, like the same worry with a new face, not a new problem. Reddit's slide is a separate, company-specific story — user numbers and a login wall, not capital spending — but it landed on the same tape and made the day look even worse than it already was. For the month, that combination left the Nasdaq down more than five percent in October, its worst month since last March, even though the Dow actually finished the month higher. Watch Monday's open to see whether this extends or reverses, and watch Tuesday's election results, sitting just downstream of a Fed that held rates this week but, with two officials pushing for a hike instead of a cut, is now leaning toward raising in December rather than easing.

A word on the midterms, three days out now: the most recent Reuters-Ipsos read had the president's approval at thirty-one to thirty-two percent, a record low for either of his terms, though that newer number comes from a single source this brief hasn't independently confirmed. Forecasters still mostly favor Democrats taking the House; the Senate is closer to a true toss-up depending which model or betting market you trust. Nothing new moved on that front overnight — it's background for Tuesday, not news in itself.

On Iran and the strait: not much changed overnight. The tanker-tracking service that watches transponder activity showed a little over half of screened tankers going dark in the past day, but it also flagged that non-tanker vessels went dark at almost the same rate — which argues that's background signal loss, not a change in evasive behavior, not a sign of anything new. Actual strait traffic is still frozen at two vessels nearly a week ago, against a normal baseline of eighty-five a day — that reading hasn't updated in six days now, the longest gap we've seen. The composite risk indices barely moved, still reading extreme. No new claims from Iran's side, and no response yet to the reopening offer Iran's foreign minister floated weeks ago.

Sports, quickly: the Yankees' season has been over since October seventh, swept by Tampa Bay back in the division series. The Knicks lost Friday night at Philadelphia, ninety-eight to ninety-three — Tyrese Maxey and LeBron James led the Sixers, Jalen Brunson led New York with twenty-four points and Karl-Anthony Towns added a double-double. We're not going to guess at their record on air — the numbers we're seeing don't agree with each other closely enough to trust, and that's not a risk worth taking just to fill a line. And Northwestern heads to Oregon this afternoon for that first-ever matchup between the two programs, kickoff three-thirty Eastern — results after we go to air.
