## Lead

<div class="kicker">IRAN &amp; HORMUZ</div>
<h3 class="lede">First direct US-Iran exchange in a month reopens the oil-shock question Friday's Fed speech never had to answer</h3>

<p>US Central Command said Sunday its forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island, inside the Strait of Hormuz, after observing IRGC personnel "preparing to launch rockets with sea mines into the Strait" — in the words of CENTCOM spokesman Capt. Tim Hawkins — marking the first publicly acknowledged direct US strike on Iranian positions in more than a month. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said the strike killed and wounded "several" fighters and civilians, without giving a toll, and vowed to "punish those responsible"; CENTCOM separately rejected the IRGC's framing of the episode as an "act of aggression," calling its own action "limited, precise" and aimed at an imminent minelaying threat to commercial shipping. Iran responded within hours, firing ballistic and anti-ship missiles from Tehran, Lorestan, Karaj, Khorramabad and Shiraz, plus drones, at two US installations in Jordan — King Hussein Air Base and Al-Azraq Air Base, according to Iranian state media. A US official said nearly all incoming projectiles were intercepted, with no significant impact reported as of press time. [NEW]</p>

<p>Oil moved first and hardest. Brent's front-month contract, trading in the high $80s through Friday, jumped to roughly $90.5-90.7/bbl in early Monday trade (+2.7-2.9%; trackers disagree on the exact print and there is no clean settle this early), while WTI rose into the mid-$80s (+~2.5%). Equity futures reversed the "quiet 24 hours" framing this issue had been tracking as recently as Saturday: S&P 500 futures fell as much as 0.27% and Nasdaq 100 futures 0.23% in the immediate aftermath, though both had pared losses to roughly flat-to-down-0.1% by the New York premarket as the intercepted-with-no-impact read settled in. VIX futures, at 17.3, sit well above Friday's 14.43 spot close — a volatility bid spot hadn't caught up to as of writing. Asia closed mixed (Nikkei -0.14%, Hang Seng flat), and Europe's Stoxx 600 traded just below flat with energy shares outperforming on the oil move.</p>

<p>The timing compounds Friday's other story. Fed Chair Warsh used his Jackson Hole keynote to argue inflation, not employment, is the Fed's "predominant" concern, and traders responded by pushing September hike odds to 57-59% from 35% Thursday — a hawkish repricing built on an assumption of resilient growth and domestically driven price pressure. [continuity from Saturday's issue] An oil shock complicates that framework from both directions at once: higher energy costs feed straight into the same inflation prints Warsh flagged as the binding constraint, while a serious Hormuz disruption is also a demand shock to a global economy the Fed is simultaneously trying to cool. [read: this is the first live test of whether Warsh's Fed treats an oil-driven inflation impulse the same as a demand-driven one — a hawkish chair who hikes into a geopolitical supply shock risks being read as fighting the wrong problem, while standing pat looks like exactly the discretionary, data-dependent approach he spent Friday defending.] Nothing so far suggests a repeat of February's initial closure at scale — the exchange looks contained to a single strike-and-retaliate cycle with no reported US casualties — but markets spent five months pricing a slow bleed in Hormuz throughput, not a fresh military exchange, and Monday's session is still working out which regime it's in.</p>

<p>The exchange also ends the lull flagged Saturday, which had already been complicated by a separate UK Maritime Trade Operations warning: a crude tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile roughly 12 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, late Saturday evening — an incident distinct from the Larak Island exchange and still unattributed. [NEW] Whether Tehran treats Sunday's exchange as closed or opens a new response window is what the oil market will spend Monday pricing; CENTCOM's statement gave no indication of further planned US action, and no third-country diplomatic channel was reported active as of this writing.</p>
