## Lead: Wall Street's AI trade unwinds into a correction as a Nvidia SEC-probe report and Anthropic's own stock stumble puncture the week's rally

The S&P 500 closed Friday at 6,910.39, down 265.44 points or 3.70%, its seventh straight loss and enough to put the index more than 10% below its October record close — a technical correction by the 4 p.m. ET settlement data. [fact] Trackers disagree on the exact drawdown: one puts the Oct. 6 record close at 7,818.93 (an 11.6% fall from there), another at 7,790.9 (11.5%); this brief reports both rather than averaging them. [fact; flag, conflicting trackers] The Dow fell 949.77 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33; the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1,261.47 points, or 5.28%, to 22,617.73, erasing its gain for 2026. [fact] The Cboe Volatility Index jumped 26.2% to 35.89. [fact]

Chip stocks led the rout. Nvidia fell 14.01% to $125.53 after Reuters, citing the Wall Street Journal, reported the SEC has opened an inquiry into Nvidia's disclosures following Jensen Huang's Nov. 12 resignation as CEO, including whether the company misled investors; the scope was not immediately clear, Nvidia has not commented, and an SEC spokesperson said the agency does not confirm or deny investigations. [fact] California's attorney general separately said his office is weighing "whether the law and facts justify" action, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said this week he wants "answers in the next 10 days" from Nvidia's board and outside counsel — a window that runs out around Nov. 27–28. [fact] Broadcom fell 12.26% on an unconfirmed report that Google would cut next year's chip orders; AMD and Micron each fell more than 13%. [fact; flag, Broadcom driver single-sourced/unconfirmed]

The selloff also swallowed the stock this brief's Lead covered two days ago. Anthropic's ANT, which closed its Thursday Nasdaq debut at $97.17 — up 17% from its $83 IPO price after opening at $111.50 — fell a further 8.50% Friday to $88.91: still 7.1% above the offer price, but 20.3% below Thursday's opening trade. [fact; DEVELOPING] Renaissance Capital's Matthew Kennedy had framed a push toward $140 as confirmation AI enthusiasm had "fully returned" (issue No. 41); two sessions in, ANT is instead trading down with the sector it was supposed to validate — a read on sentiment, not a verdict on the IPO itself. [read]

The drop came even as rate expectations stayed hawkish, an unusual combination. Fed Governor Lisa Cook said this week that "waiting longer to implement rate increases risks getting behind the curve," having preferred a hike at the Fed's late-October meeting. [fact] BNP Paribas' Bénédicte Lowe characterized the session as "panic selling" as investors judge whether AI enthusiasm has peaked; Deutsche Bank's Binky Chadha called the market "very oversold" and said underlying growth and earnings remain strong — two framings of the same move this brief does not adjudicate between. [read, both attributed]

*Watch:* whether Monday's open extends the losing streak to eight sessions or stabilizes; whether the SEC confirms a formal Nvidia investigation before Bessent's 10-day window closes; and whether ANT finds a floor above its $83 offer price once the quiet period lifts.
