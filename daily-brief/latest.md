## Lead: GDP revised sharply higher to 4.3%, PCE inflation accelerates to 3.8% — both land the same morning, both cut the same way, five business days before a Fed meeting where a hike is already priced as near-certain

The Bureau of Economic Analysis's second estimate, released Wednesday at 8:30 a.m. ET, put Q3 real GDP growth at 4.3% annualized — a 0.9-percentage-point upward revision from the 3.4% advance estimate, and up from Q2's 2.2%. [fact, BEA] BEA attributed the revision mainly to stronger investment, concentrated in intellectual property products — notably software — plus stronger goods consumption, partly offset by a downward revision to services spending (mainly health care). Real final sales to private domestic purchasers, a cleaner read on underlying private demand, rose 5.8%, also revised up 0.9 point; real gross domestic income rose 4.4%; current-dollar GDP rose 6.2%. Corporate profits from current production rose $206.0 billion in the quarter, down sharply from Q2's $384.0 billion gain. [fact, BEA]

The same morning, BEA's separate Personal Income and Outlays release showed the headline PCE price index up 3.8% year-over-year in October, accelerating from September's 3.6%; core PCE (excluding food and energy) rose 3.3%, up from 3.1%. [fact, BEA] That is a distinct measure from the GDP price index reported in the growth release itself, which BEA said was unchanged at 2.1% headline and 2.5% core on a quarterly-annualized basis — the two inflation readings move differently and shouldn't be conflated; this issue is citing both because an investor reading one number off a headline could easily get the wrong one. [fact, BEA]

Mechanism: stronger growth and firmer inflation arriving in the same data cluster is the opposite of what would give the Fed room to pause. Issue No. 42 already reported that fed-funds futures had priced a December quarter-point hike at better than 90% odds as of a Nov. 17 snapshot, up from roughly two-thirds odds on Nov. 5, with Fed officials' late-October remarks and minutes read as consistent with a hike rather than a cut. [fact, unchanged; this brief did not obtain a fresher futures read this run] Nothing in Wednesday's data works against that pricing — a GDP revision this large, paired with accelerating core inflation, is the kind of print that forecloses a dovish surprise rather than inviting one, five business days ahead of the Dec. 8-9 FOMC meeting.

Second-order consequence: the growth beat is not obviously broad-based. BEA's own attribution — software/IP investment and goods spending leading the revision, services spending revised down — is consistent with AI-capex-driven investment doing more of the lifting than organic household demand, a distinction worth separating from the headline number itself; this is a read, not something BEA's release states directly.
