## Lead: Amazon closes the hyperscaler earnings arc with a miss on guidance, not growth — and the market keeps punishing capex uncertainty over beats

Thursday night closed the three-day run of mega-cap tech earnings this brief has tracked since Wednesday, and Amazon's print extended the same divide rather than resolving it. The setup already looked fragile: Thursday's regular session closed lower across the board — Dow -0.25%, S&P 500 -0.30%, Nasdaq Composite -1.38% — before Amazon's numbers landed after the bell. [fact, NEW] AWS revenue rose 39% year-over-year to $45.98 billion [fact, NEW] — the unit's fastest growth in roughly five years and above the Street's ~37.5% estimate, putting its annualized exit run-rate near $184 billion — with CEO Andy Jassy guiding AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028 on the call. [fact, NEW] But total revenue of $201.94 billion missed the $202.22 billion consensus narrowly, and EPS of $1.89 missed the $1.97 estimate by roughly 4%. [fact, NEW] Q4 revenue guidance of $240 billion-$247 billion came in, by multiple trackers' reckoning, largely below estimates. [fact, NEW] The stock, which had already closed Thursday's regular session down 4.25% at $243.79 ahead of the print, fell further to $226.65 in Friday premarket trading as of 6:19a ET — down roughly 7% from Thursday's close, though other after-hours snapshots taken at different reference times showed moves ranging from about -6% to -9%, a spread this brief reports rather than compresses into one number. [fact/flag, NEW]

Read: the reaction completes a pattern flagged in Wednesday's issue. Microsoft beat on revenue and EPS, spent less capex than its own guide, and rose after hours. Alphabet beat on revenue, more than doubled its capex, watched Search deceleration swallow the headline, and fell about 10.5%. Amazon now sits in between — its core growth engine, AWS, accelerated and beat, which is the Microsoft pattern, but a soft Q4 guide produced something closer to the Alphabet reaction. [read] The common thread across three different companies' prints this week: the market is no longer rewarding revenue beats on their own; it is pricing each hyperscaler on whether its forward guidance credibly reconciles growth with the scale of AI capital spending already committed. [read]

That reaction landed hours after a macro backdrop that, on its own, argued for calm. The BEA's Q3 advance GDP estimate came in at +3.4% annualized [fact, NEW] — above the ~3.2% consensus and up sharply from Q2's final 2.2%, though below the Atlanta Fed's ~3.6% GDPNow nowcast. Weekly jobless claims for the week ended Oct. 24 fell to 194,000, below the ~199,000 Reuters poll consensus, with Reuters describing the level as near a 57-year low. [fact, NEW] Neither print moved futures positively Friday morning: S&P 500 futures were down 1.04% and Nasdaq-100 futures down 2.32% as of 6:09a ET [fact, NEW] — a decline this brief reads as Amazon- and tech-earnings-driven, not a reassessment of the broader economy, since the macro data itself came in firm. [read]

Second-order consequence: with Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon all now reported, the quarter's verdict is a sector-wide one rather than a single-stock data point — capital discipline gets rewarded, capital-spending uncertainty gets punished, regardless of whether the underlying growth engine is actually accelerating, as AWS's plainly is. [read] That verdict lands as this brief could not independently reconcile secondary sources' estimates of CME's implied odds of a Dec. 9 rate hike closely enough to print a number this run [flag] — sharpening, rather than resolving, the question of whether a firm GDP print and a resilient labor market give the Fed room to hold its hawkish line even as the AI-capex trade wobbles.
