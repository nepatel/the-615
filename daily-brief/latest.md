## Lead: A $2 trillion-plus AI-driven rout wipes out the post-election rally, then partly reverses on a jobs report that blew past every forecast

Wednesday's relief rally didn't survive its first full trading day. Thursday, Nov. 5, delivered the worst US equity session since April 2025's tariff shock: the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52% into correction territory, the S&P 500 dropped roughly 4.4%, and the Dow lost 3.44%, or about 1,833 points. Per CNBC's reporting, the trigger wasn't the AI-regulation risk this brief flagged after the Democratic sweep in issue No. 36 — it was a narrower, more mechanical worry that AI data-center buildout itself could slow coming out of the midterm result. Information technology was the S&P's worst-hit sector, down 7.30%, its steepest drop since March 16, 2020; the Technology Select Sector SPDR (XLK) fell 8.04%. The VIX jumped 11.57 points intraday to 28.09, its highest level since March 31 and its biggest one-day point gain since April 4, 2025. [fact, NEW]

The damage concentrated at the index's top. The Magnificent Seven lost more than $1 trillion in combined market cap in a single session; Nvidia alone shed over $440 billion, falling more than 9% and turning negative for 2026 (-2.6% YTD) for the first time this year. Breadth collapsed with it — NYSE decliners outnumbered advancers 2,344 to 416, and 174 S&P 500 names, 34.6% of the index, closed at least 20% below their 52-week highs. [fact, NEW]

Friday's October jobs report cut the other way. Nonfarm payrolls rose 165,000 against a consensus clustered near 59,000-65,000 — one of the largest beats of this cycle — and unemployment fell to 4.1% from 4.2%, also below forecast. August was revised up to 186,000 from 133,000 and September to 60,000 from 29,000, pointing to a labor market that cooled far less than the prior two prints had implied. [fact, NEW] Treasury yields rose on the data: the 10-year traded at 4.859% Friday afternoon, pushing back against the case for a near-term Fed cut; this brief could not independently confirm updated December-cut odds this run [flag]. Equities only partly recovered — Nasdaq +1.09% to 24,579.42, S&P +0.31% to 7,376.86, Dow still -0.46% to 51,285.73 — closing the week well below Wednesday's post-election highs on every index. Nvidia itself rebounded 2.78% to $186.64. [fact, NEW]

Read: the two stories are mechanically distinct but landed on the same tape. Thursday was a repricing of AI-capex duration — the same mechanism this brief has tracked in hyperscaler capex guides, now hitting the chip complex directly rather than the cloud platforms — arriving the day after an election gave the market something calmer to rally on. Friday's beat is a separate signal that both complicates and partly offsets it: strong headline job growth is unambiguous good news, but it came with average unemployment duration at 27.1 weeks, the highest outside the pandemic era since 2016 — a sign, per CNBC's own framing, that the headline strength sits atop a more uneven labor market underneath. [fact/read, NEW] Watch: Monday's open, for confirmation the AI-capex repricing has run its course rather than paused; whether Nvidia's return to positive YTD performance holds; and Tuesday's October CPI, the next data point markets will read against a jobs number this strong.

## Iran & Hormuz

Iran's war escalated again Friday night, on its own track from the market story above. Trump confirmed new US strikes on Qeshm Island — the same island the US hit Nov. 2, three days before the midterms — calling them "minor" and declining to give details beyond describing a strike that knocked out an anti-aircraft device in "five, maybe fifteen minutes." [fact, NEW] Iranian state media (Tasnim) reported two missile impacts on Qeshm with no official damage assessment; Iran's air defense separately claimed it downed a US MQ-9 drone over the island, and the IRGC said two tankers struck mines and caught fire in the strait — all single-sourced to Iranian state media and not independently corroborated this run [flag]. CENTCOM called the situation "relatively calm" hours later. Diplomatically, Iran's Bagheri Kani said Tehran wants "practical measures," not "paper commitments," before any new negotiating phase — a harder line than Trump's own claim of "a lot of progress" in talks.
