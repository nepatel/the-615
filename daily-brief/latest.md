## Lead: The AI trade starts discriminating — Marvell's beat-and-raise gets sold anyway

Marvell Technology reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of $2.739 billion Thursday after the close, a record, up 37% year-over-year and above the roughly $2.71-2.72 billion consensus. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.94 topped the $0.93 estimate; GAAP EPS was $0.33 on GAAP gross margin of 53.1% (58.9% non-GAAP). Data Center revenue reached $2.171 billion, up 46% year-over-year and now 79% of total sales, up 18% sequentially. The company guided third-quarter revenue to $3.15 billion, plus or minus 5% — above the roughly $3 billion figure CEO Matt Murphy had flagged a full quarter early — with non-GAAP EPS of $1.10, plus or minus a nickel. [NEW]

None of that stopped the stock from falling as much as 8.4% in after-hours and premarket trading, dragging Nasdaq 100 futures down roughly 0.3% into Friday's open even as S&P and Dow futures held closer to flat. The mechanism: alongside the beat, Marvell guided third-quarter non-GAAP gross margin to 57.5-58.5%, below the 58.9% just reported. CFO Dan Durn told analysts the sequential dip is being driven by custom-ASIC programs — chips built to hyperscaler specification for AI accelerators — ramping faster than the company's off-the-shelf optical and switching lines, which carry fatter margins. [fact] Rubrik, the backup-and-recovery software company, fell a similar 9% overnight on its own beat-and-raise: EPS of $0.22 against a four-cent estimate, revenue of $427.3 million against $396.3 million consensus, both blown past, followed by an almost identical sell-off. [read: two beat-and-raises punished the same night, on the same mechanism — expensive growth stocks that had run hard into the print (Marvell +187% year-to-date, Rubrik +85% over six months) getting sold on any imperfection in the mix rather than the headline number, is a different market behavior than two days earlier, when Nvidia's own beat-and-raise was rewarded outright.]

That contrast with Wednesday matters more than either print alone. Nvidia's Q3 guide beat consensus by nearly $4 billion and traded up 6% because nothing in the print complicated the growth story; Marvell's guide beat by a comparable margin and traded down because the mix did. [read: the AI-capex trade is no longer being priced as one undifferentiated basket — investors are starting to discriminate between hardware makers whose growth comes with margin dilution (Marvell's custom-silicon ramp, priced at hyperscaler-negotiated rates) and those whose growth doesn't (Nvidia's merchant-GPU pricing power). That's a more selective phase of the trade than the "any AI beat gets bought" pattern that held through most of August, and it complicates the setup for whichever comes next.] Marvell's customer book is also more concentrated than Nvidia's: its custom-ASIC and optical-interconnect revenue leans on a small set of hyperscalers, chiefly Google and Amazon, and Thursday's sell-side commentary specifically cited uncertainty over the timing of incremental Google-related revenue as a factor in the reaction. [read: concentration risk Nvidia's broader hyperscaler base doesn't carry as acutely.]

The result lands the futures tape in a more unsettled place than Wednesday's Nvidia-driven exuberance suggested three days ago — Nasdaq 100 futures red, S&P and Dow closer to flat — just as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh prepares to deliver his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair at 10:00 a.m. ET. [fact] A CNBC survey found 45% of respondents don't expect Warsh to expand on the rate outlook at all, and Fed officials remain publicly divided between a resilient growth tape and inflation still running above target. [read: Warsh now speaks into a market already discriminating within its highest-conviction trade rather than uniformly euphoric — a tougher room to move with a "blank piece of paper," his own description of the speech, than the one he'd have faced Wednesday afternoon.]
