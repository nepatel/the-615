## Lead: Oil sinks, stocks hit records, yields ease — on a Hormuz peace the water itself hasn't confirmed

Monday's session moved every major asset class in the same direction at once. Brent settled at $100.06, down 3.67%, and was still sliding before Tuesday's open — $98.28 on a live quote at 6:05a ET; WTI settled at $93.93 and traded as low as $89.87 pre-open. [NEW] The 10-year eased three basis points to 4.97%, off the 5.02% intraday high from Sept. 15 (issue No. 17). [continuity] Equities didn't just drift higher on the lower-rate math: the Nasdaq closed at a record 27,122.09 (+2.26%), the S&P at 7,764.70 (+1.49%), the Dow at 52,048.83 (+0.71%). [NEW] A single session with crude down mid-single-digits, yields easing, and equities up 1.5%-2.3% is a textbook geopolitical-risk-premium unwind. [read]

The mechanism being priced is specific: U.S. Central Command said Monday that oil "is moving" through the strait and that "momentum is building" toward normalization — a claim from an interested party, not an independent count. [flag] It sits atop the unconfirmed Iran-Oman reopening framework Araghchi floated over the weekend (issue No. 17), still not confirmed by Oman as of this run. [continuity/DEVELOPING]

What the water shows doesn't support that scale of repricing. The same Monday, UKMTO logged its fourth tanker strike since Sept. 17: the LR Stephanie, hit by an unidentified projectile entering the strait, two crew injured, vessel proceeding onward. [NEW] Straits.live still calls the closure "contested," not lifted; transit data (PortWatch's 8-vessel count against an 85/day baseline; Kpler's various framings from 13/day to sub-10/day on weekends) shows nothing like a volume recovery that independently justifies Monday's move. [read] War-risk premia remain 40x the pre-crisis baseline, with six P&I clubs having withdrawn coverage entirely. [read]

Some caution: the same session carried a distinct, well-documented driver for the equity leg — AMD closed above a $1T market cap for the first time, the fourth US chipmaker to do so, as Meta's Muse AI agent hit No. 1 on the App Store and reignited AI-infrastructure demand bets (Philadelphia Semiconductor Index +~4.3%). [NEW, see Technology] Some of Monday's equity strength is coincident with the oil-and-rates move, not caused by it.

Into a five-week gap before the next FOMC (Oct. 27-28): the market is pricing meaningful Gulf de-escalation on one interested party's "momentum" language and one unconfirmed diplomatic claim, against a strait where a tanker took a projectile hit the same day. If Oman denies the corridor claim, or the next strike lands on a larger target, Monday's unwind has real room to reverse across all three asset classes at once. [read]
