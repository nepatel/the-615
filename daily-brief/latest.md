## Lead: CoreWeave blows past estimates, Alphabet slides on capex and a DeepMind exit — the AI trade is splitting into winners and payers

CoreWeave reported second-quarter results Tuesday after the close that beat on both lines: revenue of roughly $2.58B (some outlets say $2.6B) against a $2.56B consensus (Bloomberg), up 112% year over year, and an adjusted per-share loss of $1.03–$1.14 (figures vary slightly by outlet) against a consensus loss of roughly $1.20–$1.41. [fact: beat confirmed across CNBC, Yahoo Finance, and Investing.com reporting; exact decimals not fully reconciled] Shares jumped 13–14% in after-hours trading to roughly $102 (CNBC). Backlog now exceeds $104B, up 246% year over year, on 51 active data centers and 1.5 gigawatts of active power as of June 30. Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to $12.4B–$13.2B, above the roughly $12.63B consensus, and disclosed new business in the quarter with Anthropic and Meta.

But the same quarter that vindicated CoreWeave's compute-scarcity thesis also widened its GAAP net loss to $626M, from $290M a year ago, driven by surging interest expense on the debt funding its buildout. That's precisely the mechanism Nvidia's $500B financing push — the non-binding MOUs with Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR disclosed Monday — is aimed at addressing: shifting some of that interest-rate exposure off compute providers' balance sheets and onto asset managers willing to underwrite debt backed by the chips and contracts themselves. CoreWeave's print is the clearest evidence yet of why that market needs to exist. Demand and backlog are real and growing; financing cost is eating into the upside anyway. [read: the beat validates AI-compute demand, but doesn't resolve who absorbs the cost of building the supply]

Alphabet is the other side of the same coin. Shares fell as much as 3.6% Tuesday (TradingKey) on two fronts landing at once: a DeepMind leadership shakeup, with CEO Demis Hassabis stepping down to chairman and AI scientist Jeff Dean departing to start a new venture, and a capex number that's now hard to wave off. Alphabet raised its 2026 capital-expenditure forecast to $195B–$205B, with quarterly capex up 100% year over year to $44.9B — pushing free cash flow negative for the first time. [fact] The same week, Alphabet closed a $25B senior notes offering with maturities running from 2028 to 2066 to help fund the buildout. [fact] Read: the market is starting to price hyperscaler AI capex as a credit event as much as an operating expense — the same repricing question CoreWeave's own interest bill just made concrete from the supply side. [read]

Tuesday's broader tape reflected the split rather than resolving it: the Dow fell 0.34% to 53,791.85, the S&P 500 slid 0.32% to 7,728.20, and the Nasdaq dropped 0.6% to 26,445.45 (Washington Post) — a down day driven as much by Alphabet's single-stock move and Hormuz-linked crude strength as by anything systemic. Energy majors outperformed again on the oil spike (see Iran & Hormuz, below); airlines gave back ground on jet-fuel costs, a split that's now recurred through most of this war.

Second-order, and the reason tonight matters: Cisco reports after the close (consensus $1.17 EPS on $16.83B revenue), with options traders pricing an 8.2%–8.3% post-earnings move — above the stock's own 7.75% four-quarter average. UBS is modeling 29% product-order growth on AI-infrastructure demand but flags gross-margin pressure, cutting margins to roughly 66% on higher component costs. [read] If Cisco's networking backlog echoes CoreWeave's, it reinforces that infrastructure demand is still outrunning financing comfort — the dynamic today's July CPI print, due at 8:30a ET after this issue goes to press, complicates further either way: a hotter-than-expected core reading tightens financial conditions right as hyperscalers are issuing record volumes of debt to fund the buildout Wall Street's been asked to trust.
