## Lead: MoU deadline lapses, Trump threatens to bomb Oman, UAE says it intercepted two Iranian missiles — and the 30-year Treasury hits a 19-year high hours before FOMC minutes

The 60-day window Washington and Tehran gave themselves in June's Islamabad-brokered memorandum of understanding to reach a "final deal" expired Monday with no agreement. [NEW] Asked if he'd extend it, Trump told reporters "No," said he was in no rush, and separately claimed his administration has established a direct backchannel to Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps — a claim the IRGC's spokesman, Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohbehi, called "delusions and nightmares...caused...by defeat and desperation in the war." Iran's foreign ministry piled on a second denial, with spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei separately rejecting reports of unofficial U.S.-IRGC contact via Iraqi Kurdistan officials and an alleged secret meeting in Erbil. [NEW] In the same Fox News interview, Trump turned on Oman, the mediator that brokered the still-unfinished shipping-lane technical deal with Iran: "If Oman gets in the way, we'll bomb the s--- out of them." [NEW] It's the second time this year Trump has threatened a Gulf state with no history of conflict with Washington and a record of quietly freeing detained Americans; Oman's government issued no public response, though Omani social-media commentators pushed back openly. [read: singling out the mediator, not Tehran, suggests the White House dislikes the substance of the emerging Iran-Oman route — a fee-free transitional period with inbound traffic in a northern lane through Iranian waters and outbound in a southern lane through Omani waters, coordinated via a proposed joint center — more than it's willing to say directly]

The deadlock escalated further Tuesday evening: UAE air defenses detected and intercepted two ballistic missiles fired from Iran toward the country, the first falling outside territorial waters and the second within them; an emergency alert went out across several emirates around 6:52p local before being lifted ten minutes later, with no casualties reported. [NEW] It's the first direct Iran-on-UAE missile launch acknowledged in months, a step beyond the ADNOC tanker strikes of the past week (see Iran & Hormuz).

Markets are treating the deadlock as durable, not headline, risk.
