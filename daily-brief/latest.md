## Lead: Nvidia's market cap correction meets its highest-stakes earnings call yet

Wall Street slid into Nasdaq correction territory Monday, hours before Nvidia reports under an interim CEO, an unresolved espionage-adjacent board probe, and dueling congressional subpoena tracks.

Monday was the worst session yet in the five trading days since Jensen Huang's ouster. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.5% to 24,509.76 — its third consecutive losing session and roughly 10.9% below its Oct. 6 high, putting the index in correction territory. The S&P 500 fell 1.9% to 7,416.59 and the Dow dropped 739 points (1.4%) to 51,608.17. [fact] Nvidia itself fell another 4.12% ahead of tonight's report, compounding Thursday's roughly 4.3% drop on the resignation news itself; one market analysis put the stock's cumulative decline at roughly 29% and $1.7 trillion in market value from its 2026 peak. [fact; flag, single-sourced on the cumulative figure] Coverage attributed the broader tech-led selloff to a mix of Nvidia-specific governance turmoil and renewed friction over chip-export policy, though the specific policy trigger wasn't independently verified this run. [read; flag]

The governance situation has not resolved, it has multiplied. Nvidia's board said Friday it is still selecting a search firm for a permanent CEO, with no timeline given; the Financial Times reported the board has approached Arm CEO Rene Haas, which Nvidia has not confirmed, calling candidate speculation "premature." [fact] An online petition for Huang's reinstatement has passed 170,000 signatures, with Elon Musk and other tech executives publicly backing him. [fact] Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent added a second front Friday, telling CNBC the real issue is whether Nvidia "knew its client" in the Megaspeed matter, and faulting Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's department for moving too slowly to restrict the chip exports at the center of the scandal — an unusually public cabinet-level rebuke. [fact] Congressional scrutiny is now running on two uncoordinated tracks: three House committees want Huang for a closed-door, transcribed interview Dec. 1, while Senate Banking Chairman Tim Scott has separately requested a public hearing with Huang on Dec. 3 and Lutnick on Dec. 10 — Lutnick's office has accepted voluntarily, Huang has not confirmed either date. [fact]

The mechanism from here is straightforwardly a test of whether markets are pricing an idiosyncratic Nvidia problem or a verdict on AI spending broadly. Nvidia reports fiscal Q3 after tonight's close under interim CEO (and sitting CFO) Colette Kress, with Wall Street clustered around $109–111 billion in revenue and $2.49–2.53 in adjusted EPS against Nvidia's own guide of $108 billion plus or minus 2% at a 74%-plus-or-minus-50-basis-point gross margin. [fact] That the stock has now fallen into its own correction — on top of the index-wide one — ahead of a print analysts still expect to beat, by Zacks' telling Nvidia has topped estimates in each of the last four quarters by an average 5.8 points, suggests the market is pricing governance risk and export-policy risk separately from the demand picture. [read] The counter-evidence sits one sector over: CoreWeave, Applied Materials and Cisco all beat estimates and raised guidance last week (issue No. 40), which argues the underlying AI-infrastructure build is intact even as Nvidia's own stock absorbs company-specific shocks. [fact, prior issue]

*Watch:* whether tonight's number and call commentary — on China exposure, the succession search, and the Megaspeed fallout — stop the stock's slide or accelerate it; whether a search-firm or candidate update lands before the print; and whether Huang confirms either congressional date this week.
