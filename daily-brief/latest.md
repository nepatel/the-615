## Lead: A hawkish Fed minutes and a blown retail comp print collide with Hormuz-driven oil — the "bad-data-is-good-news" trade breaks down

Wednesday's FOMC minutes did something markets hadn't priced: they showed the case for a rate *increase*, not a cut, moving well beyond the three regional presidents (Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari, Dallas's Lorie Logan) who dissented in favor of a quarter-point hike at the 9-3 vote to hold at 3.50%–3.75%. [NEW] There was reportedly no discussion of a cut anywhere in the minutes — a real reversal from a debate that began 2026 assuming inflation would cool enough to ease. [fact] That single document did more to move the rates market this week than any data print: the 2-year yield actually fell on the week (3.80% Fri) while the 10-year and 30-year rose (4.53%/5.27%), a bear-steepener that reads as the market pricing a Fed that holds short rates higher for longer while long-end term premium widens on fiscal and inflation-persistence worry. [read] Gold caught the same bid, pushing above $4,600/oz Friday — its highest since mid-May — on a combination of that fiscal unease and safe-haven demand tied to Hormuz. [NEW]

Into that setup landed Walmart's Wednesday print, arguably the most important data point of the week for anyone tracking the consumer. Revenue beat ($187.9B, +5.9% YoY) and adjusted EPS beat ($0.81 vs. $0.74 consensus), but U.S. comparable sales slowed to 2.6% — the weakest pace in more than six years — and full-year EPS guidance ($2.80–$2.87) came in below the $2.90 Street consensus. [NEW] The stock fell more than 9% intraday. [fact] Target and TJX told a friendlier version of the same story — value retailers still winning traffic — while Lowe's flagged renewed consumer hesitation on large discretionary purchases; Home Depot backed its outlook. [NEW] Put together: the "growth-scare data is Fed-friendly" pattern flagged in the last issue has broken. The Fed is now leaning hawkish at the exact moment consumer-spending data is cracking at the edges — a combination the market hasn't had to price in this cycle, and one that puts real weight on Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as Fed chair next Friday. [read]

Oil is the wildcard layered on top. Brent settled Friday at $93.78 (+2.4%) and WTI's October contract at $87.83 (+2.3%), both the highest since July 24, as the Strait of Hormuz crisis entered its 175th day with no reopening in sight and fresh escalation: the 60-day US-Iran memorandum deadline expired Monday with no deal, Trump threatened Sunday to "bomb the s*** out of Oman" if it interferes with negotiations he's simultaneously conducting outside official channels, and the UAE — a US-aligned Gulf state that had stayed commercially engaged with Tehran throughout the war — cut off all trade, commercial and financial dealings with Iran after intercepting two Iranian ballistic missiles Tuesday. [NEW] A sustained push toward $95 Brent adds directly to the same inflation-persistence case the FOMC minutes just made room for, which is the mechanism connecting all three stories this week: a hawkish Fed, a cracking consumer, and an oil market pricing supply risk from a war with no negotiated end in sight, all converging on Warsh's Jackson Hole debut. [read — this is the frame, not yet the market's consensus read]

## Iran & Hormuz

This window's central fact is the UAE's break with Tehran. After air defenses intercepted two Iranian ballistic missiles Tuesday — one falling in UAE territorial waters, the first such intercept since mid-July — Abu Dhabi announced just after midnight Wednesday it was suspending "all activities, trade exchanges, and financial transactions with Iran... until further notice," closing off one of Tehran's last significant economic lifelines at a moment its own oil exports are already visibly strained. [NEW] The 60-day US-Iran MoU deadline (signed at Versailles in June) expired Monday with no breakthrough; Trump said the deadline "wasn't a hard deadline" and there's no sign its expiration triggers new US strikes, but he separately threatened Monday to bomb Oman "if it gets in the way" of a deal — the second such threat of the war — while Oman continues mediating shipping-route talks with Tehran that Iran's IRGC says involve no direct US contact. [NEW] Those Oman-Iran talks have produced a finalized route map (northern inbound lane through Iranian waters, southern outbound lane through Omani waters, plus a proposed joint coordination center) but Tehran is explicit the map alone doesn't reopen the strait; Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf laid out the actual conditions this week — full removal of the naval blockade, lifted oil sanctions, and unfrozen assets, tied to the original 14-point MoU. [NEW] Treasury Secretary Bessent said further sanctions, described as the "toughest in history," are coming Monday; Iran's armed forces chief warned of a "crushing" response to new threats. [NEW, flag: sanctions substance not yet public — watch Monday]
