## Lead: The Fed holds with a hawkish dissent, Microsoft's discipline beats Alphabet's AI-capex surge, and Wall Street turns to GDP, claims and Amazon before tonight's close

Wednesday stacked three market-moving events into about four hours, and the pattern that emerged is as important as any single number. The Fed held its target range at 3.75%-4.00% for a third straight meeting, by a 10-2 vote — but the dissents ran hawkish, not dovish: Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack both wanted a quarter-point hike. [fact, NEW] Chair Kevin Warsh told reporters he doesn't "believe in forward guidance, absent exigent circumstances," called the economy strong, and said the committee would consider rate changes at only seven of eight meetings in 2027 and six of eight in 2028 — fewer decision points, not more easing. He denied politics factored into a decision landing six days before the midterms. [fact, NEW] Prediction markets, which had priced an Oct. 28 hold at roughly 83%-85% going in, now put the odds of a quarter-point hike at the Dec. 9 meeting at somewhere between 75% and 85% depending on the venue (DeFi Rate's Kalshi/Polymarket blend vs. a separate Yahoo Finance-sourced figure) — a sizable jump from the low-70s a week earlier. [fact/read, NEW]

Then came the split that mattered more for positioning. Microsoft's fiscal Q1 revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion, beating the Street's $90.61 billion estimate, with EPS of $4.92 (one tracker shows $4.89 on a constant-currency, OpenAI-adjusted basis — the gap is real and this brief flags it rather than picking one) against a $4.72 estimate. [fact, NEW] Azure grew 48% in constant currency, beating Microsoft's own 44%-45% guidance, while quarterly capital spending of $45.2 billion came in below the company's own "over $50 billion" outlook. [fact, NEW] Alphabet told a different story: revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion — its 13th straight quarter of double-digit growth, beating a $123.76 billion estimate — with Cloud revenue more than doubling, up 103% to $30.7 billion, and operating margin at 31.8%. [fact, NEW] But Search revenue growth decelerated to 12% from 17% the prior quarter, and capital expenditure more than doubled to $51.4 billion. [fact, NEW] Microsoft rose roughly 4%-5% after hours; Alphabet, which had closed the regular session up 1.6% at $321.89, fell about 10.5% to roughly $288. [fact, NEW]

Read: the stock reaction split almost exactly along capital-spending discipline, not the headline beat-or-miss. Microsoft spent less than it told investors it would and still posted the faster cloud growth; Alphabet spent more than double the prior year and still saw its core ad-search engine slow down. A revenue beat wasn't enough to protect a stock whose capex trajectory and growth mix both moved the wrong way in the same quarter. That is the question Amazon now has to answer tonight, after the 4p ET close and a 5p call: AWS has posted the industry's highest growth rate most of this year, but Amazon has also been among the most aggressive capital spenders — if its Q3 print reads like Alphabet's (capex up sharply, a core segment decelerating) rather than Microsoft's (spending under its own guide, growth beating it), expect a similarly unforgiving reaction. [read]

The second-order consequence sits one layer down: a Fed that just signaled its own center of gravity is shifting toward a December hike — not away from one — removes the rate-cut cushion that has underwritten a year of aggressive AI infrastructure spending by the hyperscalers. Cheaper financing was always the quiet assumption behind capex guides measured in the hundreds of billions; Wednesday's dissent axis argues that assumption needs re-pricing, right as Thursday's calendar delivers the GDP advance estimate and jobless claims at 8:30a ET — both still unreleased as this went to press — and Amazon's results test whether Wednesday's capex-discipline verdict was stock-specific or sets the bar for the whole group. [read] Watch: 8:30a for GDP (consensus ~3.2% annualized) and claims (last reading 194,000, a three-month low); the regular session's reaction to Wednesday's dual earnings prints, still working through the complex at the open; and whether Amazon's after-hours move tonight confirms or breaks the Microsoft-vs.-Alphabet split.
