## Lead: Wall Street's AI trade cracks — S&P 500 and Dow close in correction, Nasdaq erases its 2026 gain, as the chip-and-AI complex leads a $1-trillion-plus air pocket

Friday was the worst single session of the AI-led unwind that has been building since Nvidia's governance crisis broke open two weeks ago: the S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, its seventh consecutive daily loss and, per multiple wire reports, more than 10% below its October 6 record close of 7,818.93 — the conventional threshold for a market correction. [fact] The Dow Jones Industrial Average gave up 949.73 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33, reversing an early-session gain. [fact] The Nasdaq Composite was hit hardest, down 5.28% to 22,617.73 — a decline that erased the index's entire 2026 advance, per AP and NPR's reporting of the session. [fact] For the week, the S&P 500 lost 8.6% and the Nasdaq 10.0%, the worst weekly showing for both in well over a year by AP's count; the small-cap Russell 2000 fell 7.5%. [fact]

Mechanism: the selling was concentrated almost entirely in the chip-and-AI-infrastructure complex that has carried the broader market's gains all year. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) sank 10.7%; Nvidia alone fell on the order of 13-14% by Friday's close, according to Forbes and TradingKey's respective reporting, pushing its decline since Jensen Huang's Nov. 12 resignation past 10% and compounding a stock that was already down roughly 28% over the trailing month per issue No. 41. [fact; flag, two outlets give slightly different point-loss figures] Anthropic's ANT, which had closed its Thursday debut at $97.17 (+17% from its $83 IPO price, see issue No. 41), gave back most of that pop — Breitbart reported the stock fell 9.1% on just its second session — making the newest and most-watched AI listing on the tape a visible casualty of the same unwind it had arrived into. [fact] Corning and Bloom Energy, both proxies for AI-linked data-center buildout rather than chips directly, were cited by multiple outlets as also falling sharply, evidence the move is being read as a capex-cycle story, not a semiconductor-specific one. [fact/read]

Layered onto the broader AI-capex-skepticism narrative, which has been building since mid-November on questions of whether hyperscaler spending can be repaid by revenue growth, is a new and Nvidia-specific overhang: the Wall Street Journal reported Nov. 17, citing people familiar with the matter, that the SEC has opened a civil investigation into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors — a probe whose scope was not specified but which multiple sources told the Journal touches disclosure questions connected to the Huang-Megaspeed matter already the subject of December's House testimony (see Board). [fact, single primary source via WSJ; flag] Nvidia has not been accused of wrongdoing and an open SEC inquiry does not imply one will be found. [read] Separately, Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each fell more than 5% in Friday's own session, per AP, confirming the selloff's transmission beyond US markets even before Friday's Wall Street session opened. [fact]

Second-order consequence: this is now the second major data point in nine days on where the market is resetting AI-sector expectations — first Anthropic's IPO priced a $1.5 trillion valuation on growth alone (issue No. 41), and now the broader complex is being asked whether that growth justifies current capital intensity. The two aren't unrelated: Anthropic's own $26 billion private placement and nearly $1 trillion in disclosed infrastructure commitments through 2045 (issue No. 41) are underwritten by the same hyperscaler balance sheets — Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, AMD, Broadcom — now absorbing the equity-market repricing directly. [read] None of the wire coverage this brief checked called the move a bubble bursting outright; several, per BigGo's aggregation of Wall Street commentary, characterized it as a valuation adjustment within an intact bull market rather than a reversal of the underlying AI-spending thesis. [read, attributed]
