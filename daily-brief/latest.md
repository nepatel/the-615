## Lead: A hot CPI print cracks the AI trade — Kospi's worst day since the bubble began, and oil falls even as Hormuz gets more dangerous, not less

September's CPI landed hot at 8:30a ET Wednesday: headline inflation rose 3.7% year-over-year (not seasonally adjusted), above a roughly 3.6% consensus, while core CPI (ex-food and energy) accelerated to 2.6% y/y from 2.4% in August. [NEW, fact, BLS] On a seasonally adjusted monthly basis, headline CPI rose 0.6% — up from August's 0.4% — while core rose 0.3%, matching August. [fact, BLS] BLS attributed the headline surprise primarily to fuel prices; in one of the report's odder line items, candy and chewing gum prices rose 7.7% y/y on higher cocoa costs. [fact, secondary coverage of the release, minor color]

The print did what hot CPI prints do to a market pricing a soft landing: it raised the implied cost of capital at the exact moment the index's single most expensive trade — AI infrastructure capex — has no margin for a higher discount rate. [read] The mechanism: a hotter-than-expected inflation path narrows the case for near-term Fed easing, which matters disproportionately to companies whose valuations assume both cheap financing for data-center buildout and uninterrupted hyperscaler demand. Asia opened into that repricing first and got hit hardest. The Kospi fell roughly 8% intraday, its sharpest move of this cycle, pulled down by Samsung Electronics (-9.9%) and SK Hynix and Kioxia Holdings (each down more than 11%) — the three firms most directly levered to AI memory-chip demand. [NEW, fact, Reuters via Euronext/Irish Times, single primary source for the Kospi figure] The Nikkei 225 closed down 4.90% (Yahoo), with other wires citing figures from 4.8% to 5.4% depending on snapshot time — not reconciled here. [fact/flag] Wednesday's US cash session already carried the move stateside: the S&P 500, which had set a record high only the week before, fell 1.1%, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.9%. [fact, Reuters] This morning's premarket shows no bounce — S&P futures -0.30%, Nasdaq 100 futures -1.17% as of 6:08a ET — while the VIX is up over 4% and gold has caught a safe-haven bid, up 1.73% to $4,179.10. [fact, Yahoo quotes, this morning]

What's conspicuously not moving in sympathy is oil, and that's the cross-asset tell worth sitting with. Brent is down 2.22% to $103.71 and WTI is down slightly to $93.63 this morning — even as the Hormuz situation on the Board got, if anything, more disordered overnight: IMF PortWatch's transit count fell to just 2 vessels on Oct. 11 (a new cycle low), ABC is describing strike frequency at its highest level since the war began, and straits.live is reporting disputed tanker incidents it says it can't yet reconcile. [fact, see Board] Two explanations are live, not mutually exclusive: either the market has already priced the supply-side Hormuz risk as high as it's going to go absent a confirmed production outage at Aramco (none has been confirmed, per this run's research — see Iran & Hormuz), or demand-side risk-off from the equity rout is simply outweighing the geopolitical premium for now. [read] Either way, a reader positioned for "Hormuz risk = oil up" needs to update: the correlation broke this morning. Watch today's PPI, retail sales and jobless claims at 8:30a for whether the inflation story broadens or was fuel-driven noise, and whether Friday's open sees Asia's chip-sector rout stabilize or extend into a second session.
