## Lead: A Houthi missile salvo aimed at the port absorbing Aramco's Hormuz bypass collides with a US-Iran phased-deal trial balloon, whipsawing oil in a single session

The most important oil story of the last 24 hours isn't one story — it's two contradictory ones landing on the same tape within hours of each other. Thursday morning, the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted six Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Yanbu, the Red Sea port that has absorbed Saudi Arabia's entire strategy for routing crude around the Strait of Hormuz since the East-West "Petroline" pipeline restarted Sept. 22. The Houthis later claimed they had struck "a sensitive target" in Riyadh and Aramco facilities specifically at Yanbu; Saudi authorities reported no damage or casualties, and civil-defense alerts covering Mecca, Tabuk, Jeddah, Yanbu and Taif were lifted within hours. [fact] Almost simultaneously, Reuters and Bloomberg reported that US and Iranian negotiators in New York are exploring a phased arrangement — Tehran reopens the strait to navigation in exchange for Washington lifting its naval blockade of Iranian ports — with a senior Iranian official calling it the most realistic path forward. [fact] Oil spiked to just over $108 a barrel intraday on the combination of the attack and a separate report that Asian refiners are importing crude at the fastest pace since the war began, then gave back roughly half those gains once the phased-deal story broke, settling Brent at $106.60, up 3.4% on the day. [fact]

The mechanism worth sitting with is the target selection, not just the price move. Markets had been treating the Petroline restart as the release valve on Hormuz risk — a way for roughly 4 million barrels a day of Saudi crude to reach tankers without transiting the strait at all, decoupling at least part of global supply from the blockade. A strike aimed at the exact port that bypass discharges into, whether or not it does physical damage, is a direct challenge to that thesis: it signals the Houthis can extend pressure to the alternative route, not just the strait itself. [read] That the attack failed to land doesn't fully neutralize the signal — war-risk premia held steady this run at 7.5%-10% of hull value rather than spiking further, suggesting the market is for now treating Thursday as a near-miss rather than a regime change. [fact/read] But it sits awkwardly next to the same day's diplomatic story, which points the opposite direction: an actual reduction in blockade risk, not an escalation of it. Two signals, on the same route, in the same 24 hours, pulling oil in opposite directions — which is exactly what a $108-to-$106.60 round trip looks like on a chart. [read]

The knock-on read for a portfolio: none of this yet shows up in the numbers that would confirm a real change in physical supply. Kepler's preliminary strait-transit count ticked up to 13 crossings Friday from the high-single-digits seen earlier in September — better, but still roughly 15% of the 85-a-day pre-crisis baseline, and not corroborated yet by PortWatch's own slower-updating series (see Hormuz counters). Dennis Ross, who has negotiated this region before, put the odds of an actual deal this week at only about 30%. [fact] That combination — a real but unconfirmed uptick in traffic, a credible but long-shot diplomatic track, and a demonstrated willingness by the Houthis to test the alternative route — argues for treating Thursday's price relief as provisional rather than a turn. [read] It also lands at an inconvenient moment for the inflation debate: crude near a post-attack high going into next Wednesday's August PCE print gives the Fed one more input pointing toward the hawkish side of an already-hawkish September, with retail gasoline already at AAA's highest-ever level for this point in the calendar year. [read] The bond market's separate rout — the 10-year brushing a 2007 high and the 30-year a 2004 high this week — was already pricing sticky inflation before Thursday's oil action; a crude market that can't decide between escalation and de-escalation in the same session does nothing to resolve that question one way or the other. [read]
