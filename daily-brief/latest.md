## Lead: Democrats sweep the House and Senate, delivering the midterm rebuke markets had already priced — and solving, for now, the question the Qeshm strikes raised about Election Day

The projection arrived in two stages overnight. The AP called the Senate for Democrats late Tuesday, a seven-seat swing that flipped Ohio, Iowa, South Carolina, Maine, North Carolina, Kansas and Texas; the House call followed early Wednesday, completing a clean sweep of both chambers. [fact, NEW] Democrats needed a net gain of four Senate seats and three House seats to take control; they cleared both marks comfortably enough that networks called the chambers before several individual races were fully tabulated. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries is now positioned to become the first Black speaker in the chamber's history, pending the incoming House's own vote to formalize it. [fact, NEW] Coverage from the Washington Post and others frames the result as a rebuke of the administration, tying it to two threads this brief has been carrying for weeks: persistent voter anger over prices, and the Iran war — including Monday night's Qeshm Island strikes, which landed hours before polls opened despite Trump's own repeated pledge not to hit Iran before the vote (issue No. 35). [fact/read, continuity]

The market's reaction is the mechanism worth sitting with. A Democratic sweep of Congress is, on its face, the more "disruptive" outcome for an administration's legislative agenda — and risk assets rallied anyway. VIX collapsed 11% overnight to 18.52, S&P and Nasdaq futures both pushed roughly 0.7%-0.8% higher pre-market, and Dow futures jumped over 1.2%, even as the cash indices had already closed mixed Tuesday (S&P +0.36%, Nasdaq +0.83%, Dow -0.38%). [fact, NEW] The read: markets are pricing divided government as a check on fiscal and trade unpredictability rather than as gridlock risk — the same framing investors apply after most midterms, but with extra weight here given how much of this year's volatility has been administration-policy-driven rather than cyclical. [read] The dollar sold off on the result (DXY -0.55% to 101.31, lowest since late September per CNBC) and gold caught the other side of that trade, rallying roughly 2.6%-2.9% to the low $4,000s — a textbook risk-off-into-a-weaker-dollar move that is unusual paired with an equity futures rally, and points to investors buying insurance against political uncertainty even as they add risk. [fact/read, NEW]

Oil is the one market still digesting Monday night's strikes rather than the election. Brent sources disagree by nearly $2 within the same early-morning hour — Yahoo's $102.66 against straits.live's own $104.15 read minutes earlier — which this brief reads as a market still searching for a level after Monday's 7.4% plunge on de-escalation hope collided with the Qeshm strikes' re-escalation risk (issue No. 35). [fact/flag, continuity] The straits.live dark-tanker screen, meanwhile, is essentially unchanged at 69.4% versus Tuesday's 69.1% — not the second consecutive jump that would confirm a trend tied to the strikes, just a plateau at an elevated level the site itself cautions could be ambient AIS noise. [fact, DEVELOPING] For the investor reader, the actionable split is: election risk is now resolved and priced, with equities and vol both reflecting relief; Hormuz risk is not resolved, sits exactly where Monday night left it, and is the variable still capable of moving crude and the inflation path from here. Watch: any further Iranian response to the Qeshm strikes; whether Wednesday's Brent session actually settles; and Friday's jobs report, the first hard data point the new Congress will have to react to.
