## Lead: The US-Iran deal clock ran out Monday. Trump answered by threatening to bomb Oman.

The 60-day window Washington gave itself to convert June's ceasefire memorandum into a lasting Strait of Hormuz settlement expired Monday with no agreement and no extension. [NEW] Asked directly whether he'd extend the June 18 MoU, Trump said "No," then told Fox News's Trey Yingst that if Oman "gets in the way" of reopening the strait, "we'll bomb the s*** out of them" — a threat aimed at a longtime US partner that has spent the crisis trying to broker exactly the reopening Washington says it wants. [fact: quotes dated 8/17] [read: naming Oman, not Iran, as the obstacle is the tell — Washington's frustration has shifted from Tehran's intransigence to the mediator's inability to deliver terms Iran will actually honor, seven weeks after the MoU promised a settled outcome]

Iran's Foreign Ministry offered a sliver of the deal Trump wants credit for: spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said Tehran and Oman had reached "an understanding...regarding the map of the transit route" — the same routing-only progress reported Friday, now confirmed from Tehran's side. [DEVELOPING] But the IRGC moved to close off the backchannel Trump has repeatedly cited, calling his claims of informal talks with the Guard "fantasies caused by delusions" and denying any US contact. [NEW] The split — a civilian ministry conceding technical progress while the military wing denies talking to Washington at all — reads as the same factional gap this brief has tracked all month, not a new rupture. [read] The Oman threat itself is a break in kind, not just degree: this war has run through months of strikes, sanctions and rhetoric aimed at Iran directly, but Trump had not previously threatened to bomb a third-party Gulf state acting as mediator. Oman has hosted the only sustained US-Iran channel of the entire crisis; making the mediator a target, even rhetorically, raises the question of who is left to carry messages between Washington and Tehran if Muscat steps back. [read]

The shipping numbers back the stalemate rather than any thaw. Two trackers converge on the same collapse: Kpler counted 5 vessels transiting Saturday and zero Sunday, against a 31-vessel prior weekend and a pre-war baseline near 130/day; MarineTraffic's daily count shows crossings falling 19.5% week-on-week, from 19 on Aug. 11 to 3 on Aug. 16. [NEW] Neither captures the last 24 hours: a vessel was struck by an unidentified projectile at 09:48 UTC Tuesday with a crew member injured, per straits.live's tracker — the latest in a strike pattern Iran hasn't paused despite, or perhaps because of, the deadline pressure. [NEW, flag: single-sourced]
