## Lead: The Fed's chair is Kevin Warsh, not Powell, and the base case today is a hold at 3.75%-4.00% — a correction, landing on the day it matters most

The Federal Reserve's chair is Kevin Warsh, not Jerome Powell — the Senate confirmed him 54-45 on May 13, 2026, and he took the gavel May 15; Powell remains a Fed governor, a term that runs to 2028, but hasn't chaired a meeting in over five months. [fact] The policy rate itself has moved too: the FOMC voted 12-0 on Sept. 16 to raise the target range a quarter point, to 3.75%-4.00% — the first hike since July 2023. [fact] Yesterday's issue of this brief (No. 31) referred to "Powell" and to a target "3.50-3.75%, unchanged since June 17" — both wrong, and both fixed as of this issue. This brief's own archive tracked the hike and the chair change correctly as recently as Sept. 24; the error crept back in once Fed coverage lapsed for a month between meetings. Nothing else in prior issues is affected. [fact, correction]

[fact] A hold is priced as close to a lock: a Kalshi-linked prediction market shows roughly 95% odds of no change, 6% of a hike, 1% of a cut; Manifold's independent market also shows cut odds at 1%. CME FedWatch, the standard reference, again returned a server error at press time, so this brief is using the same prediction-market proxies as yesterday. [fact/flag] The mechanism worth sitting with: September's hike wasn't a one-off the committee is eager to reverse. [fact/read] Watch whether Warsh's 2:30p remarks reinforce or soften that December signal.
