## Lead: October CPI comes in hot and the 10-year punches through 5% — a furious, CoreWeave-powered rebound in stocks papers over a bond market that keeps getting more hawkish

Tuesday's October CPI landed hotter than expected on both lines: headline CPI rose 0.4% month over month against a 0.3% consensus, and core CPI rose 0.3% for a third straight month against a 0.2% consensus, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics' 8:30 a.m. release. [fact] The BLS again published no 12-month rate — a continuing side effect of the data gap left by last year's shutdown — so every annual inflation comparison cited this fall, including in this brief, is a modeled estimate rather than an official figure. [fact] Fed Governor Christopher Waller had said before the release that a third consecutive 0.3% core print could put a larger, half-point December increase "on the table," and traders took him at his word: the 10-year Treasury yield jumped 11 basis points to close Tuesday at 4.99%, the 2-year rose roughly 17–18 basis points to about 4.72%–4.74% (sources diverge on the exact print), and futures moved to fully price a December hike with two more seen by next June. [fact, flag on exact 2Y print] Equities read the data as a straightforward negative: the S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,520.93, giving back roughly a third of Monday's Hormuz-truce rally in a single session.

Wednesday reversed the mood without reversing the rate move. Bond markets were shut for Veterans Day, but equities traded, and CoreWeave's Tuesday-night earnings beat — $3.66 billion in revenue, up 168% year over year and 3.4% above the $3.54 billion consensus, adjusted operating income of $379 million against $200–260 million guidance, and a raised full-year outlook to $13.0–13.3 billion — gave the AI-infrastructure trade its best hard data point since last week's capex-payback selloff. [fact] The S&P 500 rallied to roughly 7,619–7,626 (+1.3%–1.4%, outlets differ on the exact close) and the Nasdaq Composite gained about 1.6% into the mid-25,000s, led by chipmakers. [fact, flag on exact closes] That happened with the bond market closed and the dollar still climbing — DXY settled Wednesday at 102.92, up sharply from Monday's 101.73 — which is the tell that Wednesday's rally was a single hard data point (CoreWeave's print) layered onto a macro backdrop that kept getting more hawkish underneath it, not a reversal of that backdrop. [read] Nvidia itself did not participate: its board's investigation into CEO Jensen Huang stretched into a second evening of meetings Wednesday after a Wall Street Journal report that directors were weighing his removal, which sent the stock down further intraday; no decision had been confirmed as of this run. [fact; see Technology]

By Thursday morning the rate move had kept going. The 10-year crossed 5.00% for the first time this brief has confirmed, with the 30-year holding near 5.35%, per CNBC's early read, while gold — which should benefit from the same risk-aversion lifting yields — instead fell to roughly $4,000–$4,007 an ounce, down about 3% from Monday's $4,138.25 close, consistent with investors pricing higher real rates rather than panic. [fact] Equity futures were choppy and modestly softer early Thursday, weighed down again by Nvidia: one pre-market snapshot had Nasdaq-100 futures down about 0.96%, S&P 500 futures off roughly 0.37%, and Dow futures up about 0.16% — a split tape, with large-cap industrials diverging from tech even before the open. [fact, flag: a single timestamped snapshot, other reads differ] The mechanism worth tracking: a 10-year above 5% raises the discount rate on every long-duration, capital-intensive bet — AI infrastructure chief among them — at the same moment CoreWeave's print is being cited as proof that the capex cycle still works. Those two forces are now pulling against each other inside the same stocks, and Friday's PPI, plus whatever Nvidia's board ultimately decides, will go a long way toward settling which one wins the week. [read]

*Watch:* whether Nvidia's board resolves Huang's status before Friday's PPI print adds a second hot read to the week; whether Thursday's early yield move holds into a confirmed close above 5%; and whether Applied Materials' results after tonight's close get read as an AI-capex signal or get swamped entirely by the rate story.
