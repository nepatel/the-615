## Lead: July CPI cools in line, hike odds slide toward 40% — but oil's overnight reversal complicates the read, and PPI is today's real test

July's Consumer Price Index rose 3.4% year-over-year (+0.1% month-over-month), with core CPI — ex-food-and-energy — at 2.5% YoY (+0.2% MoM), both a tenth of a point below June and squarely on the Dow Jones consensus survey, per Wednesday's BLS release. Shelter did most of the monthly work (roughly two-thirds of the increase, +0.1%), food added another 0.1%, and energy fell 1.5% on the month even though it's still running 14.7% higher year-over-year — gasoline alone is up 24.6% YoY, the line item that ties this report directly to the Hormuz-driven crude rally of the past two weeks. [fact: BLS, CNBC]

Markets read the in-line print as room for the Fed to sit still. CME FedWatch-implied odds of a September hike eased to roughly 40% (60% no-change) as of Wednesday evening, continuing a slide from 67% in late July through 44.4% a week ago — a trajectory that started with July's shock -23,000 payrolls print and continued through Wednesday's data, even as bond traders notably haven't abandoned the hike bet altogether. [Bloomberg] The 10-year eased to 4.68%, the 30-year to 5.24%, the 2-year to 4.18%; the S&P 500 added 0.26% to 7,748.50 and the Nasdaq Composite 0.54% to 26,588.49, both extending into this morning's futures (S&P +0.2%, Nasdaq-100 +0.6%).

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh — who delivered a hawkish shock at his first meeting, with nine of 18 participants penciling in a 2026 hike — is reportedly still open to raising in September if incoming data runs hot. [single-sourced: FT, via CNBC, citing people familiar with his thinking] That's precisely the test today's July PPI (8:30a ET, consensus +0.2% MoM) and jobless claims (consensus 202K vs. 199K prior) set up before the open. [read] PPI matters more than usual this cycle because it's the more direct transmission channel for oil-driven cost-push pressure into core goods — a hot print reopens the hike debate; a soft one pushes September toward a clean hold and likely pressures the dollar further. Watch the reaction in the front end specifically: a hot PPI that moves the 2-year more than the 30-year would confirm markets are repricing Warsh's reaction function; a move concentrated in the long end instead would say it's still an energy-pass-through story, not a Fed one.

Crude, meanwhile, is telling a different story than it was 48 hours ago. Brent fell 1.19% to $87.92 and WTI 1.39% to $82.11 overnight — a sharp reversal of Tuesday's +2.5%/+2.7% spike — even though the underlying Hormuz standoff hasn't budged: Iran restated Wednesday that the strait stays closed until its conditions are met, directly contradicting President Trump's "100% control"/"wall of steel" claims from earlier in the week. [fact] The likelier explanation isn't de-escalation — it's profit-taking after a three-plus-day run, compounded by Tuesday's first confirmed U.S. Navy kinetic strike on a blockade-running vessel (see Iran & Hormuz), which reads as harder enforcement rather than any softening of the standoff. [read]

Net effect for the desk: a rate-path narrative that's been steadily de-risking (67%→44%→~40% hike odds) is now the dominant driver of the tape, with Hormuz behaving like a slow-moving tail risk rather than the day's price-setter — the reverse of Tuesday's dynamic, when oil did the driving. That makes this morning's PPI more consequential than a typical mid-tier release. [read]
