## Lead: Stocks erase Friday's rout and then some, oil holds near its highs, as fresh Hormuz claims land without a flinch — now comes the Fed and Big Tech

Monday's session did something Friday's didn't: it shrugged off the Strait of Hormuz. Two fresh, uncorroborated incident reports landed squarely in market hours — an explosion near Sirik amid claimed anti-ship missile fire at 11:58Z (7:58a ET), and a gCaptain report of a tanker disabled and a second vessel afire in the Persian Gulf at 18:46Z (2:46p ET) — and equities kept climbing through both. The Dow added 146.42 points (+0.28%) to 52,361.58; the S&P 500 jumped 53.09 (+0.70%) to 7,676.77; the Nasdaq Composite rose 214.04 (+0.82%) to 26,169.38. [fact, NEW] The S&P and Nasdaq didn't just recover Friday's losses — both closed above Thursday's pre-selloff levels, meaning Monday's rally more than erased the damage this brief described in issue No. 30. The Dow didn't: its gain clawed back only about 22% of Friday's 652-point drop, the weakest of the three. [fact/read, NEW]

That split is a tell. The S&P and Nasdaq carry more weight in the mega-cap tech names reporting this week — Microsoft and Alphabet after Wednesday's close, Amazon after Thursday's — while the Dow holds more of the oil-sensitive industrials and transports still facing a Brent benchmark stuck near $116. [read] Oil itself barely moved on the fresh strike claims: Brent trades at $116.00 and WTI at $102.04 this morning, both a shade under Friday's settles ($116.72/$103.27) rather than above them. [fact, NEW] If Monday's reports had been priced as credible escalation, the textbook reaction — a fresh leg up in crude — didn't happen. Read: traders appear to be discounting Iranian strike claims by default, after a cadence of IRGC assertions this month — an Oct. 17 two-supertanker mine claim that the JMIC said had "no substantiation," an Oct. 20 claim of two more mined tankers, an Oct. 24 claim of two disabled supertankers — that UKMTO, CENTCOM and independent monitors have repeatedly failed to corroborate. [read]

That skepticism now has one more data point to weigh against. Iran's armed forces chief said early Tuesday (07:09Z) that the strait is "completely closed," per Mehr and other state media — a claim with no independent verification, and one CENTCOM has called false before. [fact, NEW] Meanwhile straits.live's dark-tanker screen, which jumped sharply Friday, has essentially plateaued rather than accelerated: 70.8% of screened tankers ran dark in the past 24 hours versus Friday's 72.0%, both well above the 96.0 seven-day average. The pattern of disputed claims is itself the story this week. An Oct. 17 IRGC claim that two supertankers struck mines was met by a JMIC advisory the next day stating there was "no substantiation" to it; UKMTO's own account of that period described a rocket-propelled-grenade strike and an unidentified-projectile strike, not mines. An Oct. 20 claim (two more tankers, mines) and an Oct. 24 claim (two supertankers disabled by mines) followed the same arc — asserted by Iranian state channels, never corroborated by UKMTO, a flag state, or an insurer. Monday and Tuesday's claims — the Sirik explosion, the disabled tanker, and now a flat assertion of total closure — sit on top of that unresolved run, with President Trump's dismissal of the Oct. 23 seven-vessel claim as fabricated still unanswered by either government. [fact, continuity]

Parliament speaker Ghalibaf has said the strait stays shut until all seven conditions, based on the Islamabad memorandum, are met.
