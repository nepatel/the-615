## Lead: Markets reprice from a Fed cut to a December hike, swallowing Nvidia's earnings beat whole

Six weeks ago, futures markets were still debating how many more rate cuts the Fed would deliver this cycle. This week they are debating whether it hikes instead. [read, framing] CME FedWatch put the odds of a quarter-point increase at the Dec. 9 meeting at roughly 91% as of Nov. 16, with Kalshi and Polymarket both near 89% and a small minority of traders beginning to price a half-point move. [fact, multi-sourced prediction-market data] The catalyst was October's CPI print nine days ago — core inflation +0.3% for a third straight month, as reported in issue No. 40 — reinforced this week by Fed Governor Christopher Waller, who said the committee should hike in December given "sticky" inflation. [fact] The Fed's target range has sat at 3.75%–4.00% since its October meeting; a hike next month would be its first increase since September. [fact, CNBC]

The repricing has moved every rates-sensitive market at once. The 10-year yield traded near 5.10% Tuesday, having now held above 5% for 27 separate sessions this year, per Bloomberg. [fact] The dollar index touched its highest level in 19–20 months this week, around 103. [fact; flag, no clean Tuesday close found] Gold — which should catch a safe-haven bid from macro stress — instead fell 3% to $3,866 on Nov. 12, as the same hawkish repricing that lifted yields also raised the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. [fact/read] Equities took it worst: Monday was the market's roughest session in two weeks (Nasdaq Composite -2.5%, S&P 500 -1.9%), and Tuesday extended the slide — S&P 500 -1.0% to its lowest close since July 29, Dow -1.6%, Nasdaq -0.7%. [fact, Washington Post/CNBC; flag, percent moves only, exact closing levels not independently confirmed this run]

Nvidia's third-quarter report, released after Tuesday's close, should have been the week's good news arriving at the worst possible moment to be ignored. Revenue came in at $115.8B against a roughly $109B Street consensus — up about 103% year-over-year — with adjusted EPS of $2.68 [flag, single-sourced] and a Q4 revenue guide of about $130B, plus or minus 3%, against a consensus nearer $125B. [fact, multi-sourced on revenue and guide] Shares rose only around 4% in after-hours trading (estimates ranged 1.3%–4.8% across sources in the first hour) and were up 3.96% premarket Wednesday, per CNBC — a muted pop for a quarter this strong. [read] Newly installed board chair Harvey Jones used the call to rule out any Huang return, leaving interim CEO Colette Kress in place with no successor timetable; an unresolved Singapore-Malaysia chip-smuggling export probe continues to hang over the stock (see Business). [fact] One secondary tally put Nvidia's two-session pre-earnings drawdown at $424B and its pullback from October's peak at 29%, or roughly $1.7T [flag, single-sourced, unconfirmed against exchange data] — context for how low a bar "muted" was clearing.

The thread connecting both stories is the discount rate. A December hike raises the rate used to value the long-duration cash flows priced into every AI-capex stock, Nvidia foremost among them; that a beat-and-raise quarter moved the stock only a few points either way is itself a verdict on how much of this year's AI rally has been a rates story as much as an earnings one. [read] For now, markets are treating Nvidia's leadership vacuum as a company-specific problem rather than a referendum on AI spending broadly [read] — a read that December's FOMC decision, arriving three weeks after whichever Huang congressional date actually holds (see Technology), will test directly.

*Watch:* whether today's FOMC minutes (2p ET, covering Oct. 27–28) show the committee already split toward a December move; whether Nvidia's after-hours gain survives Wednesday's cash session; and which of Huang's three unreconciled congressional dates — today, Thanksgiving, or Dec. 1 — actually holds.
