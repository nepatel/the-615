## Lead: Oil closes at a war-high, stocks sink, as a fresh unverified Iranian mine claim piles onto Thursday's unresolved fabrication dispute

Friday's calm didn't hold. Brent settled at $116.72 a barrel, up 3.14% on the session and the highest close of the seven-month war, per oilprice.com and straits.live, which agree on the figure; WTI settled at $103.27, up 3.82%. [fact, NEW] The Brent-WTI spread widened to roughly $13.45, the widest this brief has tracked — a sharper divergence than the ~$8.59 spread flagged in issue No. 29 and a sign the crude complex, not just the benchmark everyone quotes, is under stress. [fact/flag, NEW] That settlement reverses the partial, Yahoo-feed pullback this brief reported Friday morning, when Brent had eased to $109.67 after Thursday's $111.62 print; by the close, the Yahoo and straits.live feeds had converged on a number well above either. [fact, NEW]

Equities followed oil lower through the full session rather than extending Friday morning's calmer tone. The Dow fell 652.82 points, or 1.23%, to 52,215.16; the S&P 500 slipped 0.52% to 7,623.68; the Nasdaq Composite eased 0.21% to 25,955.34, its second straight weekly loss. The VIX rose 2.21% to 19.45, putting it on track to close a week above 20 for the first time in some weeks, per Yahoo Finance's wrap. [fact, NEW] That is a meaningfully worse outcome than the futures-implied open this brief carried Friday morning (S&P futures -0.20%, Nasdaq 100 futures -0.03%) — the session widened the damage rather than holding the morning's partial recovery from Thursday's panic. [fact/read, NEW]

Treasury yields moved the other way from Thursday's flight-to-quality bid. The 2-, 10- and 30-year all rose roughly 4 basis points into Friday's official close (to 4.69%, 5.00% and 5.33%), reversing part of Thursday's 13-16bp drop that this brief flagged as inconsistent with a stronger-than-expected jobless-claims print. [fact, NEW] Read: Thursday's bid for safety was already fading before any new Hormuz claim arrived Friday night — the bond market, unlike equities, did not re-extend the fear trade into the weekend. [read]

Into that setup, a new and still-unverified claim: Iran's IRGC Navy said overnight that two supertankers struck and were disabled by naval mines in what it calls the mined corridor of the strait. No maritime authority, flag state or independent monitor has confirmed it, and UKMTO has issued no matching advisory. [fact, NEW] It follows directly from Thursday's seven-vessel claim, which President Trump branded fabricated on Truth Social and which also remains uncorroborated; nothing has moved to settle that dispute one way or the other since issue No. 29 went out. [fact, continuity] Separately, straits.live's AIS dark-tanker screen jumped to 180 of 250 vessels dark in the past 24 hours (72.0%), up sharply from Friday's 103-of-190 reading (54.2%) and well above the 100.2 seven-day average — though the tracker's own methodology note says 180 sits close to what ambient AIS-signal dropout alone would produce, so this one reading should not yet be read as a change in evasive behavior. [fact/flag, DEVELOPING]

None of this resolves the asymmetry this brief flagged Friday: if Iran's claims are substantially true, the market's Friday pricing — a new oil high, a steeper equity selloff, yields drifting back up — looks like the correct reaction to a real escalation; if they are substantially fabricated, as the president contends, Friday's close priced in another round of claims that may not hold up. [read] One wrinkle cuts against a clean "fear trade" reading either way: gold fell 1.3% to roughly $4,008 even as the rest of the tape moved toward risk-off, the same unreconciled pattern this brief has now flagged twice. [fact/flag] Watch: whether UKMTO, a flag state or any insurer corroborates or debunks the new mine claim; whether Saturday's dark-tanker share holds above 70% or reverts toward the 100.2 baseline; whether PortWatch's transit count updates past its six-day-old Oct. 18 reading; and whether Monday's Asia open extends Friday's selloff or reverses it again.
