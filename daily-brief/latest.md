## Lead: UAE severs economic ties with Iran after Hormuz missile alert; Trump vows "most crushing" sanctions yet as Brent tops $93

The UAE said Wednesday it is suspending all trade and financial transactions with Iran "until further notice" — the sharpest break yet between two historically close trading partners — after its Ministry of Defense said two Iranian ballistic missiles were fired toward UAE waters Tuesday evening, one falling short in international waters and the other landing inside UAE territorial waters. [NEW] A missile-threat alert had sounded in Dubai that evening. Iran denied responsibility; state media called it a "false-flag operation," and no group has claimed the launch. [fact: UAE statement and MoD assessment vs. Iran's denial — neither independently verified by us]

Hours later, Trump escalated on Truth Social, promising the "most crushing economic operation" ever mounted against Iran and threatening "TREMENDOUS Economic Consequences" for any country whose banks, businesses, airports or government entities give Tehran "any type of lifeline." [NEW] It extends the administration's five-month "Operation Economic Fury" campaign; specifics of the new measures haven't been published. Iran's FM Araghchi dismissed it as "economic terrorism"; state media said Tehran has already adapted to existing restrictions. [read: the UAE break is the first hard supply-chain fact to come out of this flare-up, not just rhetoric]

Oil is pricing the UAE break. Brent is bid to $93.92 (+2.51%) against Wednesday's $91.62 settle; WTI's October contract is at $86.55 (+2.56%). [flag: no fresh settle yet, real-time levels only] Mechanism: the UAE has been one of the last Gulf states still moving meaningful non-Iranian-linked volume through Hormuz, with >80% of transits now via the Omani corridor the US Navy escorts.
