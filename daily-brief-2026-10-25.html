Lead

A SECOND NIGHT, STILL NO CORROBORATION

Markets stay shut on Friday's war-high close while Saturday's mine claim goes unanswered, and Hormuz's own risk tracker changes how it counts the evidence

No trading session has intervened since Friday's close, so Friday's levels stand as the last verified prints heading into FOMC week: Brent's $116.72 settle (+3.14%, the highest close of the seven-month war) and WTI's $103.27 (+3.82%), both per oilprice.com and straits.live, which agree on the figures; Treasury's 2-, 10- and 30-year at 4.69%, 5.00% and 5.33%; DXY at 101.60 (-0.05%, investing.com); gold at $4,007.90 (-1.29%, Yahoo Finance). [fact, continuity] Nothing here has moved because nothing has traded — the gap between Friday's close and Monday's open is this issue's real subject.

Into that quiet came Saturday's claim: Iran's IRGC Navy said overnight Friday-into-Saturday that two supertankers were struck and disabled by naval mines in the strait. As of straits.live's own 10:17 UTC Sunday timestamp, neither UKMTO nor any flag state had corroborated it — now well past 24 hours outstanding, and consistent with the pattern this brief has tracked through the week: IRGC mine and strike claims have gone unmatched by a specific UKMTO advisory more often than not. [fact/continuity, flag] Thursday's separate seven-vessel claim, which President Trump branded fabricated, also remains unresolved either way.

Something did move on the Hormuz dashboard overnight, but it's the measurement, not the war. straits.live's page no longer publishes the dark-tanker percentage this brief has carried for weeks (Friday's reading was 72.0%); in its place is a "flagged-vessel screen" showing 25 high-risk and 178 moderate-risk vessels of 1,926 tracked in the Gulf over 24 hours, and the page now marks its own AIS feed stale. [fact, NEW, flag] Read: that is not evidence that evasive shipping behavior improved or worsened over the weekend. It is evidence the tracker changed its own methodology mid-crisis, which means Friday's 72.0% and today's new figures cannot be compared, and this issue is where that particular series' continuity breaks. [read]

The two indices straits.live does keep on a consistent basis moved only fractionally: Crisis Pressure held at 88±10 (Extreme), and the Escalation Forecast rose a point to 62±11 (High), reversing Friday's one-point dip. [fact, continuity] The VLCC war-risk premium this brief has tracked at roughly 32x pre-crisis is now 18 days stale, dated to Oct. 7 — the instrument that would normally reprice fastest on a genuine mine strike hasn't published a new read in nearly three weeks, which says something about how thin actual underwriting activity in the strait has become even as claims continue. [fact/read, flag]

Read: a closed market cannot test a claim the way a trading session can. Friday's selloff already priced in one unconfirmed account; Saturday's claim arrives with no price discovery available to accept or reject it before Monday's Asia open, the first session that will. [read] Watch: whether Tokyo or Hong Kong extend Friday's risk-off tape at Sunday evening ET's open; whether UKMTO or a flag state corroborates or debunks the new mine claim before then; whether PortWatch's Oct. 18 transit count — now seven days stale — updates at all before the Fed meets Tuesday-Wednesday; and whether straits.live restores a dark-tanker comparison or keeps the new flagged-vessel format.
