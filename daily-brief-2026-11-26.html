Lead

NVIDIA / REGULATION

On a dark trading day, the story that moved is regulatory: Commerce quietly left Megaspeed off the Entity List over four agencies' objections, and Nvidia's congressional calendar hardens into three fixed dates

There is no trading today, and nothing in the macro or markets column moved — Thanksgiving closes US equity, bond and commodity markets outright, and the data cluster that was due Wednesday (October PCE, Q3 GDP's second estimate, jobless claims, durable goods, a $44 billion 7-year Treasury auction) could not be confirmed this run despite searches; treat that as a reporting gap, not a non-event, and expect it folded into the next issue once results are indexed. The one thread that did move sits underneath the Nvidia governance story this brief has carried since Huang's Nov. 12 resignation and Nov. 22 reinstatement: the regulatory track against Megaspeed, the Singapore-based firm at the center of the chip-diversion and Alice Huang intelligence-tie allegations.

Issue No. 42 flagged, as unverified, a reported same-day Commerce addition of Megaspeed to the Entity List. That reporting has reversed, not confirmed: per the Financial Times, Commerce has in fact held off on new Entity List additions for roughly two months, and Megaspeed was not among the names added — despite the State, Energy, Treasury and Defense Departments all separately pushing for its inclusion. [FT via secondary aggregation; this brief did not independently reach the FT report or a primary BIS release] Commerce has not added a single company to the Entity List in over a year — by the FT's count, the longest such stretch in 18 years. Separately, Commerce's own investigation into suspected chip smuggling by Megaspeed was reportedly shifted to the Justice Department after contact from Nvidia, which placed Megaspeed on its own internal block list back in June 2025.

Mechanism: this matters for the Dec. 1–3 congressional sequence precisely because it is the thing lawmakers are set to ask about. Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter go before the House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and China Select Committees in transcribed, closed-door interviews Dec. 1 and Dec. 2 respectively — now corroborated by Axios independent of this brief's issue No. 42 sourcing — with Huang's first public appearance, before Senate Banking as the sole witness, following Dec. 3. An administration that spent last weekend persuading Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang on a national-security assurance is now sending him into hearings where the open question is why the agency responsible for designating national-security risk left the company at the center of the scandal off its own blacklist, notwithstanding four other agencies' objections. That is a harder story for the administration to control than a market move, but it is the one piece of this arc that changed in the last 48 hours.

Second-order consequence: expect Sen. Elizabeth Warren's line of questioning — she has already said Monday's reinstatement "does not change the fact" that Nvidia owes Congress answers on export enforcement — to fold the Megaspeed non-listing directly into the Dec. 3 hearing; it hands her a concrete, agency-level inaction to point to rather than only the Huang personnel matter. Nothing here moves NVDA's price today, since markets are shut, but it sets the frame for whatever Friday's thinly-traded half session and next week's reopening bring. This brief will not relitigate the reinstatement itself or Monday's rally — both fully reported in issue No. 42 — and will treat anything on NVDA's actual Tuesday/Wednesday closes as unconfirmed until a fresh print is located.

Watch: whether BIS or Commerce issues any on-the-record response to the FT reporting before Dec. 3; whether the Dec. 1–2 closed-door interviews leak detail ahead of the public hearing; and the first verifiable NVDA close of the week, still outstanding as of this issue's press time.
