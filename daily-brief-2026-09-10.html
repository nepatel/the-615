Lead

THREE FRONTS, ONE 24-HOUR WINDOW

Brent tops $100 for the first time since July as Iran hits a US ally's airbase in Jordan, Houthis strike Saudi refining country, and a Black Sea terminal takes a drone hit — the same 24 hours

Tuesday brought the sharpest single-day widening of the war since it began. US Central Command struck and destroyed or disabled five Iranian crude carriers — M/T Kaviz, Charminar, Horizon 1, Riesco and Derya — after the IRGC tried twice in two days to hit a US Navy warship with ballistic missiles; video of the Riesco sinking circulated within hours. [NEW] [fact] It is the same "tanker for tanker" doctrine Adm. Brad Cooper described after Saturday's smaller exchange, now applied at more than double the scale — two Iranian tankers disabled then, five sunk or disabled now. [fact]

Iran's retaliation landed somewhere new: overnight Tuesday into Wednesday, the IRGC fired 20 ballistic missiles at Jordan's Muwaffaq Salti/Al Azraq air base, the principal host for US air operations in the kingdom. Jordanian and US Patriot PAC-3 batteries intercepted 18; two fell in open areas; no casualties were reported. [NEW] [fact] Iranian state media (Fars) claimed the strike damaged maintenance hangars and F-35/F-16/F-15 dispersal sites; a Trump administration official called the barrage "ineffective against integrated air and missile defences." [fact — claims conflict, neither independently verified this run] It is the first time in this war that Iranian strikes have landed on Jordanian soil rather than at sea or inside Iran itself — a geographic widening, not just an escalation in tanker count. [read]

The same 24-hour window produced two more supply-side hits, on different continents. Iran-aligned Houthi forces fired missiles and drones at energy and economic sites across Saudi Arabia's south — Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jazan and Najran — Tuesday, wounding 73 and forcing a temporary halt to some operations near Jazan's 400,000-bpd refinery, one of the kingdom's largest. [NEW] [fact] And Wednesday morning, drones hit the Caspian Pipeline Consortium's Black Sea export terminal at Novorossiysk, damaging the tanker Seaviolet at mooring three; the vessel completed loading and the mooring itself was reported undamaged. [NEW] [fact, single-sourced to trade press this run] Neither CPC nor Ukraine has claimed responsibility; Russia has previously blamed Ukraine for the terminal's recurring drone strikes. [fact]

[read] The mechanism that matters for a portfolio is not any single strike but the pattern: three geographically distinct pieces of global supply — Iranian Gulf exports, Saudi refining capacity, and Kazakh Black Sea export infrastructure that routes around the Gulf entirely — all took hits inside one day. That is a different shock than the Hormuz chokepoint story the market has been pricing for 194 days, where the redundancy argument was that alternate routes and non-Gulf refining capacity could absorb some of the loss. Wednesday's set of strikes narrows that redundancy on two of its own load-bearing pieces simultaneously. Brent's reaction — a $2.81 jump to a $100.73 settle, its first close above the century mark since July 24 — priced exactly that: not a single flashpoint, but the removal of slack.

Equities and rates moved together in the uncomfortable direction: the Dow fell 405 points (-0.77%) to 52,380.66, the S&P 500 slid 0.48% to 7,636.36, the Nasdaq eased 0.64%, while the 10-year rose to 4.83% and the 30-year to 5.30% — oil and yields both up is the stagflationary cross-current the Board has flagged for weeks, and it now sits directly in front of Thursday's CPI print. [fact] [read] Trump's public line — Iran "will be hit again at a much harder and higher level" if it retaliates further — plus Tehran's Foreign Ministry calling the US strikes "a clear threat to regional and international peace" both argue the tanker-for-tanker exchange is escalating in scale on both sides, not settling into a stable rhythm. That raises the stakes for Thursday's Aug. CPI (cons. +0.4% m/m/3.4% y/y): a hot print arriving on top of $100 oil hands hawks a stronger case heading into the Sept. 15-16 FOMC than the already-contested 49-57% hike odds implied when Brent was trading $20 cheaper.
