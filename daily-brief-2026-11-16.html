Lead

AI TRADE, DOUBLE TEST

Nvidia's first earnings call since Jensen Huang's ouster collides with a reinstatement campaign and Anthropic's record IPO pricing, in the AI trade's highest-stakes 72 hours yet

Two of the AI boom's defining events land within two days of each other this week. Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter results Tuesday after the close, with a 5 p.m. ET call — the company's first earnings since Jensen Huang's Nov. 12 resignation as CEO. Anthropic is separately reported, though not yet confirmed by the company itself, to price its IPO as early as Wednesday and begin trading Thursday on Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT." [fact; flag, Anthropic's exact timetable unconfirmed] Complicating the first event: a campaign, reported Sunday, to bring Huang back.

The Financial Times reported Nov. 15 that Huang is discussing a possible return as CEO with allies who are assembling what one described as a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby Nvidia's board, with plans to seek support from the White House. [fact, FT via multiple outlets] A public "Bring Jensen Back" petition has drawn more than 160,000 signatures, listing Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan among its backers — affiliations the petition itself says are self-reported and unverified. [fact; flag, petition sourcing] The push is not unanimous even among Republicans: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had called for a "dramatic shake-up" of Nvidia's leadership after Huang's exit, a reminder that the administration isn't uniformly behind a comeback. [fact] One Huang ally acknowledged to the FT the central obstacle plainly: "Having an affair with a subordinate is a fireable offence" — a reference to Huang's relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's VP of healthcare and a direct report, on top of the still-unresolved board investigation, run by outside counsel at Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, into his relationship with Alice Huang. [fact, background established issues No. 39–40] Nvidia and Huang's representatives declined to comment to the FT. Arm CEO Rene Haas remains the other name under board consideration for a permanent successor, per earlier FT reporting, with CFO Colette Kress running the company on an interim basis in the meantime. [fact; flag, single-sourced on Haas]

The stakes for Tuesday's call are high on their own terms. Nvidia fell 12.75% for the week ended Friday on the Huang news alone, closing below $4 trillion in market value; Wall Street's consensus revenue and EPS estimates for the quarter were not independently confirmed this run. [fact, prior issue; flag on consensus] A clean beat-and-raise from Kress would test whether investors can keep treating Huang's departure as a Nvidia-specific governance problem rather than a read on AI spending broadly — the framing that held through last week's selloff, when AMD and Broadcom both rallied on rotation. A miss, or fresh reinstatement noise breaking during the call itself, would make that separation much harder to sustain. [read]

The mechanism linking the two events: Anthropic's underwriters are pricing the AI sector's largest-ever IPO into the 24 hours immediately following Nvidia's print, against a valuation that is itself already sliding. Filing math implies up to $1.405 trillion fully diluted at the top of Anthropic's stated $70–78 per-share range (825 million Class A shares, plus a 123.75-million-share overallotment) — but Bloomberg-sourced reporting says investor demand has pulled private talk down to roughly $1.2–1.3 trillion, versus $1.4–1.5 trillion a few weeks ago and roughly $2 trillion as recently as early October. [fact; flag, divergent and still-moving figures, unconfirmed by the company] Nvidia's tone Tuesday — specifically on AI-capex guidance — is the last major data point underwriters get before Anthropic's bankers set a final price Wednesday. [read]

Watch: whether the Huang reinstatement push gains actual board traction before Tuesday's call; whether Nvidia's guidance language addresses the leadership transition directly; and whether Anthropic ultimately prices within, above or below its stated range.

Politics

Kansas's governor's race remains uncalled, with Democrat Cindy Holscher's lead over Republican Ty Masterson still around 7,600 votes — unchanged from issue No. 40 — as mandatory close-race audits continue in six statewide races. [DEVELOPING, no horse-race movement] One procedural detail not previously reported here: under Kansas law, a recount request must be filed by 5 p.m. the day after a county's canvass certifies results, and audits must finish before that certification; a completed recount is due five days after any request is filed. Because certification is staggered by county this cycle, there is no single statewide deadline today. [fact, Kansas Secretary of State/Ballotpedia] Masterson has not clarified whether his Nov. 4 call for "another look" referred to the ongoing audit or a future recount request. Elsewhere on the same audit list, Republican Chris Mann has separately declared victory in the Kansas attorney general's race, which also remains officially uncalled; no new movement was found there this run. [fact; DEVELOPING] Inauguration remains set for Jan. 11, 2027.

Economics

October producer prices, released Friday, undercut consensus on the month but ran hot year-over-year: final-demand PPI rose 0.3% month-over-month (Dow Jones consensus: +0.4%), while the 12-month rate jumped to 6.5% — the fastest annual pace since November 2022. [fact, BLS; resolves the flag carried in issue No. 40] September was revised up to +1.4% from +1.3%; August was trimmed to +0.4% from +0.5%. Food prices rose 1.3% on the month; energy slipped 0.1%. Core measures diverge by definition across sources: core (ex-food/energy) ran +0.3% m/m by one read, while a measure also excluding trade services ran +0.5% m/m and +4.9% y/y by another — not reconciled here. [flag, methodology difference between secondary sources] Part of the year-over-year jump reflects base effects from last year's shutdown-disrupted reporting, the same dynamic flagged in October's CPI two issues ago. Separately, December Fed rate-cut expectations have faded further, which helped push the dollar index to roughly 102.8 by Nov. 11 — its highest level since April 2025 — and tracks with the sharp move higher across the Treasury curve reflected in Friday's 2Y/10Y/30Y levels above. [fact] Retail sales, due tomorrow, are this week's next read on whether the consumer is absorbing the hotter wholesale prints without visibly slowing.

Geopolitics

Russia and Ukraine held their first direct, in-person talks since February last week: two days of US-mediated negotiations in Abu Dhabi, Nov. 10–11, with Putin aide Vladimir Medinsky and Ukraine's Rustem Umerov leading their delegations and US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner mediating. [fact, multi-sourced: NPR, Washington Post, Anadolu] The first day centered on broad European-security framing; the second moved to humanitarian issues, prisoner exchanges and security guarantees, with "major differences" remaining and no agreement announced. [fact] Russia objected to direct European participation in the three-way format but said EU representatives could join at a later stage; Kushner said the talks would set up separate engagement with Europe "in the near future." [fact] Fighting continued on the ground throughout the two days. [fact] Three earlier 2026 rounds produced no breakthrough, which tempers expectations for this one too. [read] Notably, this round is the first since February — an eight-month gap during which the front lines moved but the diplomatic track did not; US officials have offered no public timetable for a follow-up session.

Iran & Hormuz

Iran's deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, said Saturday that Tehran has presented Washington with a "two-day plan" to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and that the next move is now the US's; Iran says it will not restore IAEA access to its nuclear sites until the war is formally ended and a diplomatic understanding is reached. [fact, WANA] There is no US confirmation of receiving or accepting any such plan. This acknowledgment of an active process is a shift from security chief Mohsen Rezaei's Nov. 11 flat denial that talks were underway at all — though Tehran still keeps the strait closed pending its seven conditions either way. [read]

[flag] Separately, Windward-based reporting counted 9 cargo vessels and tankers crossing with AIS transponders off between Nov. 12–13 (5 inbound, 4 outbound).
