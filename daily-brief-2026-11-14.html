Lead

NVIDIA, HEADLESS

Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO after a second affair surfaces, leaving the chip giant leaderless nine days before its highest-stakes earnings call yet

Nvidia's board announced Thursday that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, CEO and a board member, effective immediately, ending a 33-year run at the company he co-founded and turning the AI boom's most-watched earnings call — due Nov. 17 — into a debut for an interim chief instead. [fact, per Nvidia's Nov. 12 Form 8-K] Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while keeping her CFO title; the board named Harvey C. Jones, former CEO and executive chairman of Synopsys, as Nvidia's first-ever independent board chair. The company says it is weighing internal and external candidates for a permanent successor and gave no timetable. Jones: "The Board has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team." Kress: "Our focus remains on serving our customers, partners, developers and shareholders." [fact]

The resignation came nine days after Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation — run by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel at Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz — into Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman and former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer accused of smuggling Nvidia chips into China; US intelligence reportedly assesses her as having passed information to Beijing. Huang admitted the affair, apologized to his family and Nvidia's staff, and the board initially said he would stay on as CEO through the inquiry. [fact, multi-sourced] What changed, by multiple accounts, was a second relationship: the New York Times reported Wednesday night that Huang, 63, had also had a relationship with Kimberly Powell, 48, Nvidia's VP of healthcare and a direct report, citing five sources; the Wall Street Journal reported the revelation "jolted" Nvidia's historically deferential board into preparing an immediate ouster. Neither Nvidia, Huang nor Powell has confirmed the Powell relationship on the record. [fact, single-sourced on the Powell relationship specifically; flag]

The mechanism from here is governance, not product. Nvidia heads into its Nov. 17 print — still, by market cap, the most consequential earnings call tracked in this brief — run by a CFO on an interim basis, with no permanent CEO named, an unresolved espionage-adjacent board inquiry, and a congressional briefing request (three House committee chairs, predating the resignation) whose status is now unclear. [fact, background from issue No. 39; DEVELOPING] The second-order read: a stock that is a primary input into this year's broader AI-capex trade now carries a leadership vacuum through its most-watched print of the cycle, in the same five days that Cisco, Applied Materials and CoreWeave all beat estimates and raised guidance (see Business) — an unambiguously bullish demand signal arriving alongside an unambiguously messy governance story. [read]

Markets split the difference rather than resolve it. Nvidia fell roughly 4.3% Thursday to close near $166, then fell further Friday — down about 2% on the day and 12.75% for the week, per one market wrap. [fact; flag, single-sourced on the weekly figure] The S&P 500 fell only 0.35% Thursday and 0.40% Friday; decliners outnumbered advancers roughly 3-to-1 Thursday, but AMD and Broadcom both rallied as money rotated to Nvidia's chip peers rather than leaving the sector outright. [fact] That the broad market shrugged while the single stock most identified with the AI trade cratered is itself informative: Wall Street is treating this as an idiosyncratic Nvidia governance problem rather than a verdict on AI spending — a read Tuesday's print will test directly. [read]

Watch: whether Nvidia names a permanent CEO before Nov. 17; whether Huang's congressional briefing happens on any date now that he is no longer CEO; and whether the board's investigation findings become public before, during or after the earnings call.

Economics

October CPI, released Tuesday, ran hotter than consensus: headline +0.4% month-over-month, seasonally adjusted (vs. roughly +0.3% expected) and core (ex-food-and-energy) +0.3% (vs. +0.2% expected) — the same core pace as August and September. [fact, BLS] Shelter (+0.3%) drove over 30% of the headline gain; energy rebounded 0.7% after September's 5.2% jump, led by gasoline (+1.0%); food rose 0.5%, recreation 0.8%, motor-vehicle insurance 0.7%, partly offset by used cars and trucks (-1.2%). BLS again withheld the year-over-year all-items rate because the 2025 shutdown left no October 2025 base for comparison; September's y/y rate was 3.7%. [fact, BLS] Stocks sold off on the print Tuesday (S&P 500 -0.4%) before rebounding sharply Wednesday on renewed AI-stock buying — a rebound Thursday's Huang news then interrupted (see Lead). October PPI was due Friday per BLS's own schedule, with a Dow Jones consensus calling for +0.4%; no actual October PPI print was confirmed this run despite the scheduled release date — flagged for next issue.
