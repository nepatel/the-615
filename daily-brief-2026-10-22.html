Lead

HORMUZ / MARKETS

Iran claims its biggest wave of Hormuz hits yet; oil jumps, stocks sell off worldwide into a hawkish Fed week

Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said Thursday that its forces hit seven vessels across the Persian Gulf in the prior 24 hours — the largest single-day tally Tehran has claimed since the crisis began in February. Four vessels, which the IRGC said were using a route it considers unauthorized for ship-to-ship oil transfers, were struck simultaneously in the north-central Gulf after being tracked overnight and were left burning; three more, west of the Strait of Hormuz, reportedly hit naval mines and were disabled. [fact, NEW, IRGC via Reuters] Tehran supplied no vessel names, flags, casualty figures or independently verifiable evidence for any of the seven, and the claim should be read as Iranian wartime messaging, not a confirmed incident count. [fact/flag] The US military separately disputed the mine portion of the claim specifically, saying shipping lanes have been cleared and that missiles and drones, not mines, are responsible for the attacks on commercial vessels it has tracked. [fact, NEW] What UKMTO itself confirms is narrower: a tanker hit by an unidentified projectile 30 nautical miles southeast of Iraq's Al Faw on Tuesday evening, with no injuries or damage reported by its master. [fact, NEW] Tehran, for its part, warned Washington that continued attempts to move vessels through routes it deems prohibited could draw attacks on US military bases across the region — a wider-response threat this brief has not seen stated so explicitly before. [fact/flag, NEW]

Markets reacted to the claim, not to any independent confirmation of it, and reacted hard. Brent and WTI both jumped more than 2% intraday, with Yahoo's BZ=F near $111.62 and straits.live's own feed near $111.95 as of this morning — the two quote sources this brief tracks now agree on direction after yesterday's split, resolving that flag from issue No. 27, even as they still differ on magnitude. [fact] Asia closed broadly lower: the Nikkei down 2.40% to 63,070.00, the Hang Seng down 3.03% to 22,934.68, the Shanghai Composite down 1.48% to 3,658.03, with China's CSI 300 separately reported 20% below its June peak — a bear-market threshold for that benchmark. [fact] Europe was following lower at midday, and US index futures pointed to a rough open: S&P futures down 1.03%, Nasdaq 100 futures down 1.74%, with the VIX up nearly 11% to 18.50. [fact] Bitcoin gave up a push above $84,000 and was retreating toward $82,000 in the same window — a cross-asset risk-off move, not an oil-complex-only one. [fact/read] One genuine oddity: gold fell 1.15% to $4,102.37 even as everything else around it sold off, the kind of mixed signal this brief flags rather than smooths over; it may reflect dollar positioning or margin-driven liquidation across correlated longs rather than any read on the Hormuz claim itself, but no single source confirms either explanation. [read/flag]

The selloff wasn't only about Hormuz. Coindesk and other outlets tied part of Asia's tech-sector weakness to mixed US earnings unsettling AI-spending expectations: Tesla fell roughly 2%-3% after hours Wednesday despite beating on adjusted EPS ($0.48 vs. ~$0.44-0.45 consensus), after cutting its 2026 capital-spending guidance to roughly $20 billion and reporting operating income down 56% to $718 million, while Meta moved the other way, raising the low end of its own spending guidance the same week. [fact, NEW] That divergence — one AI-capex bellwether pulling back as another leans in — is itself feeding the market's unease about whether the spending boom is being underwritten by demand or by momentum, a question this brief will keep separate from the Hormuz-driven oil move rather than conflating the two. [read]

The timing compounds the stakes for next week. The FOMC meets Oct. 27-28 with cut odds already thin — 15%-24% across the venues this brief tracks, unchanged from issue No. 27 — and a committee that held 9-3 in July with all three dissents arguing for a hike, not a cut. [fact/flag, continuity + NEW context] A crude spike that holds into the October inflation-reference period would hand that committee a hotter energy pass-through just as it weighs easing, reinforcing rather than undercutting the thin-odds read. [read] Thursday's 8:30a ET jobless claims print is now the last clean domestic data point before the meeting; PCE and the Q3 GDP advance estimate both land Oct. 29, the day after the decision — a date this brief can newly confirm, closing the gap flagged in issue No. 27. [fact, NEW] The claim also lands eleven days before midterms and ten days before OPEC+'s Nov. 1 meeting, which already held November output flat at 31.01m bpd on Oct. 4; a sustained spike into that meeting raises the political cost of holding output steady again, though no OPEC+ delegate has signaled a change in response to this week's claims. [read] This brief is deliberately not re-running the approval and generic-ballot numbers carried in issue No. 27 — nothing in them moved overnight — but the broader point stands: a wartime escalation claim landing this close to an election, on a day the market is already pricing in an energy-driven inflation risk, raises the bar for whatever the administration says next, whether or not Thursday's attacks are ever independently confirmed. [read] Watch: whether any government, carrier or insurer independently corroborates any of Iran's seven claimed hits; whether Brent holds above $110 through the NY open and settle; Thursday's claims number as the next read on how much room the Fed actually has; and whether OPEC+ signals anything before Nov. 1.
