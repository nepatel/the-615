Lead

AI SELLOFF

The S&P 500 and Dow fall into correction, the Nasdaq 100 posts its worst week since 2020 — then futures rip back Monday morning

Wall Street's AI-led unwind hit its sharpest point Friday: the S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, down 8.6% on the week in what multiple outlets now call a correction, roughly 12% below October's high. [fact] The Nasdaq 100 fell 5.61% to 25,028.98, down 10.4% on the week — its worst week since March 2020 — while the Dow fell 1.93% to 48,151.82. [fact] The VIX jumped 26% to 35.89, its sharpest single-day move of the selloff. [fact] This was a forced-deleveraging week, not a classic flight to safety: gold itself fell 2.5% Friday to $3,532, down roughly 10% on the week, selling off alongside equities rather than hedging them. [fact; read on characterization]

Mechanism: Nvidia was the proximate trigger. Shares had already fallen about 7% Friday on broader AI-spending doubts when Reuters reported, citing two people familiar with the matter, that SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at the company; the stock closed down 14.01% at $125.53, capping a 23% weekly decline — its worst week since the 2008 financial crisis, per CNBC. [fact] Reuters could not determine when the inquiry began or specify which practices are under review; the SEC declined to comment and Nvidia did not respond to Reuters' requests. [fact] The news landed three days after board chair Harvey Jones told investors on the earnings call that ousted CEO Jensen Huang will not be reinstated, and two days after Nvidia's own Q3 beat (see issue No. 41) — so a governance crisis, an accounting inquiry and a still-strong print are now all being priced at once. [fact/read]

The selloff wasn't confined to Nvidia. Advanced Micro Devices, Micron Technology and Broadcom each fell roughly 12%–14% Friday as the chip complex repriced together, and coverage from NPR and others framed the week as "loud alarms" over whether AI capital spending — much of it debt-financed — can generate matching returns, with multiple outlets drawing dot-com comparisons. [read] One aggregated tally put Nvidia's loss since October's high at roughly $2.7 trillion in market value; this brief treats that figure as directional, not precise, given its single secondary source. [flag, aggregator]

Second-order consequence: Anthropic's debut rally also faded into the selloff. One tally put ANT down 8.5% Friday to $88.91, still above its $83 IPO price; AP reported a steeper 10.7% drop — sources disagree, but either way the stock gave back most of Thursday's 17% pop (see issue No. 41). [fact; flag, divergent] Some coverage ties the broader AI-doubt wave to the recent midterms, where candidates favoring a data-center-buildout slowdown won at both federal and state level — a read, not a mechanism this brief can quantify. [read] Treasuries rallied even as stocks fell — the 10-year dropped more than 10bp to 4.94%, the 2-year below 4.65% — yet fed funds futures still price a December hike as near-certain (92%–98%, sources differ), because October's hot PPI print (see issue No. 41) hasn't been offset by anything since. [fact; flag, divergent] Treasury Secretary Bessent warned Sunday that the Fed's hawkish rhetoric itself could hurt the economy, a tension between policy stance and market stress that now runs straight into Wednesday's PCE print. [fact/read]

Monday's bounce, so far confined to futures: S&P 500 futures were up 2.2% and Nasdaq-100 futures up somewhere between 2.8% and 3.6% depending on source and timestamp as of roughly 5a ET; Taiwan's benchmark, coming off its worst week since 1991, rallied 2.2%, while South Korea's Kospi was still down 2.6% on continued caution. [fact; flag, ranges] Regional authorities moved to stabilize: South Korea discussed a market-stabilization fund, Taiwan tightened short-selling rules, and China eased margin financing — official concern about contagion, not just a dip buyer's bounce. [fact]

Watch: whether the bounce survives the 9:30a cash open or fades the way Thursday's Anthropic pop did; whether Wednesday's PCE print or the Nov. 29 OPEC+ ministerial reignites selling if either disappoints; and whether Nvidia's accounting disclosure deepens the bear case or fades the way its 2002–03 SEC inquiry eventually did, with a cease-and-desist and no penalty. This brief does not guess which.

Economics

Walmart's fiscal Q3 (quarter ended Oct. 31, reported Thu. 11/19) beat: revenue $188.8B, up 5.2%, and adjusted EPS $0.65 against a $0.64 consensus; US comparable sales ex-fuel rose 2.9%, and global e-commerce rose 17%. [fact] Walmart raised its FY27 outlook — net sales now seen up 4.9%–5.2%, adjusted EPS $2.84–$2.89, from $2.80–$2.87 previously — the clearest pre-selloff read on holiday-season consumer demand this brief has; sources disagreed on the stock's own reaction that morning, so that figure isn't repeated here. [fact; flag on stock reaction] The quarter covers the run-up to Black Friday, not the holiday weekend itself; Walmart's next readout of actual holiday demand won't arrive until its Q4 report in February. Management flagged a new $120 combined Walmart+/Sam's Club Plus membership weighing on Sam's Club operating income through fiscal 2028's third quarter, and warned elevated diesel prices could prolong freight-cost pressure. [fact] Wednesday's October PCE print (see Board) now carries the added weight of confirming or denying the hawkish repricing described in the Lead; if Oxford Economics' 3.8% y/y nowcast holds, it would be the hottest headline PCE reading cited in recent issues, forcing the Fed to weigh that against the equity-market stress now visible on the Board. Geopolitics

Trump held back-to-back sit-downs with Putin (~40 minutes) and Xi (~1 hour) in Shenzhen on the sidelines of APEC Nov. 19 — a trilateral format not attempted before in this war, with Ukraine, Iran and nuclear disarmament on the agenda. [fact; NEW] The Kremlin's own account put Ukraine at the center of the Putin meeting; Trump said Putin is ready for a deal, while Putin reportedly told him talks "weren't moving fast enough." [read, attributed] No joint statement on Ukraine followed — one analyst had predicted as much beforehand, reasoning it would leave Beijing more exposed to accountability for the war's continuation — and Trump declined to say whether he would separately meet Zelensky. [fact/read] Separately, Zelensky said Monday that Ukrainian forces sank a Russian warship docked at a naval base in Benghazi, Libya, and disabled a cargo vessel off the Libyan coast, hours, he said, after a suspected Ukrainian drone struck a different Russian cargo ship roughly 2,000 miles away in the Baltic Sea. [fact; NEW, single-sourced to Ukraine's own account; Russia had not commented at check time] Ukraine's air force said it downed 161 of 186 Russian drones launched since the evening of Nov. 21; Ukrinform reported 131 frontline clashes over the same period, concentrated around Pokrovsk. [fact; flag, Ukrainian-sourced] Chinese state media described the Shenzhen summit as having "groundbreaking significance" despite both individual sit-downs running under an hour; a White House official had said beforehand that Trump would raise the US-Israeli war against Iran with Xi but was not expected to raise Ukraine at all, a framing the eventual agenda appears to have gone beyond. [fact/read] Reuters separately carried a headline-only claim that Ukrainian strikes have cut Russia's oil-refining capacity by roughly half over three weeks; this brief could not corroborate the figure further and does not repeat it as confirmed. [flag, headline-only, unconfirmed] No standalone China/Taiwan development beyond the summit itself cleared this brief's sourcing bar this run. For markets, the summit's lack of a Ukraine breakthrough removes one potential catalyst for a broader risk rally, leaving Wednesday's PCE print and the Nov. 29 OPEC+ ministerial as the nearer-term events to watch instead.
