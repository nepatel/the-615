Lead

MARKETS / FED DAY — A CORRECTION

The Fed's chair is Kevin Warsh, not Powell, and the base case today is a hold at 3.75%–4.00% — a correction, landing on the day it matters most

Start with a correction. The Federal Reserve's chair is Kevin Warsh, not Jerome Powell — the Senate confirmed him 54-45 on May 13, 2026, and he took the gavel May 15; Powell remains a Fed governor, a term that runs to 2028, but hasn't chaired a meeting in over five months. [fact] The policy rate itself has moved too: the FOMC voted 12-0 on Sept. 16 to raise the target range a quarter point, to 3.75%–4.00% — the first hike since July 2023. [fact] Yesterday's issue of this brief (No. 31) referred to "Powell" and to a target "3.50–3.75%, unchanged since June 17" — both wrong, and both fixed as of this issue. This brief's own archive tracked the hike and the chair change correctly as recently as Sept. 24; the error crept back in once Fed coverage lapsed for a month between meetings. Nothing else in prior issues is affected. [fact, correction]

Today is Warsh's second rate decision since that hike — the FOMC's two-day meeting concludes with the decision at 2p ET, followed by his press conference at 2:30p; there's no Summary of Economic Projections at this meeting, so no fresh dot plot. [fact] A hold is priced as close to a lock: a Kalshi-linked prediction market shows roughly 95% odds of no change, 6% of a hike, 1% of a cut; Manifold's independent market also shows cut odds at 1%. CME FedWatch, the standard reference, again returned a server error at press time, so this brief is using the same prediction-market proxies as yesterday. [fact/flag] The mechanism worth sitting with: September's hike wasn't a one-off the committee is eager to reverse. Minutes released Oct. 7 said "most participants" judged a further increase "would likely be appropriate by year-end" — a hold today is a pause inside an active hiking cycle, not a pivot toward easing. [fact/read] Watch whether Warsh's 2:30p remarks reinforce or soften that December signal.

Crude, meanwhile, eased rather than extended Monday's shrug at fresh Hormuz incident claims: Brent trades at $114.58 and WTI at $101.16 this morning, both below yesterday's already-soft readings, even as Iran's army reframed Monday's tanker-fire incident as an alleged mine strike and threatened the vessel's owner, Sinokor, with legal action. [fact, DEVELOPING] If a resurgent energy-driven inflation shock were building, it isn't showing up in the tape — one of the arguments for a hawkish surprise today looks weaker this morning than it did Monday. [read]

Into this lands the week's two biggest prints: Microsoft and Alphabet report after today's close, Amazon follows Thursday — both within about a day of the Fed decision, and a day ahead of Thursday's Q3 GDP advance estimate and September PCE print. [fact] Consensus figures for all three aren't cleanly sourced this run: trackers disagree widely enough — Microsoft's EPS estimate alone ranges roughly $4.71 to $4.82 depending on source — that this brief isn't printing a hard number for any of them. [flag] The second-order read: a Fed that holds without signaling an end to its hiking cycle, landing hours before two mega-cap prints the market is already pricing for strength, leaves little room for either name to miss quietly. Watch: 2p ET for the decision, 2:30p for Warsh, and after-hours tonight and tomorrow for Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon. [read]
