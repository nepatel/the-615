Lead

MARKETS

Wall Street's AI trade cracks — S&P and Dow close in correction, Nasdaq erases its 2026 gain

Friday was the worst single session of the AI-led unwind that has been building since Nvidia's governance crisis broke open two weeks ago: the S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, its seventh consecutive daily loss and, per multiple wire reports, more than 10% below its October 6 record close of 7,818.93 — the conventional threshold for a market correction. The Dow Jones Industrial Average gave up 949.73 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33, reversing an early-session gain. The Nasdaq Composite was hit hardest, down 5.28% to 22,617.73 — a decline that erased the index's entire 2026 advance, per AP and NPR's reporting of the session. For the week, the S&P 500 lost 8.6% and the Nasdaq 10.0%, the worst weekly showing for both in well over a year by AP's count; the small-cap Russell 2000 fell 7.5%.

Mechanism: the selling was concentrated almost entirely in the chip-and-AI-infrastructure complex that has carried the broader market's gains all year. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) sank 10.7%; Nvidia alone fell on the order of 13-14% by Friday's close, according to Forbes and TradingKey's respective reporting, pushing its decline since Jensen Huang's Nov. 12 resignation past 10% and compounding a stock already down roughly 28% over the trailing month per issue No. 41 [flag, two outlets differ slightly]. Anthropic's ANT, which had closed its Thursday debut at $97.17 (+17% from its $83 IPO price, see issue No. 41), gave back most of that pop — Breitbart reported the stock fell 9.1% on just its second session — making the newest and most-watched AI listing on the tape a visible casualty of the same unwind it had arrived into. Corning and Bloom Energy, both proxies for AI-linked data-center buildout rather than chips directly, were cited by multiple outlets as also falling sharply, evidence the move is being read as a capex-cycle story, not a semiconductor-specific one.

Layered onto the broader AI-capex-skepticism narrative that has been building since mid-November is a new, Nvidia-specific overhang: the Wall Street Journal reported Nov. 17, citing people familiar with the matter, that the SEC has opened a civil investigation into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors — a probe whose scope was not specified but which sources told the Journal touches questions connected to the Huang-Megaspeed matter already the subject of December's House testimony (see Board) [flag, single primary source]. Nvidia has not been accused of wrongdoing and an open SEC inquiry does not imply one will be found. Separately, Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each fell more than 5% in Friday's own session, per AP, confirming the selloff's transmission beyond US markets even before Wall Street opened.

Second-order consequence: this is now the second major data point in nine days on where the market is resetting AI-sector expectations — first Anthropic's IPO priced a $1.5 trillion valuation on growth alone (issue No. 41), and now the broader complex is being asked whether that growth justifies current capital intensity. The two aren't unrelated: Anthropic's own $26 billion private placement and nearly $1 trillion in disclosed infrastructure commitments through 2045 (issue No. 41) are underwritten by the same hyperscaler balance sheets — Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, AMD, Broadcom — now absorbing the equity-market repricing directly. None of the wire coverage this brief checked called the move a bubble bursting outright; several, per aggregated Wall Street commentary, characterized it as a valuation adjustment within an intact bull market rather than a reversal of the underlying AI-spending thesis.

Watch: whether Monday's Asia and European opens extend Friday's losses or stabilize; whether the SEC inquiry produces further disclosure from Nvidia ahead of Dec. 1's House testimony; and whether Walmart's confirmed Thursday beat-and-raise (see Business) gives the market a non-AI anchor for Monday, or gets overwhelmed by the same selling.

Business

Walmart's fiscal Q3 results, reported Thursday, confirmed the beat flagged as pending in issue No. 41: revenue of $188.8 billion, up 5.2% year-over-year and above the $187.4 billion FactSet consensus; adjusted EPS of $0.65 against a $0.64 estimate (GAAP EPS of $0.62). US e-commerce sales rose 23% and global e-commerce 17%, though Flipkart's sale-timing shift weighed on the growth rate by roughly 500 basis points, with an offsetting boost expected in Q4. Walmart raised full-year guidance — net sales growth of 4.9-5.2% and adjusted EPS of $2.84-$2.89, up from $2.80-$2.87 — giving this brief's best read on Main Street holiday demand a firmer footing than the market's AI-led turmoil (see Lead) would otherwise suggest. Shares were reported up modestly on the print before Friday's broader selloff overtook the tape [flag, pre-selloff reaction].
