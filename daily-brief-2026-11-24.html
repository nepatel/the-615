Lead

GOVERNANCE, REVERSED

Nvidia's board reverses itself in six days, reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO with White House input, and Wall Street stages its biggest rally since April 2025

Nvidia's board voted unanimously Sunday to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director, effective immediately upon Monday's announcement — six days after chairman Harvey Jones told investors the board did not intend to bring him back. The board said it received input from "senior levels of the United States government" and is now confident the move poses no national-security risk; President Trump told CNBC's Eamon Javers "I convinced them. I made the deal," and that he personally promised the board Huang had not broken national-security law — "they did a pretty good investigation, and they felt that he, himself, was exonerated." Huang had resigned Nov. 12 after reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a Nvidia-linked executive suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset; CFO Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, returns to the CFO role, and Jones remains chairman. An independent-director committee with outside counsel continues investigating the foreign-intelligence allegations "to conclusion," and a separate National Security and Export Control Committee is standing up with its own counsel.

Mechanism: Nvidia shares closed up 31.48% to $165.05 Monday — options volume hit a reported record above 11 million contracts — and dragged the broader market with it: the S&P 500 posted its best day since April 9, 2025 (+4.73% to 7,237.17), the Nasdaq Composite its best since the same date (+6.51% to 24,089.56), and the Dow gained 1,447.98 points (+3.01%) to 49,595.31. That reversed, in one session, most of a correction that had put the S&P below 7,000 at Friday's close for the first time since mid-April; chip and AI-infrastructure names led, with Micron up 19%, AMD near 7%, and CoreWeave up 22%, and the S&P's tech sector alone rising 9%. Premarket Tuesday is already giving some of it back — Nvidia down more than 4% near 5 a.m. ET, Nasdaq-100 futures off 0.6% — and Asia split sharply overnight: South Korea's Kospi jumped 6.32% on the chip-rally spillover while Japan's Nikkei fell 3.91% and Australia's ASX 200 dropped 1.82%, suggesting Monday's move is being read locally rather than as settled global repricing.

Second-order consequence: a sitting president's account of personally brokering a public company's CEO reinstatement, on the strength of a private national-security assurance to its board, is a governance event independent of Nvidia's fundamentals — Columbia Law's Dorothy Lund flagged exactly this in board-reversal terms, and Sen. Elizabeth Warren said the reinstatement "does not change the fact" that Nvidia still owes Congress answers on export enforcement. Tech investors Elon Musk, Gavin Baker and Brad Gerstner had lobbied publicly for Huang's return; Musk posted that the board's "mistake has been corrected." The reversal also lands the same day Commerce reportedly added Megaspeed — the Singapore-based firm at the center of the China chip-diversion probe and Alice Huang's alleged intelligence ties — to its trade Entity List, after Treasury Secretary Bessent had said on Nov. 13 that Commerce alone was slow-walking that listing; this brief did not locate a primary BIS release confirming Monday's addition, so treat it as reported, not verified.

The sequence behind the reversal, per CNBC's reporting of people familiar with the board's thinking: senior administration officials contacted directors overnight Friday to say Huang had not violated national-security law; the board then brought Huang into its own deliberations over the weekend, he joined board meetings Saturday and Sunday, the vote came Sunday, and Trump spoke to Huang personally after it — telling him, in Trump's own account, "I ask you to do one thing, Jensen. Work hard." Nvidia's stock had fallen roughly 25% in the week before the reversal, and some investors and employees had begun openly questioning Kress's leadership — context that cuts against reading Monday's reinstatement as a pure governance correction rather than, in part, a response to the share-price pressure itself.

Watch: whether Tuesday's premarket giveback extends into a second-day fade or Monday's move holds; Huang's now three-stage Congressional calendar (Dec. 1 House, Dec. 2 Teter, Dec. 3 Senate Banking, public); and whether the independent committee's investigation — explicitly "to conclusion" — produces findings that reopen the reinstatement question before Huang testifies.
