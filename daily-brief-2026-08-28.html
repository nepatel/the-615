Lead

THE AI TRADE STARTS DISCRIMINATING

Marvell's beat-and-raise gets sold anyway — margin mix, not growth, is now what moves these stocks

Marvell Technology reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of $2.739 billion Thursday after the close, a record, up 37% year-over-year and above the roughly $2.71-2.72 billion consensus. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.94 topped the $0.93 estimate; GAAP EPS was $0.33 on GAAP gross margin of 53.1% (58.9% non-GAAP). Data Center revenue reached $2.171 billion, up 46% year-over-year and now 79% of total sales, up 18% sequentially. The company guided third-quarter revenue to $3.15 billion, plus or minus 5% — above the roughly $3 billion figure CEO Matt Murphy had flagged a full quarter early — with non-GAAP EPS of $1.10, plus or minus a nickel. [NEW]

None of that stopped the stock from falling as much as 8.4% in after-hours and premarket trading, dragging Nasdaq 100 futures down roughly 0.3% into Friday's open even as S&P and Dow futures held closer to flat. The mechanism: alongside the beat, Marvell guided third-quarter non-GAAP gross margin to 57.5-58.5%, below the 58.9% just reported. CFO Dan Durn told analysts the sequential dip is being driven by custom-ASIC programs — chips built to hyperscaler specification for AI accelerators — ramping faster than the company's off-the-shelf optical and switching lines, which carry fatter margins. [fact] Rubrik, the backup-and-recovery software company, fell a similar 9% overnight on its own beat-and-raise: EPS of $0.22 against a four-cent estimate, revenue of $427.3 million against $396.3 million consensus, both blown past, followed by an almost identical sell-off. [read: two beat-and-raises punished the same night, on the same mechanism — expensive growth stocks that had run hard into the print (Marvell +187% year-to-date, Rubrik +85% over six months) getting sold on any imperfection in the mix rather than the headline number, is a different market behavior than two days earlier, when Nvidia's own beat-and-raise was rewarded outright.]

That contrast with Wednesday matters more than either print alone. Nvidia's Q3 guide beat consensus by nearly $4 billion and traded up 6% because nothing in the print complicated the growth story; Marvell's guide beat by a comparable margin and traded down because the mix did. [read: the AI-capex trade is no longer being priced as one undifferentiated basket — investors are starting to discriminate between hardware makers whose growth comes with margin dilution (Marvell's custom-silicon ramp, priced at hyperscaler-negotiated rates) and those whose growth doesn't (Nvidia's merchant-GPU pricing power). That's a more selective phase of the trade than the "any AI beat gets bought" pattern that held through most of August, and it complicates the setup for whichever comes next.] Marvell's customer book is also more concentrated than Nvidia's: its custom-ASIC and optical-interconnect revenue leans on a small set of hyperscalers, chiefly Google and Amazon, and Thursday's sell-side commentary specifically cited uncertainty over the timing of incremental Google-related revenue as a factor in the reaction. [read: concentration risk Nvidia's broader hyperscaler base doesn't carry as acutely.]

The result lands the futures tape in a more unsettled place than Wednesday's Nvidia-driven exuberance suggested three days ago — Nasdaq 100 futures red, S&P and Dow closer to flat — just as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh prepares to deliver his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair at 10:00 a.m. ET. [fact] A CNBC survey found 45% of respondents don't expect Warsh to expand on the rate outlook at all, and Fed officials remain publicly divided between a resilient growth tape and inflation still running above target. [read: Warsh now speaks into a market already discriminating within its highest-conviction trade rather than uniformly euphoric — a tougher room to move with a "blank piece of paper," his own description of the speech, than the one he'd have faced Wednesday afternoon.]

[DEVELOPING: basis specified] Lowell's letter: "Governor Cook has never committed mortgage fraud or any intentional wrongdoing, and there is no legally cognizable cause for removing her from the Federal Reserve Board." No White House response as of press time. [fact] The dispute follows the Supreme Court's June 5-4 ruling blocking Trump from removing Fed governors at will absent cause — a threshold the White House is now trying to clear formally on the FHFA referral rather than test again on an emergency docket. [read] The timing is awkward for the administration on its own terms: a fresh Fed-independence fight lands the same morning as Warsh's inaugural Jackson Hole address, and any renewed removal attempt would put a second contested governor-count question in front of markets just as the new chair is trying to establish credibility on rate policy independent of the White House that appointed him. [read] A successful removal before the Sept. 16 FOMC decision would be unprecedented in the Fed's modern history — no sitting governor has previously been excluded from a rate vote by White House action rather than resignation or term expiration — which is the underlying reason the Supreme Court treated the June case as consequential enough to take up on the merits rather than resolve on the emergency docket alone. Geopolitics

Russia launched a "massive" overnight barrage into Thursday across nine Ukrainian cities — 258 attack drones plus Oniks anti-ship and Iskander-M/KN-23 ballistic missiles, of which Ukraine's air force said it downed or suppressed 233 — a sharp escalation from last issue's 162-drone count two days earlier. [NEW] Falling debris and fires injured four in Kyiv; a daytime strike hit a Kharkiv shopping center and a residential building; the barrage killed one person in Zaporizhzhia and one in Kharkiv region, with at least 14 more wounded elsewhere. [fact] Ukraine's own campaign continued in parallel: a drone strike hit the Ryazan refinery (17M tonnes/year capacity, producing petrol, diesel and aviation fuel) — one of the main fuel arteries into Moscow — Thursday, part of a run of more than a dozen Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil infrastructure since Aug. 2, concentrated in Ryazan and Volgograd oblasts. [DEVELOPING] Zelenskyy, on the broader campaign: "Russia must feel that every day of this war will only come at a higher cost for it." Iran & Hormuz

Qatar's Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani met Iranian FM Araghchi in Tehran Thursday — the first high-level Qatari visit to Tehran since the war began Feb. 28 — to discuss reviving U.S.-Iran dialogue and a proposed phased Hormuz plan under negotiation with Oman: a temporary shipping corridor, a joint mine-clearance project, and a proposed joint coordination center to manage maritime traffic and collect vessel information — components not previously detailed in this brief. [DEVELOPING] The open question, per regional coverage, is whether the IRGC accepts reduced operational control under any such arrangement. [read] No agreement was announced. The visit came as UKMTO published (8/27, incident logged 8/25) a second unattributed strike this week — an unnamed tanker off Khasab, Oman, hit by an unknown projectile, fire extinguished, crew safe — four days after the still-unattributed Metro Venetian strike (see Board). [NEW] Separately, the U.S. Navy confirmed the USS Theodore Roosevelt strike group will depart San Diego in the coming weeks for a 7-plus-month Middle East rotation, relieving the George Washington in the Arabian Sea by mid-to-late September — a long-horizon commitment that reads against any near-term reopening. [read] Treasury's "economic D-Day" sanctions campaign (announced Monday, covered last issue) continues; Bessent has framed it as giving trading partners time to comply before harsher measures hit, targeting shipping, oil, crypto, gold and aviation networks routed through the UAE, Hong Kong, China, Singapore and Switzerland — scope detail not previously logged. [fact] Tehran has said it will respond "in a seismic manner," with no specifics provided yet.
