Lead

MOU EXPIRES, TRUMP THREATENS OMAN

US-Iran truce deadline lapses as UAE says it intercepted two Iranian missiles — and the 30-year Treasury hits a 19-year high hours before FOMC minutes

The 60-day window Washington and Tehran gave themselves in June's Islamabad-brokered memorandum of understanding to reach a "final deal" expired Monday with no agreement. [NEW] Asked if he'd extend it, Trump told reporters "No," said he was in no rush, and separately claimed his administration has established a direct backchannel to Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps — a claim the IRGC's spokesman, Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohbehi, called "delusions and nightmares...caused...by defeat and desperation in the war." Iran's foreign ministry piled on a second denial, with spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei separately rejecting reports of unofficial U.S.-IRGC contact via Iraqi Kurdistan officials and an alleged secret meeting in Erbil. [NEW] In the same Fox News interview, Trump turned on Oman, the mediator that brokered the still-unfinished shipping-lane technical deal with Iran: "If Oman gets in the way, we'll bomb the s--- out of them." [NEW] It's the second time this year Trump has threatened a Gulf state with no history of conflict with Washington and a record of quietly freeing detained Americans; Oman's government issued no public response, though Omani social-media commentators pushed back openly. [read: singling out the mediator, not Tehran, suggests the White House dislikes the substance of the emerging Iran-Oman route — a fee-free transitional period with inbound traffic in a northern lane through Iranian waters and outbound in a southern lane through Omani waters, coordinated via a proposed joint center — more than it's willing to say directly]

The deadlock escalated further Tuesday evening: UAE air defenses detected and intercepted two ballistic missiles fired from Iran toward the country, the first falling outside territorial waters and the second within them; an emergency alert went out across several emirates around 6:52p local before being lifted ten minutes later, with no casualties reported. [NEW] It's the first direct Iran-on-UAE missile launch acknowledged in months, a step beyond the ADNOC tanker strikes of the past week (see Iran & Hormuz).

Markets are treating the deadlock as durable, not headline, risk. Brent is bid to $91.78 (+0.99%) this morning, a fourth straight session of gains and roughly 27.5% above the pre-crisis ~$72 benchmark. [NEW] That's compounding a separate stress that was already building: the 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.33% Tuesday, a 19-year high, on twin worries — a $432.3B July fiscal deficit, the highest monthly total since March 2021 with a fiscal-year-to-date shortfall near $1.8T, and inflation that isn't cooperating just as oil turns higher again. [NEW] That's the backdrop for today's 20-year bond auction and the 2p ET release of FOMC July minutes, the first look at how explicitly the meeting's three hawkish dissenters — Cleveland's Hammack, Minneapolis's Kashkari, and Dallas's Logan, the most divided vote since September 2016 — argued for a hike the committee didn't take. [DEVELOPING — see Economics]

Equities and Asia are pricing the yield spike more than the Hormuz story directly, though the two are now feeding each other. The S&P 500 fell 0.7% Tuesday to 7,691.76, a third straight loss since last Thursday's record close, on a chipmaker selloff tied to rate-sensitive AI-infrastructure names — Nvidia fell 2.3% on 102M shares traded, AMD 4.3%. Futures are down another 0.18%/0.37% (S&P/Nasdaq-100) this morning. [NEW] Asia caught it harder: South Korea's KOSPI plunged 5.8% to 6,471.17 on volume of 302M shares worth roughly $16.4B, triggering a sell-side circuit breaker within minutes of the open as Samsung and SK Hynix fell 7.5% and 9.9% on chip-sector contagion, and Kioxia dropped double digits; the Nikkei fell 2.6% to 65,703.78. [NEW] [read: the mechanism running through both stories is the same one — higher-for-longer real yields are repricing the market's most capital-intensive, most-crowded trade (AI infrastructure) at the same moment a geopolitical premium is showing up in headline energy costs, a combination that squeezes the Fed from both directions heading into a minutes release, a 20-year auction, and a Jackson Hole keynote that were already going to be read for daylight between Chair Warsh and his three hawkish dissenters. It also raises the stakes on Nvidia's Aug. 26 print: a name already trading as a rates proxy reports one day after the PCE/GDP/durable-goods cluster that will be the next major input into that same rate debate.]

Iran & Hormuz

Beyond the missile intercept and MoU expiry (see Lead), the Iran-Oman shipping-lane technical deal advanced on detail without resolving the underlying dispute: Tehran's foreign ministry says a route map is agreed — inbound traffic through a northern lane in Iranian waters, outbound through a southern lane in Omani waters, a proposed joint coordination center to manage traffic and collect vessel information, and a fee-free transitional period with no tolls — but Iran continues to say the strait won't actually reopen until the U.S. naval blockade lifts and other conditions are met. [DEVELOPING] Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf added a public list Monday: Iran wants U.S. sanctions on its oil lifted and military threats ended before it will reopen the strait, alongside a separate trip to Iraq on bilateral security and border issues. [NEW] [flag: single wire pickup, not cross-verified] Two more projectile strikes hit bulk carriers in the last 24-72 hours, killing one seafarer and injuring another; a separate cargo vessel off Yemen was declared a constructive total loss after multiple strikes, and maritime intelligence flagged a sharp course deviation by a Liberia-flagged product tanker southeast of Qeshm Island alongside a 64-nautical-mile oil slick traced to a dark, stationary VLCC in the southeastern waiting area.
