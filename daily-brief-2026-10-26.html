Lead

FOMC WATCH

Markets price a Wednesday hold, but the live question — and the one the Fed itself flagged in September — is a second rate hike in December

Correct the record from recent issues first: this brief has carried the Oct. 27-28 FOMC meeting as a live-but-thin cut debate, "~20%-24% odds, unchanged," as recently as Saturday. That framing was already stale. A cut has not been the real action this cycle. On Sept. 16, the Fed raised its target range a quarter point to 3.75%-4.00% — its first hike since July 2023 — citing a strong labor market, elevated inflation, and the energy shock from the Iran war, per TradingEconomics and RSM's reporting (sources disagree on the vote count: some report unanimous or 12-0, which sits oddly against a 9-3 hold-with-dissent the meeting before). [fact, correction]

That September meeting's projections matter more than this week's decision. Per RSM and TradingEconomics, 16 of 18 officials saw at least one more quarter-point hike as likely appropriate by year-end — a dot plot pointing straight at December. (Morningstar's own read of the same projections — that the median sees two hikes already done and no more — conflicts with that and is unreconciled here; treat as a single flagged discrepancy, not resolved.) [fact/flag]

Against that backdrop, Wednesday's decision is close to a formality: market-implied odds tracked across FXStreet, Invezz and CNBC's aggregation of CME FedWatch pricing (dated Oct. 22-24) put a hold at roughly 90%, a hike at 9-10%, and a cut effectively off the board. The December meeting is a different story — a hike there is priced at roughly 72%-80% depending on the tracker, up from about 70% shortly after Chair Warsh took the gavel in May. [fact, NEW/correction]

The mechanism worth watching: this is not demand overheating. The inflation the Fed is fighting is substantially cost-push — the same Iran-war oil shock that put Brent at a war-high $116.72 Friday before easing this morning — compounded by AI-capex demand and resilient growth, not a classic wage-price spiral. [read] A December hike aimed at an energy-driven price problem risks slowing growth without doing much to its actual source, a tension Warsh hasn't been forced to resolve: he has said, as recently as Oct. 16, that he won't precommit to an outcome, a deliberate break from the Fed's standard practice of broadcasting its intentions well ahead of a meeting — odd, given how plainly September's own dot plot already did exactly that. [read]

The market's cross-asset reaction this morning is consistent with "hike risk deferred to December," not resolved: equity futures are modestly higher (S&P +0.30%, Nasdaq 100 futures +0.2%) on relief that Wednesday brings no surprise, even as oil and gold both ease rather than rally together, a tape that reads as repricing, not a clean risk-on or risk-off move. [fact/read] Watch Wednesday's statement and press conference for any language on December; watch Thursday's Q3 GDP and September PCE print, which lands as the next real data point in the hike-or-hold debate before the committee meets again.

Iran & Hormuz

The most consequential item in the Hormuz file this morning is a denial, not a claim: CENTCOM said Sunday evening (Oct. 25, 18:04 ET per wire aggregation) that shipping routes remain clear of Iranian mines. No outlet has yet connected that statement by name to Saturday's IRGC claim that two supertankers were struck and disabled by naval mines — but the two sit in direct tension, and this is the first on-record US military comment bearing on that claim since it surfaced. [fact, NEW]

Fresh claims kept arriving regardless. Times of India reported Monday (02:40 ET) that Iran fired a cruise missile at vessels in the strait, and separately ran a headline (07:21 ET) asserting October is the highest-attack month on record — the latter a headline characterization, not a verified count, and both are single-sourced with no UKMTO or flag-state corroboration. [fact/flag, NEW] Gulf News reported Sunday afternoon that roughly 500 vessels are delayed amid a new tanker-attack claim; Al Jazeera reported an oil slick washing up on UAE beaches, prompting closures at Umm Al Quwain; separately, a video circulating as footage of an attack on an Emirati tanker was fact-checked Sunday as outdated, not current. [fact, NEW]

[fact/read]
