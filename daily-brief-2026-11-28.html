Lead

GROWTH UP, INFLATION UP

GDP is revised sharply higher to 4.3%, and PCE inflation accelerates to 3.8% — both land the same morning, both cut the same way, five business days before a Fed meeting where a hike is already priced as near-certain

The Bureau of Economic Analysis's second estimate, released Wednesday at 8:30 a.m. ET, put Q3 real GDP growth at 4.3% annualized — a 0.9-percentage-point upward revision from the 3.4% advance estimate, and up from Q2's 2.2%. BEA attributed the revision mainly to stronger investment, concentrated in intellectual-property products — notably software — plus stronger goods consumption, partly offset by a downward revision to services spending (mainly health care). Real final sales to private domestic purchasers, a cleaner read on underlying private demand, rose 5.8%, also revised up 0.9 point; real gross domestic income rose 4.4%; current-dollar GDP rose 6.2%. Corporate profits from current production rose $206.0 billion in the quarter, down sharply from Q2's $384.0 billion gain.

The same morning, BEA's separate Personal Income and Outlays release showed the headline PCE price index up 3.8% year-over-year in October, accelerating from September's 3.6%; core PCE, excluding food and energy, rose 3.3%, up from 3.1%. That is a distinct measure from the GDP price index reported in the growth release itself, which BEA said was unchanged at 2.1% headline and 2.5% core on a quarterly-annualized basis — the two inflation readings move differently and shouldn't be conflated; both are cited here because an investor reading only one headline number could easily take the wrong one.

Mechanism: stronger growth and firmer inflation arriving in the same data cluster is the opposite of what would give the Fed room to pause. Issue No. 42 already reported fed-funds futures pricing a December quarter-point hike at better than 90% odds as of a Nov. 17 snapshot, up from roughly two-thirds odds on Nov. 5, with Fed officials' late-October remarks and minutes read as consistent with a hike rather than a cut. Nothing in Wednesday's data works against that pricing — a GDP revision this large, paired with accelerating core inflation, is the kind of print that forecloses a dovish surprise rather than inviting one, five business days ahead of the Dec. 8–9 FOMC meeting.

Second-order consequence: the growth beat is not obviously broad-based. BEA's own attribution — software/IP investment and goods spending leading the revision, services spending revised down — is consistent with AI-capex-driven investment doing more of the lifting than organic household demand, though that distinction is a read, not something BEA's release states directly. Separately, weekly jobless claims and October durable-goods orders were both scheduled for the same Wednesday 8:30 a.m. window; this brief could not locate either actual print despite searching, so both are not confirmed as of press time rather than estimated from the last available reading (claims for the week ending Nov. 14 — an older week — came in at 203,000 against a 198,000 estimate, released Nov. 19; that is stale context, not this week's number).

Watch: whether a fresher rate-futures read, once obtained, shows hike odds pushing past the high-90s on this data; whether December's durable-goods and claims prints, once located, confirm or complicate the investment-led growth story; and Sunday's OPEC+ 2027-framework decision landing into an oil market already down double digits on the month (see Board).
