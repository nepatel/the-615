Lead

HORMUZ ESCALATION

US strikes Iran's Larak Island, Tehran hits back at Jordan, and Tuesday brings two more tanker attacks — reopening a war-risk trade the market had started to look past

The Strait of Hormuz crisis, quiet enough Friday-into-Saturday that the prior issue flagged a rare 24-hour lull, is escalating again. US forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island Saturday after observing IRGC personnel preparing to fire sea mines into the strait; CENTCOM described it as "limited, precise action against IRGC minelaying forces posing an imminent threat," and Iran says three IRGC personnel were killed. [NEW] Iran retaliated Sunday with what the Guards called "Punishment of the Aggressor" — a combined missile-and-drone strike on the King Hussein and Al Azraq bases in Jordan; Jordan said it intercepted eight missiles and reported no US casualties. It was the first direct US-Iran military exchange in over a month. Tuesday brought a second wave: two tankers, the Liberian-flagged Senegal Prosperity and the Bahri-operated VLCC Sidr, were both struck by unidentified projectiles roughly 17 nautical miles east of Khasab, Oman, per UKMTO and Marisks reporting — no casualties or pollution on either vessel, but a clear signal that whatever degraded Saturday, Iran or its proxies retain the capacity to hit shipping. [NEW]

Mechanism: the market reaction inverted the usual playbook. Oil moved as expected — WTI up as much as 2.44% to $87.85 in Tuesday premarket trade, Brent indicative in the $90-91 range on different feeds, both benchmarks still lacking a clean settle as cash markets open. [fact] Equity futures fell in sympathy (S&P -0.58%, Nasdaq 100 -1.02% premarket) and the VIX jumped 6.3% intraday to 15.86. But gold — the asset that should catch a safe-haven bid from a hot-war escalation at the world's most important oil chokepoint — instead extended Friday's selloff, sitting $150-200 below last week's highs. [read: positioning is more levered to the Sept. 16 FOMC than to the tanker war. Friday's Warsh-driven repricing (CME FedWatch September hike odds now 66%, up from 57% at Friday's close) has become the dominant cross-asset driver, strong enough that even a live escalation in Hormuz isn't pulling gold higher — a genuine divergence from how this crisis has traded since February, and worth watching for whether it holds once cash markets are fully open.]

The second-order consequence is the one that matters most for the next two weeks of Fed positioning. Axios reported Sunday that a CENTCOM-drafted plan for broader "limited strikes" against Iran — aimed at reducing the risk to tankers, Navy vessels and Air Force aircraft — had not been approved by Trump ahead of the weekend's exchange, but could now be greenlit. [fact, single-sourced to Axios] Trump himself posted an AI-generated video Monday depicting a strike on Kharg Island, Iran's main oil export terminal, and told reporters "their navy's gone… their air force is gone… their leaders are gone… all of their anti-aircraft — I mean, it's all wiped out." [fact: direct quote] Tuesday's tanker strikes complicate that framing considerably — a degraded Iranian military is still landing rockets on commercial shipping — and raise the odds that whatever CENTCOM plan Trump is weighing gets used. [read] Any sustained oil move from here lands directly on Warsh's desk nine days before the Aug. CPI print (Sept. 11) and fifteen before the Sept. 16 decision: a supply-side price shock is exactly the wrong input for a Fed chair already arguing inflation, not employment, is the binding constraint — it hands the hawks a second data point without requiring the economy to show any underlying strength. [read] Friday's jobs report remains the more proximate swing factor, but a war-driven oil spike into that print would make an already-live September hike debate harder to talk back down.

Politics

Fed Governor Lisa Cook's attorney Abbe Lowell filed a five-page response to the White House's removal threat on Aug. 26, arguing the mortgage-fraud allegations are "as baseless now as they were a year ago" and noting that Trump, Treasury Secretary Bessent and Attorney General Blanche have each faced comparable claims about their own mortgage paperwork. [continuity: reported last issue] As of this run, no White House response to Lowell's letter has surfaced — the Aug. 26 deadline has passed with the ball still in Trump's court. [DEVELOPING] A second removal attempt without new process would likely restart the emergency-litigation cycle that already reached the Supreme Court once this year on a 5-4 ruling that found Trump had failed to give Cook adequate notice; the underlying case is set for argument in January. The Cook fight continues to run on a separate track from Warsh's rate-setting fight — one is about who sits on the Board, the other about where rates go — but both are now live at the same time as a fresh Iran escalation, crowding three separate Fed-adjacent and national-security stories into the same news cycle.

Economics

CME FedWatch's jump to 66% odds of a September hike (from 57% Friday) is the single most important number in today's Board — a nine-point move in three trading sessions, on top of Friday's already-historic repricing. [NEW] The move has come entirely from Warsh's Jackson Hole framing, not from new data: the next hard data points are Friday's jobs report and the Sept. 11 CPI print, both of which could move odds sharply in either direction. Barclays is now calling for two more hikes this year — September and December, 50bp total — a house view that would have been a fringe call as recently as two weeks ago. [fact] Today's calendar (JOLTS, ISM Manufacturing) adds incremental data before Friday's more market-moving release; neither is expected to be decisive on its own. The yield curve's shape is doing some of the same work as the FedWatch number: the 2s10s spread has compressed toward roughly 35-55bp on the week's moves [exact spread depends on which same-day 2Y/10Y pairing is used, given this run's divergent 2Y reads] — a bear-flattening pattern consistent with markets pricing near-term tightening without yet revising up longer-run growth or inflation expectations, the same pattern flagged after Warsh's Friday speech. The oil move described in the Lead is the wildcard risk to this whole picture — a sustained Brent move into the $90s would show up in headline CPI before it shows up anywhere else, and would arrive right as the Fed is trying to decide how much weight to put on non-core price pressure.

Geopolitics

Russia hit Kyiv and Odesa overnight with its sixth straight day of heavy strikes on the capital — a barrage Ukraine's air force said began at 6pm Monday and included ballistic, cruise and anti-radar missiles plus 218 drones and decoys, roughly a third of them jet-powered and harder to intercept. [NEW] At least 11 people were killed and more than a dozen wounded, including three children, per Ukrainian officials. [fact] Six consecutive days of this intensity against the capital is an escalation beyond anything logged in prior issues this month, and it's landing in the same 72-hour window as the US-Iran exchange — two separate theaters both intensifying at once, with no sign either is connected to the other beyond the general test they both pose to Washington's bandwidth. Xi's Sept. 24 Washington summit remains on the calendar; export controls remain the unresolved issue between the two capitals, with no new movement reported this run.
